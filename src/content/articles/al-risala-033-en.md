*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

The session applies al-Shafi'i's basic rule for conflicting reports: authentic texts do not truly contradict each other, so they are harmonised wherever possible, and one is preferred over the other only when harmonising fails.

The first case is the hadith permitting night raids in which women and children may be harmed, set against the ban on killing them. Al-Shafi'i reads the ban as applying when they can be identified and spared, and the allowance as covering a night attack where they cannot. He rejects the claim that one hadith abrogated the other.

The main case is the Friday bath. One report calls it "obligatory on every adult", and others simply command it. 'Umar rebuked 'Uthman for coming without bathing, but he did not send him back to bathe. The commentary takes this to mean the bath is obligatory, so neglecting it is a shortcoming, but it is not a condition for the prayer being valid. It treats the report that ablution alone is "fine" as weak.

The session ends with a list of the grounds for preferring one hadith over another. These include agreement with the Qur'an, a stronger chain, more numerous or more reliable narrators, and support from other evidence or from the practice of the Companions.
