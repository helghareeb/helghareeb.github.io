*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Sometimes one hadith states a prohibition in general terms and another hadith shows the reason for it. Once the reason is known, the ruling follows it.

**Proposing over another's proposal.** Al-Shafi'i limits this ban to cases where the woman or her guardian has already accepted the first suitor. His evidence is the case of Fatimah bint Qays. Two men had proposed to her, and the Prophet ﷺ then proposed on behalf of Usamah. He did so because she had not yet accepted anyone. The ban protects an agreement that has already been made, and a mere inclination towards the first suitor is not enough to trigger it.

**Selling over another's sale.** This ban applies while the buyer and seller are still together and either may cancel the deal (*khiyar al-majlis*). In that window, a rival offer could undo the sale. Al-Shafi'i argues that the ban only makes sense if this option exists. Auction-style bidding is allowed when the seller has not accepted the first price. The ban on bargaining over a brother's bargain is read the same way.
