*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

A prohibition from the Messenger ﷺ carries the same weight as one from Allah. The default is that a prohibition makes the act unlawful unless some evidence shows otherwise.

The session sorts prohibitions into three kinds:

- **Forbidden and void.** The prohibited thing is unlawful in itself. Examples are certain marriages: marrying a woman together with her aunt, taking a fifth wife, marrying during the waiting period, *shighar* and *mut'ah* marriages, and marrying while in ihram. Such contracts are both sinful and legally invalid.
- **Forbidden but valid.** The prohibition targets a manner of doing something that is permitted in itself. Examples are certain ways of wrapping a garment or sitting. The manner is sinful, but the garment remains lawful in itself.
- **Disliked.** The prohibition is a matter of good manners, such as drinking while standing or eating with the left hand.

The key distinction is between a prohibition aimed at the thing itself, which voids the act, and one aimed at a feature or manner of it, which does not.
