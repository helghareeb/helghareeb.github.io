*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Al-Shafi'i divides religious knowledge into two kinds.

**General knowledge.** No sane adult may be ignorant of it. It covers the pillars of Islam, the basic prohibitions, and whatever a person must do personally in worship, dealings and belief. The commentary adds that anyone engaged in trade, marriage or similar activities must learn the rules that govern them.

**Specialised knowledge.** This covers the finer branches of law that lack explicit texts and are reached through interpretation and analogy. It is a communal obligation (*fard kifayah*): enough scholars must master it so that people can always find someone to ask. If they do, the rest are released, and if no one does, everyone able is at fault. Al-Shafi'i compares this with jihad, the funeral prayer and returning greetings, and cites At-Tawbah 9:122, which says that not all believers should go out at once and that some should stay behind to learn the religion.

The session ends by urging everyone to contribute to these shared duties as far as they can.
