*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

A solitary report (*khabar al-wahid*) is one narrated by individuals rather than by a mass of narrators. The session sets out when such a report becomes binding proof.

**Conditions.** The narrators must be trustworthy and known for honesty. They must understand what they narrate, or else convey it word for word, and they must have reliable memories or written records. Their reports must agree with those of other reliable narrators and must be free of *tadlis*, which means hiding a gap in the chain. The chain must also be connected. These conditions produce the classic definition of a sound hadith.

**Reports and court testimony.** Al-Shafi'i compares the two. A report can be accepted from a single man, a woman or a slave, while testimony needs a set number of witnesses. Conflicting reports are weighed against each other, while conflicting testimonies cancel out. He argues that hadith actually needs *more* care than testimony, because a change in wording can quietly change its meaning. For the same reason, a narrator may not simply cite "a trustworthy man" without naming him.
