*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Al-Shafi'i insists that the solitary report is a foundation in its own right, not something modelled on court testimony. He points to the Prophet ﷺ, who sent single envoys to teach and convey rulings, and to the Companions, who acted on what one of them reported.

He then lists the differences between the two. Testimony sometimes needs a set number of witnesses; a report does not. Someone may be acceptable as a witness but not as a narrator because he lacks precision. The reverse is also possible: his testimony may be barred because he is a party to the dispute or would benefit, while his narration is accepted. That kind of suspicion does not arise for a narrator, because he reports rulings that apply to everyone and gains nothing personally.

The session also notes how narrations are weighed against each other. A hadith is judged mostly by its narrator, and its text is examined only when it states something impossible or conflicts with stronger evidence. Narrating from the Prophet ﷺ requires reliable sources. Reports from the Israelites may be related for reflection but not used as proof.
