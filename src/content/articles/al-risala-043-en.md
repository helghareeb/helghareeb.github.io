*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Al-Shafi'i observes that a judge's verdict is itself a single person's report, yet it is enforced. A report about what is lawful and unlawful deserves to be followed in the same way.

The heart of the session is the practice of 'Umar ibn al-Khattab. He gave up his own views on the blood money for fingers, on a widow inheriting from her husband's blood money, and on the Magians when single reports reached him. He also accepted single reports about asking permission to enter, the plague and compensation for a foetus. When he occasionally asked for a second witness, al-Shafi'i explains, it was out of caution, or because the narrator was unknown to him or weak. It was not a general requirement.

The commentary adds brief notes. It holds that the letter of 'Amr ibn Hazm is most likely authentic. It holds that the practice of the people of Madinah does not override an authentic hadith. It also defines consensus as binding proof.
