*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

**The Qur'an.** Allah sent prophets to their peoples one at a time, and proof was established through each of them alone. When three messengers were sent to one town, the extra two added emphasis; they were not needed for proof.

**The Companions and later scholars.** Al-Shafi'i then lists cases in which Companions and later scholars changed their views or rulings because of one person's report:

- The Prophet ﷺ ruled on al-Furay'ah's waiting period on her word alone.
- Zayd ibn Thabit withdrew a fatwa after hearing an Ansari woman's report.
- Ibn 'Abbas relied on Ubayy ibn Ka'b's report about Musa and al-Khidr.
- Ibn 'Umar stopped a form of sharecropping once its prohibition reached him.
- 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz and Sa'd ibn Ibrahim reversed their own judgements when a hadith reached them.

Al-Shafi'i describes this as the near-unanimous practice of scholars everywhere. He warns sternly against rejecting authentic hadiths because of analogy, reasoning or vague doubts. The commentary also explains that the forbidden kind of sharecropping is the kind that gives one party a fixed plot. Sharing a proportion of the whole harvest is allowed.
