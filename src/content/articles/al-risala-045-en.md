*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Al-Shafi'i shows that leading scholars of the Successors' generation relied on reports from single Companions, among them Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, 'Urwah ibn al-Zubayr and 'Ali ibn al-Husayn. They did so in every major centre of learning.

**The *mursal* hadith.** A *mursal* is a report in which a Successor quotes the Prophet ﷺ without naming the Companion he heard it from. It is not proof by itself, but it can be accepted when it is supported. Support can come from a connected report with the same meaning, another *mursal* through different teachers, the view of some Companions, or the general fatwa of scholars. The narrator must also be reliable and must not hide weak sources. *Mursal* reports from the senior Successors carry more weight than those from the junior ones.

**Objections.** The session also answers several objections. When a jurist leaves a hadith aside, it is for a specific reason, such as a conflicting report or a weak chain. A binding proof is different from evidence cited only for support. Rejecting the Qur'an or mass-transmitted Sunnah is disbelief. Rejecting an authentic solitary report without good reason is a serious error, but not disbelief.
