*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Consensus is the agreement of the Muslim scholars on a ruling. It always rests on some underlying evidence, so it reveals a ruling rather than inventing one. Its authority is drawn from An-Nisa 4:115, which warns against following a way other than that of the believers, and from the hadith that a group of the Ummah will always remain upon the truth.

The commentary describes three levels of consensus:

- **Necessarily known.** This covers things such as the five daily prayers and the fast of Ramadan. Denying it is disbelief.
- **Certain among scholars.** One example is the ban on marrying a woman together with her aunt. Opposing it is a grave innovation.
- **Probable.** This is consensus reported by individual scholars. It binds whoever knows of it, but someone who opposes it is not condemned, since the evidence may not have reached him.

The commentary also answers several objections. Consensus cannot contradict the Sunnah. Imam Ahmad objected only to careless *claims* of consensus, not to consensus itself. It also notes that Ibn Taymiyyah limited binding consensus to the Companions, while the commentary extends it to the first three generations.
