*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Analogy is the effort to find the right ruling for a case that no text or consensus covers. It does this by linking the new case to one that a text does address, through a shared cause. Al-Shafi'i treats *qiyas* and *ijtihad* as two names for the same activity. The commentary notes that later scholars treat ijtihad as the broader term.

There are two main types of analogy. In the first, the new case plainly shares the meaning of the original, and scholars seldom disagree about it. The second rests on resemblance, so the cause is less certain and disagreement is natural.

Al-Shafi'i also distinguishes two kinds of knowledge. Some is certain both outwardly and inwardly, like an explicit text. Some is certain only outwardly, like a solitary report or an analogy. The qiblah is his example: someone who can see the Ka'bah faces it exactly, while someone far away faces the direction he judges to be right and may be mistaken. Scholars can therefore differ over analogy and still be excused.

The commentary cites the Prophet's ﷺ own analogies, such as comparing an obligation owed to Allah with a debt owed by a parent. It also points to the Qur'anic call to "take warning" (Al-Hashr 59:2) and the Companions' use of analogy in inheritance.
