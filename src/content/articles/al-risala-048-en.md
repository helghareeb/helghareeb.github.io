*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Building on the previous session, al-Shafi'i explains that people are held responsible for what is outwardly apparent. He gives several examples:

- Someone who says the testimony of faith is treated as a Muslim, even though he might be a hypocrite at heart.
- A witness who appears upright is accepted.
- In court, a confession is the strongest evidence, followed by witnesses and then oaths.

Each kind of evidence carries a different weight. In the same way, a text is stronger than an analogy, and an analogy that plainly shares the original's meaning is stronger than one based on resemblance.

Two further points follow. First, al-Shafi'i rejects deciding a case merely because the defendant refuses to swear. He prefers to return the oath to the claimant. Second, a scholar must follow where his own reasoning leads. Picking from different scholars' opinions to suit one's preferences is blameworthy.

The commentary's example is extending the zakah rule from wheat to rice because both are stored foodstuffs. It gives this as a reasonable analogy, but not a certain one.
