*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Al-Shafi'i grounds ijtihad in the command to face the Sacred Mosque "wherever you may be" (Al-Baqarah 2:150). When the Ka'bah is out of sight, a person must reason to find its direction.

The commentary explains that the saying "every mujtahid is correct" does not mean that contradictory opinions are all true. It means the mujtahid did his duty by striving for the truth. If he reaches it, he has two rewards. If he misses it, he has one, and his mistake is forgiven.

People stand at three levels. Some can derive rulings themselves. Some weigh the views of others on the basis of evidence. Laypeople follow a scholar they trust. A layperson should follow the scholar he trusts most and may ask any qualified scholar. However, he may not hold on to a view once clear evidence shows it to be wrong, and he may not pick opinions to suit his desires.

Disagreement is acceptable only when it does not contradict a text, a consensus or a clear analogy. When it does, it is rejected.
