*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

The session centres on the authentic hadith that a judge who reasons carefully earns two rewards if he is right and one if he is wrong. Al-Shafi'i defends its authenticity. He draws the lesson that ijtihad can be right or wrong, so it does not make every conclusion true. What it guarantees is that the effort is rewarded.

He returns to his favourite analogy. Someone searching for the qiblah is looking for something that really exists, the Ka'bah, even if he may miss it. In the same way, a mujtahid seeks the one truth that exists with Allah. He is not choosing among opinions to suit himself.

Further examples show that rulings follow outward evidence. A marriage that turns out to be with a relative through nursing is void. The husband is excused if he did not know, the child's lineage is established, and the dower is still owed. Al-Shafi'i also cites the Qur'an's mention of the stars as guides (Al-An'am 6:97; An-Nahl 16:16), meaning that visible signs can point to what cannot be seen.
