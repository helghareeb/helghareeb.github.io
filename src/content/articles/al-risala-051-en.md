*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Al-Shafi'i insists that ijtihad always aims at a definite target, the one truth with Allah. This is why he rejects *istihsan* in the sense of ruling by personal preference. Analogy is tied to the revealed texts and seeks to follow them, while preference of this kind can override them.

To illustrate, the commentary gives modern examples of what it considers unjustified preference:

- relaxing the payment rules of forward sales for manufacturing contracts;
- fixing prices in long-term supply contracts despite the uncertainty involved;
- treating gold as an ordinary commodity, so that unequal or deferred exchanges are allowed.

The session then lists what a mujtahid must have. He needs knowledge of the Qur'an and Sunnah, of consensus, of the views of earlier scholars and of Arabic, along with sound judgement. He must not rush to speak, must be fair to those who disagree, and must make a full effort. Al-Shafi'i compares this with valuing goods, which only someone who knows the market may do. A person who lacks these qualifications should follow a trustworthy scholar instead.
