*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Analogies are not equally clear. Some are obvious to anyone. Since even an atom's weight of good or evil will be seen (Az-Zalzalah 99:7-8), more good or evil clearly counts for more. Since saying "uff" to parents is forbidden (Al-Isra 17:23), anything harsher is forbidden too.

Other analogies need careful study. One example is family support. Parents must support young children who cannot earn, so by analogy grown children must support parents who become needy and unable to earn. This extends to grandparents and grandchildren, but not to relatives who can support themselves.

Another example is the hadith that "profit follows liability". A buyer who returns a defective item keeps whatever it produced while it was his and at his risk. Al-Shafi'i extends this to offspring and fruit as well as earnings, against those who drew the line differently. The session concludes that some analogies are agreed upon, while others leave room for scholarly disagreement.
