*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

The Prophet ﷺ forbade trading gold, silver, wheat, barley, dates and salt for the same commodity in unequal amounts or with delayed delivery. The question is what the underlying cause is, so that the rule can be extended. In the Shafi'i view:

- the four foodstuffs share the cause of being food;
- gold and silver share the cause of serving as money.

Al-Shafi'i rejects the alternative view that the common factor is being sold by weight. Consensus confirms his position. Everyone agrees that weighed foodstuffs may be bought with gold or silver on credit, which would be impossible if weight were the cause.

The commentary gives modern examples. A kilo of tomatoes cannot be exchanged for a kilo and a half of tomatoes, but it can be exchanged on the spot for any amount of cucumbers. Non-food items such as books or phones may be traded one for two. It also explains that the hadith allowing different kinds to be traded freely "hand to hand" requires immediate exchange; it does not permit deferred sales.
