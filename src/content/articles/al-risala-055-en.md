*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Scholars agree that a killer's paternal kin (the *'aqilah*) pay the blood money for an accidental killing, spread over three years. They disagree about lesser injuries. The views the summary attributes to Hanafi opponents set a minimum: one view sets it at a third of the blood money, and another at a wound that exposes the bone. Anything below the minimum is paid by the offender alone.

Al-Shafi'i weighs two lines of analogy. One treats the kin's liability as an exception that should not be extended, like the concession of wiping over leather socks. The other argues that if the kin bear the larger sum, they should all the more bear the smaller. He adopts the second and concludes that they bear every accidental injury, even a tiny one.

He also answers two further arguments. The practice of Madinah does not settle the question when it lacks wider agreement. The fact that the Prophet ﷺ set no fixed amount for minor wounds does not leave them uncompensated, because a judge can assess them. The session closes by contrasting sound analogy with preference that has no basis in the evidence.
