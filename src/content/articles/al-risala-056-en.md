*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

In this debate, apparently with a Hanafi opponent, the question is whether a free man's paternal kin pay when he accidentally injures or kills a slave. The summary reports that most scholars say they do not. Al-Shafi'i says they do, because a slave is a human being, and the case is analogous to injuring a free person.

His opponents cite Ibn 'Abbas: "the kin do not bear a slave or a deliberate act". Al-Shafi'i replies that this is a Companion's statement, not a hadith. It can also be read as referring to offences *committed by* a slave.

On how to measure the injury, he again prefers the analogy with free persons to the analogy with property. An eye is valued at half the slave's price, as it is half the blood money for a free person. The alternative of measuring the drop in his market value treats him like livestock. Al-Shafi'i supports this with the case of women. Their blood money is half a man's, yet injuries to them are still measured as fractions of their own blood money. The session shows how he chooses between competing analogies.
