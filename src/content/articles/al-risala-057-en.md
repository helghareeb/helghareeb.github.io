*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

This session presents two questions from another debate.

**Forward sales of animals.** The opponent cites Ibn Mas'ud's dislike of selling animals by description for later delivery (*salam*). Al-Shafi'i answers with the Prophet's ﷺ own practice: he borrowed a camel and later repaid it, so an animal can be owed by description. He adds an analogy with blood money, contracts by which slaves buy their freedom, and dowers, all of which may be paid in described animals.

**Wiping over leather socks.** This is a concession based on a text. Al-Shafi'i will not extend it by analogy to turbans, face-veils or gloves. Rulings that grant a specific relief from a general rule stay within their text. He also denies that the concession conflicts with the Qur'an. The verse of ablution (Al-Ma'idah 5:6) is read as applying to bare feet, so the Sunnah qualifies it rather than contradicting it.

The session briefly repeats the earlier argument that the cause of usury in foodstuffs is edibility, not weight.
