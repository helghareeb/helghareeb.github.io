*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

This session collects rulings that are exceptions to a general rule and so cannot be extended by analogy:

- the *'araya* allowance within the ban on trading fresh dates for dried ones;
- wiping over leather socks, which does not extend to other coverings;
- the paternal kin bearing the blood money for accidental killing, which departs from the rule that each person pays for his own offence;
- the fixed compensation for a miscarried foetus.

On the foetus, al-Shafi'i reviews the reasons others have suggested and rejects them. He treats the ruling as a Sunnah to be followed without knowing its reason.

He also returns to injuries against slaves. Comparing a slave with an animal is the weakest analogy, because a slave is a human being. The better model is the case of women. Injuries to them are measured as fractions of their own blood money, so injuries to a slave are measured in the same way against his value. The session underlines that sound analogy depends on knowing where analogy can and cannot be applied.
