*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

Al-Shafi'i draws a clear line between two kinds of disagreement:

- **Forbidden disagreement** contradicts a text of the Qur'an or Sunnah, a consensus or an obvious analogy.
- **Acceptable disagreement** concerns questions that are open to interpretation or that depend on analogy.

For acceptable disagreement, he gives two examples. Two people who reach different conclusions about the qiblah should not condemn each other. A person is not declared untrustworthy while opinion about him is divided. As an example of unacceptable disagreement, the commentary cites denying zakah on modern paper money, which goes against the obvious analogy with gold and silver.

The session also returns to rulings that cannot be extended. The *musarrah* is a sheep whose udder was tied so that it seems to give more milk. It may be returned with a measure of dates, but this ruling is not applied to other defects. The rule that profit follows liability is a general principle and can be extended. Compensation for a foetus is again treated as a special case.

Finally, al-Shafi'i shows how one case can carry several rulings. A wife remarries after a false report of her husband's death. The second marriage is dissolved without divorce or punishment, but the dower, waiting period and child's lineage are all established.
