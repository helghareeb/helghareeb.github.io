*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

The verse requires divorced women to wait "three periods" (Al-Baqarah 2:228). The Arabic word, *quru'*, can mean either menstrual cycles or the intervals of purity between them. Leading Companions held each view, and all agreed that the word refers to spans of time.

The summary sets out the case for menstruation. The Prophet ﷺ used the word to mean menstruation when he told a woman to leave prayer "during the days of your *aqra'*". Another hadith counts a slave woman's waiting period in menstrual cycles. Counting by periods of purity could leave less than three complete periods. It also reads "divorce them for their waiting period" (At-Talaq 65:1) as referring to the start of that period.

Those who favour purity argue that a "period" marks a boundary, as the new moon marks the boundary between months. The summary replies that the Prophet's ﷺ usage outweighs this linguistic argument, and it concludes in favour of menstruation.

It also notes two further points. A woman who has stopped menstruating waits by months. The waiting period ends when the third menstruation ends, and no bath is required for it to end.
