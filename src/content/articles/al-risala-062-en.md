*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

This session focuses on *ila'*, a husband's oath not to have relations with his wife, and on the verse that gives him four months (Al-Baqarah 2:226-227).

If he resumes relations within the four months, the oath's effect ends and he owes only the expiation for breaking an oath. What counts is the act, not the intention. Intending to return without actually returning does not end the oath, and returning ends it even if he had intended not to.

Once four months pass, divorce does not happen automatically. The husband must either resume relations or pronounce a divorce. If he refuses both, the judge requires him to choose, and if he still refuses, the judge pronounces the divorce himself.

The session also separates *ila'* from simply avoiding one's wife (*hajr*), which needs no oath. It rejects comparing *ila'* with saying "you are divorced in four months". That statement is a divorce that takes effect when the time arrives, whereas *ila'* is an oath with its own ruling.
