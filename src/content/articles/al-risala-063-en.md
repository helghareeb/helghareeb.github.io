*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

**Returning the remainder (*radd*).** When the heirs with fixed shares have taken them and something is left over, does it go back to relatives or to the public treasury? Zayd ibn Thabit said the treasury, and Abu Hanifah said the relatives. Al-Shafi'i sides with Zayd. The verses on a person with no parent or child (An-Nisa 4:12, 4:176) fix a sister's share at half, so she cannot be given more through the remainder.

He reads the verse saying blood relatives are "more entitled" (Al-Anfal 8:75) as ending inheritance by alliance and emigration. It does not override the fixed shares. Distant relatives inherit only when there is no other heir.

**The grandfather and the brothers.** Some Companions, including Abu Bakr and Ibn 'Abbas, held that the grandfather excludes the deceased's brothers. Others, including Zayd, 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali and Ibn Mas'ud, held that he shares with them. Al-Shafi'i takes the second view. Calling the grandfather a "father" does not give him every rule that applies to a father, and an analogy that would exclude the brothers entirely runs against consensus.
