*This is a short English overview of the Arabic summary of this session in Dr. Yasir Burhami's explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's al-Risalah. For the full text, see the Arabic version on this site or the original post.*

When the Companions disagree, no one Companion's view binds the others. Some scholars give special weight to agreement among the four Rightly Guided Caliphs. Al-Shafi'i notes, however, that authentic reports of all four agreeing are rare.

The commentary reads the hadith "hold to my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the Rightly Guided Caliphs" as referring to their way of following the Sunnah, not as making each of their opinions binding. A view held by a single Companion, with no known agreement or opposition, is not treated as proof unless analogy supports it.

The session then ranks the sources of law:

1. the Qur'an and the mass-transmitted Sunnah;
2. hadiths from single narrators;
3. probable consensus;
4. analogy.

Analogy is used only out of necessity, much as dry ablution is used only when there is no water. The commentary illustrates the ranking with evidence in court, which runs from the judge's own certain knowledge, through confession and witnesses, down to a returned oath.

Its conclusion is that the Companions' views are not an independent source. They are weighed against the Qur'an, Sunnah, consensus and analogy.
