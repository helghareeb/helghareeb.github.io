In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Blessings and peace be upon the noblest of the Prophets and Messengers, our Prophet Muhammad, and upon all his family and Companions.

This is a detailed summary of what was covered in this part of the explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's *al-Risalah*, focusing on the important points and clarifications, and supplemented with some additions and explanations that help in understanding the issue more deeply.

**Introduction to *al-Risalah* and its importance:**

- *Al-Risalah* is the first work composed in the science of the principles of jurisprudence (*usul al-fiqh*), the science that lays down the rules the jurist follows in deriving Shari'ah rulings from their detailed evidence.
- Before Imam al-Shafi'i, the rules of usul al-fiqh were scattered in the statements of the people of knowledge, but he was the first to gather them and explain their foundations.
- Al-Shafi'i did not name this book "*al-Risalah*"; it was called that because it was originally a letter (*risalah*) he wrote to his friend 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi.
- *Al-Risalah* is distinguished by elevated and dense language (that is, rich in meanings), and for this reason many people were in awe of explaining it.

**Paragraph 1275 onward: a debate on the authority of the *mursal* hadith and al-Shafi'i's conditions for accepting it:**

Imam al-Shafi'i presents a debate (which may be real or imagined) on the authority of the *mursal* hadith and the conditions for accepting it, explaining his position on this issue.

- **The *mursal* hadith:** It is a hadith from whose chain a Companion has been omitted.
- **The scholars' disagreement over using the *mursal* as proof:** The scholars differed over using the *mursal* as proof, holding three views:
  - Those who accepted it without qualification (such as Imam Malik).
  - Those who rejected it without qualification (the position of the majority of the people of knowledge).
  - Those who made distinctions and accepted it under conditions and criteria (the position of Imam al-Shafi'i).
- **Al-Shafi'i's criteria for accepting the *mursal* (ways of strengthening the *mursal*):** Al-Shafi'i permits the *mursal* hadith under conditions and criteria, namely:
  - **That the meaning of the *mursal* hadith be sound, and that trustworthy memorizers share it and connect it to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ:** That is, a hadith with a connected chain comes with the same meaning as the *mursal* hadith. This shows the soundness and accurate memory of the one from whom it was accepted (that is, the narrator who narrated it as *mursal*).
  - **That this *mursal* be strengthened by another *mursal*, whether that other one is *marfu'* (traced to the Prophet), *mawquf* (stopping at a Companion), or *maqtu'* (stopping at a Successor):** That is, another *mursal* comes through another route, supporting the first *mursal*.
  - **That the omission be from a trustworthy narrator, not from someone unknown or weak:** That is, the habit of the narrator who narrated the hadith as *mursal* is to narrate *mursal* only from trustworthy narrators.
  - **That the *mursal* be free of contradiction:** That is, the *mursal* hadith does not contradict an authentic connected hadith.
- **A note on the debate:** The commentator points out that debate takes place only between people capable of reasoning from evidence (the people of knowledge), and that Imam al-Shafi'i may have been imagining someone debating him, or there may really have been someone debating him.
- **The difference between disconnected and connected:** Al-Shafi'i explains that the disconnected hadith ranks lower than the connected hadith, because the cause of the break is unknown: the narrator who was not mentioned may be weak or unknown.
- **The possible reasons for the omission:** Al-Shafi'i explains the possible reasons for a hadith being narrated as *mursal*, among them that the narrator took it from someone whose narration is shunned (that is, from a weak or unknown narrator).
- **Clarifying the strengthening of a *mursal* by another *mursal*:** He explains that it is not enough for another *mursal* to come with the same chain; it must come through another route for it to be genuine strengthening.
- **Is a Companion's statement a proof according to al-Shafi'i?:** The commentator discusses an important issue: is a Companion's statement a proof according to al-Shafi'i? He shows that the reports of al-Shafi'i's view on this issue are inconsistent, and that Imam al-Ghazali held that al-Shafi'i's position is not to use a Companion's statement as proof. He explains, however, that al-Shafi'i's explicit statement in his books, including this one, is to use a Companion's statement as proof when there is no text from the Book or the Sunnah, no consensus, no evident analogy, and no necessary demonstration that cannot be rejected. He cites the words of Imam Ibn al-Qayyim in his book *I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in*, which confirm this meaning. He shows that whoever said al-Shafi'i does not use a Companion's statement as proof attributed to him an implication of his statement, not its explicit wording. He discusses the issue "Is the implication of a statement binding (as the speaker's view)?", showing that the implication of a statement is not binding unless its author commits to it, except where the matter is plain in its indication.
- **Explaining the phrase "it indicates the soundness of the hadith's source strongly, if it is examined":** The commentator explains that this phrase shows that the matter is subtle and only the people of knowledge perceive it.
- **Al-Shafi'i's position on the *mursal* of the senior Successors and those after them:** Al-Shafi'i explains that he accepts the *mursal* of the senior Successors without qualification, whereas he accepts the *mursal* of those after them only under the stated criteria, because they are more lax about whom they narrate from, there are indications of weakness in the sources of what they narrate as *mursal*, and there is much *ihalah* in their narrations (that is, weakness due to contradiction or inattention).

**Summary of the issues covered:**

- The meaning of the *mursal* hadith.
- The correct view of al-Shafi'i's position on using a Companion's statement as proof is that he uses it as proof when other texts are absent.
- Al-Shafi'i used the *mursal* of a Companion and of the senior Successors as proof, and did not use the *mursal* of those after them as proof except under the stated criteria.
