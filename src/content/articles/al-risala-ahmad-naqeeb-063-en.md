In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Blessings and peace be upon the noblest of the Prophets and Messengers, our Prophet Muhammad, and upon all his family and Companions.

This is a detailed summary of what was covered in this part of the explanation of Imam al-Shafi'i's *al-Risalah*, focusing on the important points and clarifications, and supplemented with some additions and explanations that help in understanding the issue more deeply:

**Paragraph 1278: Censure of falling short in seeking knowledge, of accepting weak reports, and of heedlessness in distinguishing between reports:**

In this part, Imam al-Shafi'i censures falling short in seeking knowledge and being content with little, accepting weak reports, and being heedless of the distinction between sound and weak reports.

- **Censure of being content with a small amount of knowledge:** Al-Shafi'i censures the one who is content with a little knowledge and does not strive for more, and who accepts what is inferior while something weightier exists, thereby falling short in seeking knowledge. He stresses the necessity of scrutinizing reports and not giving precedence to the weak over the sound.
- **Censure of one who faults the method of distinguishing sound from weak and desires unrestricted breadth:** Al-Shafi'i censures the one who faults the method of distinguishing between sound and weak, on the pretext that one might fall into the weak and abandon the sound, and who desires unrestricted breadth in knowledge, which leads him to accept from people from whom it would have been better for him to refrain from accepting.
- **Censure of heedlessness in distinguishing between reports:** Al-Shafi'i censures the heedlessness that may afflict some people, so that they accept from someone whose like, and even better than whom, they reject; they accept from someone whose weakness they know when his report agrees with a view they hold, and they reject the hadith of a trustworthy narrator when it contradicts a view they hold. He explains that this is part of the corruption that enters upon some of them from various directions.
- **The wariness of experts toward the mursal reports of those below the senior Successors:** Al-Shafi'i points out that whoever examines knowledge with expertise and little heedlessness is wary of the *mursal* (reports with a missing link to the Prophet) of everyone below the senior Successors (*Tabi'un*), because of clear indications of weakness in the sources of their hadith and the frequency of *ihalah* (that is, weakness due to contradiction or heedlessness).
- **Explanation of some vocabulary:**
  - **Khabartu:** I came to know something precisely through testing and experience.
  - **Khaslah:** a trait, a feature, or a thing.
  - **Sabil:** a way or path.
  - **Al-madkhul:** the corrupt or defective.
  - **Tajawwuzan:** out of laxity.
  - **Al-ihalah:** weakness due to contradiction or heedlessness.
  - **Istawhasha:** to be averse to and dislike.

**Some additional benefits:**

- Clarifying the importance of distinguishing between sound and weak reports.
- Censure of blind imitation and accepting everything that is said without scrutiny.
- Clarifying the standing of the senior Successors with respect to accepting their *mursal* reports.

**Summary of the session:**

- Censure of falling short in seeking knowledge and being content with little.
- Censure of accepting weak reports without discrimination.
- Censure of heedlessness in distinguishing between reports.
- Clarifying that experts are wary of the *mursal* reports of those below the senior Successors.
- Emphasizing the importance of distinguishing between sound and weak reports.
