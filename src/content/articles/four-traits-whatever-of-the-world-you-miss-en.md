## Four Traits That, If You Have Them, Leave No Loss in What You Miss of This World

## The First Sermon

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him, seek His help and forgiveness, and seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and our bad deeds. Whoever Allah guides, none can lead astray; whoever He lets go astray, none can guide. I bear witness that none is worthy of worship except Allah alone, without partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger.

Allah says, in meaning: “O you who believe, be mindful of Allah as He deserves, and do not die except as Muslims” [Al ‘Imran 3:102].

“O mankind, be mindful of your Lord, who created you from a single soul, created its mate from it, and spread from them many men and women. Be mindful of Allah, in whose name you ask one another, and of ties of kinship. Allah is ever watching over you” [Al-Nisa’ 4:1].

“O you who believe, be mindful of Allah and speak upright words. He will put your deeds right for you and forgive your sins. Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger has attained a great triumph” [Al-Ahzab 33:70–71]. To proceed:

The truest speech is the Book of Allah, the best guidance is the guidance of Muhammad, blessings and peace be upon him, the worst matters are those newly introduced, every newly introduced matter is an innovation, every innovation is misguidance, and every misguidance is in the Fire.[^1]

## Sincere Counsel Is the Legacy of Prophethood

Noble Muslims, guidance is sincere counsel, and sincere counsel is one of the greatest gateways of religion. The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Religion is sincere counsel.” We asked: “To whom?” He said: “To Allah, to His Book, to His Messenger, to the leaders of the Muslims, and to their common folk.”[^2]

There was no prophet or messenger who did not sincerely counsel and guide his community. The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “There was no prophet before me except that it was his duty to direct his community to the best of what he knew for them and to warn them of the worst of what he knew for them.”[^3]

Most people are heedless of what benefits them: “Their reckoning has drawn near to mankind, while they turn away in heedlessness” [Al-Anbiya’ 21:1]. The community needs those who remind it and guide it rightly: “And remind, for reminding benefits the believers” [Al-Dhariyat 51:55].

That is why the Companions of the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, benefited from his counsel and guidance, and were as their Lord described them: “Among the believers are men who have been true to what they pledged to Allah. Some of them have fulfilled their vow, and some are still waiting, and they have not altered in the least” [Al-Ahzab 33:23].

The forms of goodness among the Companions of the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, were varied: knowledge, generous giving, sincere counsel, and trustworthiness. What united them was reverence for revelation and the pursuit of Allah’s pleasure.

The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, did not betray the community or conceal any knowledge; rather, he conveyed and explained. ‘A’ishah, may Allah be pleased with her, said: “Whoever tells you that Muhammad, blessings and peace be upon him, concealed anything of what was revealed to him has lied.”[^4] The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “I have left you upon clear white ground, its night like its day. No one deviates from it after me except one who is ruined.”[^5]

His Companions, may Allah be pleased with them, therefore gathered upon this guidance. They governed worldly affairs by religion and showed, in every aspect of their lives, how matters are set right, as the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, had taught them.

## Being Guided by Revelation

Servants of Allah, we need to bring our deeds back to the guidance of revelation, setting right our aims and our dealings by it, and not placing the pursuit of wealth or status ahead of Allah’s limits.

Among the greatest causes of disorder is turning away from the guidance of the Quran and the Sunnah. Through them a Muslim discerns where good and evil lie and weighs his earnings and deeds. Allah says: “Whoever Allah does not grant light has no light” [Al-Nur 24:40].

## Life Is Short, and Death Comes Suddenly

Know, brothers, that life is short, and despite its shortness, death comes suddenly. Whoever does not know when death will overtake him should prepare. The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Take advantage of five before five: your youth before your old age, your health before your sickness, your wealth before your poverty, your free time before your busyness, and your life before your death.”[^6]

What accompanies a person after his death is his deeds, so let him strive in what benefits him. The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Three follow the deceased; two return and one remains with him. His family, his wealth, and his deeds follow him; his family and wealth return, and his deeds remain.”[^7]

Do not put Allah’s light behind your back and so live in darkness. That is why the Companions of the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, learned what does not change and benefited from his counsels, upon him be blessings and peace. The counsels of the Messenger of Allah, blessings and peace be upon him, deserve to be written in the water of the eyes, if only a person would take them to heart.

## The Comprehensive Hadith

Among these counsels is what Imam Ahmad and others report from ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Amr, may Allah be pleased with them both, that the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Four things—if they are in you, you need not worry about whatever of this world passes you by: safeguarding a trust, truthfulness in speech, goodness of character, and integrity in what one eats.”[^8]

So be keen to understand his words, blessings and peace be upon him, and to grasp them fully.

“Four” means four traits by which a person preserves his religion and his decency. The hadith does not mean abandoning the effort to earn, neglecting obligatory spending, or dispensing with the other obligations because of these morals. Rather, it magnifies the importance of these traits and makes light of whatever worldly gains one misses while preserving them. The soundness of one’s religion is greater than any profit that comes through betrayal, lying, or what is unlawful.

## The First Trait: Safeguarding Trusts

The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Safeguarding a trust.”

Trust covers words, deeds, and wealth, and whatever rights, secrets, and responsibilities a person is entrusted with. Safeguarding it includes giving each right to its owner and protecting what one learns by virtue of one’s work or one’s connection with people.

The greatest trust is what lies between you and Allah, glorified is He: fulfilling His obligations and stopping at His limits. Allah says: “We offered the trust to the heavens, the earth, and the mountains, but they declined to bear it and feared it; yet man bore it. He has been unjust and ignorant” [Al-Ahzab 33:72]. He also says: “O you who believe, do not betray Allah and the Messenger, nor knowingly betray your trusts” [Al-Anfal 8:27].

And He, glorified is He, says: “Allah commands you to render trusts to those entitled to them, and when you judge between people, to judge with justice. Excellent is what Allah admonishes you with. Allah is All-Hearing, All-Seeing” [Al-Nisa’ 4:58].

The ruler is entrusted, the judge is entrusted, the governor and the guardian are entrusted, as are the teacher in his school, the professor in his university, the doctor in his hospital, and the employee in his office. Everyone who holds any charge is responsible and entrusted. The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for his flock.”[^9] He also said: “There is no servant whom Allah places in charge of a flock, and who dies on the day he dies while deceiving his flock, except that Allah forbids Paradise to him.”[^10]

So great is the matter of trust that the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, negated the completeness of faith for anyone who neglects it, saying: “There is no faith for one who has no trustworthiness, and no religion for one who does not keep his covenant.”[^11] He made betraying a trust one of the signs of hypocrisy: “The signs of the hypocrite are three: when he speaks, he lies; when he promises, he breaks it; and when he is entrusted, he betrays.”[^12]

## Trustworthiness Appears When You Are Able to Betray

Trustworthiness truly appears only when one has the power: when you are able to betray, with no one watching over you and no one seeing you except the Lord of the worlds, glorified is He. In that situation trustworthiness appears.

Among the effects of lost trustworthiness are corrupted work and the disruption of people’s interests: when an employee falls short in his duty, when a partner conceals his partner’s right, or when an official uses his position for private benefit at the expense of those entrusted to him. Trust also includes caring for one’s children and treating them justly.

Let no father’s love for one of his children lead him to wrong the others. The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Be mindful of Allah, and be just among your children.”[^13]

The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said to the man who asked him about the Hour: “When trust is lost, then await the Hour.” He asked: “How is it lost?” He said: “When authority is entrusted to those unfit for it, then await the Hour.”[^14]

## The Second Trait: Truthfulness in Speech

Then the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “And truthfulness in speech.”

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, says: “O you who believe, be mindful of Allah and be with the truthful” [Al-Tawbah 9:119]. The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Hold to truthfulness, for truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise. A man continues to speak the truth and strive for truthfulness until he is recorded with Allah as a man of truth. Beware of lying, for lying leads to wickedness, and wickedness leads to the Fire. A man continues to lie and strive for lying until he is recorded with Allah as a liar.”[^15]

The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said, as reported in the introduction to *Sahih Muslim*: “It is enough of a lie for a man to relate everything he hears.” In a version reported by Abu Dawud: “It is enough of a sin for a man to relate everything he hears.”[^16]

How greatly we need this guidance when using social media! The ease of posting does not lift the responsibility of a word, and the wide circulation of a report does not prove that it is true. Do not pass on a hadith before verifying that it is authentic, nor an accusation that wounds a Muslim without evidence, nor a report that stirs people up when you do not know its source.

In the Prophet’s dream, blessings and peace be upon him, there is a description of the punishment of a man who tells a lie that reaches the far horizons.[^17] This is a warning that should make a believer fear lying and spreading lies, however easy the means or however many the recipients.

So verify, servant of Allah, before you pass something on, before you write, and before you share: “O you who believe, if a defiantly disobedient person brings you news, verify it, lest you harm a people out of ignorance and then become regretful over what you have done” [Al-Hujurat 49:6]. “He utters no word except that there is a watcher with him, ready” [Qaf 50:18].

The firmness of truthfulness shows when its owner fears losing an advantage or having a shortcoming exposed. Let him admit his mistake, repair what he has spoiled, and not defend himself by fabricating lies against others. The escape sought through lying may be a door to a greater loss.

## The Third Trait: Goodness of Character

Then the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “And goodness of character.”

‘Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak, may Allah have mercy on him, described good character, saying: “It is a cheerful face, generously doing good, and refraining from harm.”[^18] So let a Muslim combine a pleasant manner of meeting people, kindness toward them, and guarding his tongue and limbs from harming them.

Good character is as Allah commanded His Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him: “Take what is given freely, enjoin what is right, and turn away from the ignorant” [Al-A‘raf 7:199]. His Lord praised him, saying: “And you are indeed of a magnificent character” [Al-Qalam 68:4].

The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Nothing is heavier in the balance of the believer on the Day of Resurrection than good character.”[^19] He also said: “Among the dearest of you to me, and those who will sit closest to me on the Day of Resurrection, are those of you with the best character.”[^20]

## Good Character Is Tested in Hardship

Good character is required in ease and in hardship, and its steadiness shows in anger and difficulty. Many people can be gentle when matters accord with their desires; the real struggle comes when matters go against them.

If your provision becomes tight or your work exhausts you, do not make your family and colleagues the target of your anger. Remember Allah’s words: “Those who restrain their anger and pardon people. Allah loves those who do good” [Al ‘Imran 3:134], and the words of the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him: “The strong man is not the one who overcomes others in wrestling; the strong man is the one who controls himself when angry.”[^21]

## ‘Umar’s Insight in Knowing Men

Among the reports showing that people’s character is known through close dealings is what is narrated from ‘Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, concerning vouching for a witness.[^22] Vouching is a responsibility; praise not grounded in real knowledge does not suffice for it.

‘Umar said to him: “I do not know you, and it does not harm you that I do not know you. Bring me someone who knows you.” A man among those present said: “I know him.” ‘Umar asked: “By what do you know him?” He said: “By his uprightness and virtue.” ‘Umar said:

“Is he your nearest neighbor, whose night and day, comings and goings you know?” He said: “No.”

“Then has he dealt with you in dinars and dirhams, by which scrupulousness is shown?” He said: “No.”

“Then has he been your companion on a journey, by which noble character is shown?” He said: “No.”

He said: “You do not know him.” Then he said to the man: “Bring someone who knows you.”

This draws attention to the role of neighborliness, financial dealings, and travel in knowing a person’s conduct. It does not mean that wealth corrupts all people; rather, a claim to good character needs something to confirm it in dealings and in fulfilling rights.

## The Fourth Trait: Integrity in What One Eats

Then the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “And integrity in what one eats,” that is, in food and in earnings, so that nothing enters one’s stomach except what is lawful.

Allah, glorified is He, says: “O you who believe, eat of the good things We have provided you, and give thanks to Allah, if it is He whom you worship” [Al-Baqarah 2:172]. The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “O people, Allah is good and accepts only what is good.” Then he mentioned a man who travels long, disheveled and dusty, stretching his hands toward the sky: “O Lord, O Lord,” while his food is unlawful, his drink is unlawful, his clothing is unlawful, and he has been nourished on the unlawful: “So how can he be answered?!”[^23]

A Muslim should therefore examine his food, his drink, and his clothing.

Beware, servant of Allah, of giving short measure or weight. Beware of exploiting a person’s lack of understanding or his inattention to cheat him on the scale. Keep in mind the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic: “Woe to those who give short measure: those who, when they take a measure from people, take it in full, but when they measure or weigh for them, give less. Do they not think that they will be resurrected for a tremendous Day, the Day when mankind will stand before the Lord of the worlds?” [Al-Mutaffifin 83:1–6].

Beware of cheating in selling or buying, keeping in mind the words of your Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him: “Whoever cheats is not of me.”[^24]

Beware, and again beware, of swearing false oaths to consume what is unlawful or to take what is not rightfully yours. The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “Whoever seizes the right of a Muslim by his oath, Allah has made the Fire binding upon him and forbidden Paradise to him.” A man asked him: “Even if it is something small, Messenger of Allah?” He said: “Even if it is a twig of an arak tree.”[^25] He also said: “Swearing oaths promotes the sale of goods but wipes out the blessing.”[^26] Allah says: “Those who sell Allah’s covenant and their oaths for a small price will have no share in the Hereafter. Allah will not speak to them or look at them on the Day of Resurrection, nor will He purify them, and they will have a painful punishment” [Al ‘Imran 3:77].

Beware of consuming the wealth of orphans or the wealth of the weak: “Those who consume the wealth of orphans unjustly only consume fire into their bellies, and they will burn in a blaze” [Al-Nisa’ 4:10]. “O you who believe, do not consume one another’s wealth wrongfully, except through trade by mutual consent among you, and do not kill yourselves. Allah is ever Merciful to you” [Al-Nisa’ 4:29].

The Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, said: “No flesh grows from unlawful gain except that the Fire is more fitting for it.”[^27]

And beware, and again beware, of hearing the words of Allah and the words of His Messenger, blessings and peace be upon him, then shaking your hands free of them and leaving the mosque as you entered it.

This is how the Companions of the Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him, were. They set examples of truthfulness, trustworthiness, and generous giving, and learned from him that the soundness of religion shows in the reality of life. Let us follow their guidance in worship and in dealings, and let our character bear witness to what we learn from the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger.

## Preserving Religion When Worldly Gain Is Lost

The summary lies in the words of our Prophet, blessings and peace be upon him: “Four things—if they are in you, you need not worry about whatever of this world passes you by: safeguarding a trust, truthfulness in speech, goodness of character, and integrity in what one eats.”

You may be offered something through which you would earn hundreds of thousands, but at the cost of betrayal. Leave it, and do not worry about what you miss of this world.

Something may come your way and be offered to you, but at the cost of lying and abandoning truthfulness. Leave it, and do not worry about what you miss of this world.

Something in this world may be offered to you, but at the cost of abandoning good character. Leave it, and do not worry about what you miss of this world.

Delicious food may be offered to you, but through a door other than the one Allah, glorified is He, has prescribed. Leave it, and do not worry about what you miss of this world.

Trust the promise of the Messenger of Allah, blessings and peace be upon him: “You will never leave anything for the sake of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, except that Allah will replace it for you with something better for you than it,”[^28] and the promise of your Lord: “Whoever is mindful of Allah, He will make a way out for him and provide for him from where he does not expect” [Al-Talaq 65:2–3].

The compensation a believer hopes for is not limited to more wealth or a higher position. It may be tranquility, protection, and blessing, and Allah may store up for him a reward better than this world. So let him abandon the unlawful as an act of worship to Allah, not on the condition of receiving a particular worldly compensation.

I say these words of mine and seek Allah’s forgiveness for myself and for you. Seek His forgiveness: He is the All-Forgiving, the Most Merciful.

## The Second Sermon

All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds. I bear witness that none is worthy of worship except Allah alone, without partner; He speaks the truth and guides to the right way. I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger. O Allah, send blessings, peace, and grace upon him, his family, his Companions, and those who follow them in excellence until the Day of Judgment. To proceed:

Servants of Allah, these are four traits, light in wording and immense in effect. Let each of us hold himself to account over them before he is held to account:

1. Your trust: have you fulfilled what you were entrusted with—in your work, your home, your children, and your post—as you would like others to fulfill it toward you?

2. Your tongue: have you been truthful in everything you said, wrote, and passed on, or have you related everything you heard?

3. Your character: how are you in hardship, anger, and need? There lies your true measure.

4. Your morsel: where did you earn it? Has a single dirham of the unlawful entered your stomach or the stomachs of your dependants?

Know that Allah has commanded you to do something He began with Himself, saying: “Allah and His angels send blessings upon the Prophet. O you who believe, send blessings upon him and greet him with peace” [Al-Ahzab 33:56].

O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You sent blessings upon Abraham and the family of Abraham; You are Praiseworthy, Glorious. O Allah, bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You blessed Abraham and the family of Abraham; You are Praiseworthy, Glorious.

## Supplication

O Allah, forgive us, our parents, and believing men and women. Set our hearts and deeds right, and grant us sincerity, truthfulness, and steadfastness. O Allah, help us remember You, thank You, and worship You well, and purify our hearts of association, showing off, and arrogance.

O Allah, provide us with lawful, wholesome sustenance, help us fulfill our trusts, improve our character, and make us steadfast with the firm word in this life and the Hereafter. O Allah, have mercy on our dead, heal our sick, relieve the distressed, discharge the debts of those in debt, improve the circumstances of the Muslims, and lift oppression from the oppressed.

“Our Lord, grant us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire” [Al-Baqarah 2:201].

Servants of Allah: “Allah commands justice, excellence, and giving to relatives, and forbids indecency, wrongdoing, and aggression. He admonishes you so that you may take heed” [Al-Nahl 16:90]. Remember Allah, and He will remember you; thank Him for His blessings, and He will increase you; seek His forgiveness, and He will forgive you. “The remembrance of Allah is greater, and Allah knows what you do” [Al-‘Ankabut 29:45].
