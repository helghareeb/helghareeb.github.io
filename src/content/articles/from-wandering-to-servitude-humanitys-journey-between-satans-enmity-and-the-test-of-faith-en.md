## From Wandering to Servitude

## Humanity's Journey Between Satan's Enmity and the Test of Faith

The article opens by describing the present state of the Ummah as an age of wandering, recalling how the Qur'an described the wandering of the Children of Israel when they refused Allah's command to enter the Holy Land. They told Moses, "Indeed, we will not enter it, ever, as long as they are within it; so go, you and your Lord, and fight. Indeed, we are remaining right here," and Allah decreed: "Then indeed, it is forbidden to them for forty years [in which] they will wander throughout the land" (al-Ma'idah 5:20–26). They lost a physical road on earth; our loss today is of the inner road to Allah. Most of us know the way to work, school and home, yet have forgotten the essential question: why were we created, and what is the purpose of our existence? Just as a lost traveller returns to his starting point to see where he went wrong, the Ummah can only escape its wandering by returning to the root of the matter: the first story of man with his Lord and with his first enemy.

## The First Enemy

That story begins in the highest assembly: "And [mention] when We said to the angels, 'Prostrate before Adam'; so they prostrated, except for Iblees. He refused and was arrogant and became of the disbelievers" (al-Baqarah 2:34). Iblis had worshipped Allah alongside the angels, and it is reported that he was even called "the peacock of the angels" for the abundance of his worship, yet he fell at the first test of servitude out of self-importance and arrogance toward his Lord's command. The article invites the reader to consider how many people worship Allah for a time and then disobey Him in a single matter out of arrogance or desire, and so fall as Iblis fell. Adam and his wife were then given wide bliss with a single prohibition (al-Baqarah 2:35), "But Satan caused them to slip out of it and removed them from that [condition] in which they had been" (al-Baqarah 2:36). If Satan did not leave our father alone in Paradise, he will certainly not leave us safe in this world; he still seeks to drive us out of the garden of obedience into the fire of disobedience.

## The Enemy Who Never Sleeps

Allah warns: "Indeed, Satan is an enemy to you; so take him as an enemy. He only invites his party to be among the companions of the Blaze" (Fatir 35:6). A person who knew an enemy was lying in wait would not sleep until he had taken every precaution, yet people are careless about an enmity more dangerous than any earthly foe, because this enemy seeks their ruin in religion, worldly life and the Hereafter. His means are many, as Allah describes in al-Isra 17:64: his "voice" is every means that tempts and misleads; his horses and foot soldiers are every caller to sin; his partnership in wealth is usury and consuming the unlawful; and his partnership in children comes when parents neglect the remembrance of Allah at intimacy or neglect raising their children on tawhid and chastity. He is skilled at adornment and his promises are delusion. He tells people, "You will repent tomorrow," just as Joseph's brothers said, "Kill Joseph or cast him out to [another] land; the countenance of your father will [then] be only for you, and you will be after that a righteous people" (Yusuf 12:9). Did they become righteous afterwards? The one who says "I will repent tomorrow" has no guarantee he will live until tomorrow, nor that his heart will be as soft then as it is today.

## The Wisdom of Trial

It is part of Allah's great wisdom that He made the existence of this enemy a means of testing His servants and a measure of their sincerity: "[He] who created death and life to test you [as to] which of you is best in deed" (al-Mulk 67:1–2), and "We will surely test you until We make evident those who strive among you [for the cause of Allah] and the patient, and We will test your affairs" (Muhammad 47:31). Allah did not create evil in vain nor permit trials except for a far-reaching wisdom: purification and distinction. Affliction is therefore not a sign of divine anger but a door to refinement and selection, and every trial carries a new lesson in servitude to Allah.

## Signs of Wandering in the Ummah

The article lists several manifestations of this wandering. There is a wandering of thought and creed, when Allah's Shari'ah is replaced by man-made constitutions and the slogan of democracy, "rule of the people," is raised in place of servitude to Allah and "rule of Allah." There is a wandering of identity, when the Muslim is expected to dissolve into Western culture and be ashamed of belonging to his religion. There is a wandering of hearts, when this world rises in people's souls, the Hereafter is forgotten, and prayer becomes habit rather than worship. And there is a wandering of the family, when fathers neglect raising their children on tawhid and chastity and Muslim homes are destroyed by disputes and desires. All of these are bitter fruits of Satan's whispering, who swore before Allah: "Because You have put me in error, I will surely sit in wait for them on Your straight path" (al-A'raf 7:16). He sits on the straight path itself, not only on the road of corruption: he does not fight only the wicked, but creeps into the hearts of the righteous to come between them and sincerity, or to bring in showing off and self-delusion.

## Allah Did Not Leave Us Neglected

Despite this long struggle, Allah did not abandon humanity. He sent down scriptures, sent messengers and made the way clear: "Indeed, it is We who sent down the message, and indeed, We will be its guardian" (al-Hijr 15:9). The article explains that "the message" here includes both the Qur'an and the Sunnah, since the Sunnah is a further revelation that explains the Qur'an. Allah also prescribed acts of worship as provision against this wandering. In prayer the covenant between the servant and his Lord is renewed at least seventeen times a day, as al-Fatihah is recited in every unit. Muslim reports in his Sahih (395) from Abu Hurayrah that whoever prays without reciting the Mother of the Qur'an, his prayer is deficient and incomplete; when asked about praying behind an imam, Abu Hurayrah said to recite it inwardly, for he heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say that Allah said: "I have divided the prayer between Myself and My servant into two halves, and My servant shall have what he asks." When the servant says, "All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds," Allah says, "My servant has praised Me"; at "The Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful," He says, "My servant has extolled Me"; at "Sovereign of the Day of Recompense," He says, "My servant has glorified Me" (and once, "My servant has entrusted his affairs to Me"); at "It is You we worship and You we ask for help," He says, "This is between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks"; and at "Guide us to the straight path...," He says, "This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks."

In every unit of prayer, then, the servant speaks to his Lord, and his Lord hears and answers him, a station of nearness and humility grasped only by those who reflect on its meanings. Prayer is a daily school of servitude that reminds you who you are, for whom you work, where you came from and where you are going. The article also notes that the hadith affirms the attribute of speech for Allah in the manner befitting Him.

## Servitude Is the Way Out

There is no way out of the wandering except by realizing servitude to Allah alone: "And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me. I do not want from them any provision, nor do I want them to feed Me. Indeed, it is Allah who is the [continual] Provider, the firm possessor of strength" (al-Dhariyat 51:56–58). Servitude is not humiliation but the honour of being bound to Allah and freedom from enslavement to desire and Satan. Whoever seeks victory and establishment must begin here, for Allah supports only those who realize servitude: "And Allah will surely support those who support Him. Indeed, Allah is Powerful and Exalted in Might" (al-Hajj 22:40). To seek victory while far from obedience is like seeking fruit without planting the seed. In Makkah the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions were weak and persecuted, yet he said, "Patience, family of Yasir, for your appointed meeting is Paradise" (reported by al-Tabarani in al-Mu'jam al-Awsat, al-Hakim, and Abu Nu'aym in Ma'rifat al-Sahabah, with slight differences in wording). He did not tell them to be patient until kingship came; he called them first to steadfastness in servitude, and victory followed after.

Whoever considers the condition of Muslims in Palestine, Sudan, Kashmir and elsewhere will realize that the road to deliverance is not slogans but returning to Allah. Even in secure societies, there is no escape from inner weakness except by returning to Allah with sincere repentance and reviving the meaning of servitude in hearts before appearances. Each person should ask himself: have I realized the meaning of "It is You we worship and You we ask for help" (al-Fatihah 1:5)? Am I a servant of Allah in private as in public? If I am deprived of the means, does my heart remain with the One who provides them? Here the road of light begins, and here the exit from wandering is written.

## Conclusion

The human journey, from the first prostration to Adam, through the wandering humanity lives in today, to the hope of leaving it by returning to servitude, is one great journey of servitude stretching from the moment of creation to the moment man stands before his Lord. Whoever recognizes Satan's enmity, perceives the wisdom of the Most Merciful and follows the path of servitude sincerely for Allah has been guided to the right way, saved from wandering, and granted tranquillity of heart in this world and light of face in the Hereafter: "And if there should come to you guidance from Me - then whoever follows My guidance will neither go astray [in the world] nor suffer [in the Hereafter]. And whoever turns away from My remembrance - indeed, he will have a depressed life, and We will gather him on the Day of Resurrection blind" (Ta-Ha 20:123–124). The article closes with supplications that Allah make us sincere in His worship, make the Qur'an the spring of our hearts, not make our calamity in our religion, grant this Ummah a course of right guidance, and return us all to Him in a beautiful return.
