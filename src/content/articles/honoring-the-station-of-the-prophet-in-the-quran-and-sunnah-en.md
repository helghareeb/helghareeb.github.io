## Honoring the Station of the Prophet ﷺ in the Noble Qur'an and the Prophetic Sunnah

The article begins from the unique rank of the Prophet ﷺ in Islam: he is the recipient of revelation, the means of its delivery, and the most perfect of creation in character and guidance. This lofty station is clearly reflected in the discourse of the Qur'an, in the structure of Prophetic legislation, and in the method revelation laid down to protect the sanctity of the message and the honor of prophethood. Honoring him in the Shar'i texts was not merely an emotional direction or a moral exhortation; it came as an integrated structure in which rhetorical styles, choices of expression, and practical rulings combine to show his exalted rank and establish his place in the conscience and law of the Ummah.

The Qur'an's styles in this regard vary: honor appears sometimes in the form of address, sometimes in turning away from addressing him directly in particular contexts, sometimes in placing pardon first, in defending him, or in linking complete faith to referring judgment to him and submitting to his decision. The practical Sunnah then translated this honor into legislation that regulates conduct and establishes rulings, showing that violating the honor of the Prophet ﷺ is neither an emotional nor a personal matter, but an issue touching the foundation of the religion and the dignity of revelation. The article aims to trace some manifestations of this honor in the Qur'an, then examine what the practical Sunnah indicates about rulings concerning harm to the Prophet ﷺ, while highlighting the purpose-based framework that governs these rulings and prevents both excess and negligence, through a reading grounded in established texts and interpreted in light of the aims of the Shari'ah, the consequences of rulings, and their general contexts.

## The Qur'an's Care for the Station of the Prophet ﷺ

Whoever reflects on the Book of Allah from beginning to end stands in evident reverence before its great care for the station of the Prophet ﷺ. It is filled with exaltation of his rank, gentleness in addressing him, and acclaim of his standing, in a way that reveals his high status with his Lord. Whoever contemplates how the Qur'an addresses him or speaks of him will find forms of reverence that instill love and veneration for him in the heart and increase the believer's certainty of his greatness. (For more detail, the author refers readers to the article "The Qur'an's Honoring of the Prophet ﷺ: Reflections on the Subtleties of the Qur'an's Speech about the Prophet and Its Address to Him" by Muhammad Yahya Jadu, Tafsir Center for Qur'anic Studies.)

## Qur'anic Styles of Revering the Prophet ﷺ

**Turning the address away from him to protect his station.** The Qur'an often redirects an address that would naturally be directed at the Prophet ﷺ to his Companions when the context contains something unbefitting his noble station. This is clear in Allah's words: "[Remember, O Muhammad], when Allah showed them to you in your dream as few; and if He had shown them to you as many, you [believers] would have lost courage and would have disputed in the matter [of whether to fight], but Allah saved [you from that]. Indeed, He is Knowing of that within the breasts" (al-Anfal 8:43). The whole context concerns the Prophet ﷺ, yet at the mention of losing courage and disputing, the Qur'an turns the address to his Companions, out of divine gentleness toward His beloved.

**Directing reproach to others in shared situations.** The same appears in the verses of reproach concerning the captives of Badr: "It is not for a prophet to have captives [of war] until he inflicts a massacre [upon Allah's enemies] in the land. You desire the commodities of this world, but Allah desires [for you] the Hereafter. And Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise. If not for a decree from Allah that preceded, you would have been touched for what you took by a great punishment" (al-Anfal 8:67-68). The reproach is directed at the believers, "You desire the commodities of this world," and not at the Prophet ﷺ, though the event occurred in his time, to protect his station. As al-Tabari explained, Allah wanted for them what is purer and more lasting, not the worldly goods toward which souls incline.

**Declaring his prophethood where doubt might arise.** The Qur'an explicitly mentions his prophetic station in contexts where a doubt might be raised, highlighting his prophethood and clearing his name, as in: "O Prophet, indeed We have made lawful to you your wives to whom you have given their due compensation ... [this is] only for you, excluding the [other] believers ... in order that there will be upon you no discomfort. And ever is Allah Forgiving and Merciful" (al-Ahzab 33:50). The explicit mention of prophethood, the statement of his special status, and the removal of difficulty confirm his standing and repel every doubt.

**Shielding his heart from direct exposure to insult.** Out of Allah's kindness to His Prophet ﷺ, the Qur'an does not confront him with the polytheists' insinuations and wounding words but recounts them by way of narration, protecting his heart: "And indeed, those who disbelieve would almost make you slip with their eyes when they hear the message, and they say, 'Indeed, he is mad.' But it is not except a reminder to the worlds" (al-Qalam 68:51-52).

**Joining the right of Allah and the right of His Messenger.** Among the most remarkable styles is a construction that blends the right of Allah and the right of His Messenger: "That you [people] may believe in Allah and His Messenger and honor him and respect him [i.e., the Prophet] and exalt Him [i.e., Allah] morning and afternoon" (al-Fath 48:9). Honoring and respecting are for the Prophet ﷺ and glorification is for Allah, according to the majority of exegetes.

**Placing pardon before reproach.** "Allah has pardoned you, [O Muhammad]; why did you give them permission [to remain behind]? [You should not have] until it was evident to you who were truthful and you knew [who were] the liars" (at-Tawbah 9:43). Pardon is mentioned before the point of reproach, to protect his noble heart and show his lofty rank.

**Linking complete faith to referring judgment to him.** Allah made complete faith depend on referring judgment to him, with no discomfort in the heart and full submission to his decision: "But no, by your Lord, they will not [truly] believe until they make you, [O Muhammad], judge concerning that over which they dispute among themselves and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission" (an-Nisa' 4:65).

**Special praise and distinction.** Among the forms of high praise is describing him as unlettered and making that a sign of his prophethood: "Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered prophet, whom they find written in what they have of the Torah and the Gospel ... So they who have believed in him, honored him, supported him and followed the light which was sent down with him - it is those who will be the successful. Say, [O Muhammad], 'O mankind, indeed I am the Messenger of Allah to you all ...' So believe in Allah and His Messenger, the unlettered prophet, who believes in Allah and His words, and follow him that you may be guided" (al-A'raf 7:157-158). Allah also mentioned him before the other prophets, though he came after them in time: "And [mention, O Muhammad], when We took from the prophets their covenant and from you and from Noah and Abraham and Moses and Jesus, the son of Mary; and We took from them a solemn covenant. That He may question the truthful about their truth. And He has prepared for the disbelievers a painful punishment" (al-Ahzab 33:7-8).

Allah Himself undertook his defense and marshaled for his support His soldiers of heaven and earth: "If you two [wives] repent to Allah, [it is best], for your hearts have deviated. But if you cooperate against him - then indeed Allah is his protector, and Gabriel and the righteous of the believers and the angels, moreover, are [his] assistants. Perhaps his Lord, if he divorced you [all], would substitute for him wives better than you ..." (at-Tahrim 66:4-5). And Allah crowned this honor by swearing by his noble life: "By your life, [O Muhammad], indeed they were, in their intoxication, wandering blindly" (al-Hijr 15:72). Allah swears only by what is great, so this was an honor given to no one else and a sign of his exalted rank.

## From Qur'anic Honor to Practical Legislation

This profound honor, evident in the Qur'an's address, styles, precedence, gentleness, and defense, was not merely meanings recited or principles stated. It was translated in the life of the Ummah into practical legislation, binding rulings, and disciplined conduct, shown in the Prophet's own life, rulings, and stances, and in how he dealt with those who harmed him or attacked his honor. The question that follows is how the practical Sunnah expressed this station, and what ruling the Shari'ah established for one who dared to harm the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in word or deed.

## The Practical Sunnah on the Ruling for Harming the Prophet ﷺ

Here the Sunnah moves from theoretical statement to practical application, showing unambiguously that harming the Prophet ﷺ is not a personal or emotional matter but an assault on the foundation of the religion and a violation of the sanctity of the message, requiring a stance that preserves his right, protects the dignity of the religion, and upholds justice as Allah and His Messenger intended. Through a number of authentic, established incidents, the article shows how the Prophet ﷺ dealt with different forms of harm, how he affirmed rulings and directed actions, and that defending his honor is a Shar'i duty governed by revelation and its aims, far from both excess and negligence.

**The two young Ansaris and Abu Jahl.** Al-Bukhari reports in his Sahih (3141) from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf that on the day of Badr, while standing in the ranks, he found himself between two young men of the Ansar. Each in turn quietly asked him whether he knew Abu Jahl, saying he had been told that Abu Jahl reviled the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and swearing that if he saw him he would not leave him until the one fated to die first had died. When 'Abd al-Rahman pointed Abu Jahl out, the two rushed at him with their swords and struck him until they killed him, then went to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and told him. Each claimed to have killed him; the Prophet ﷺ examined their swords and said, "Both of you killed him," and awarded his spoils to Mu'adh ibn 'Amr ibn al-Jamuh. The two were Mu'adh ibn 'Afra' and Mu'adh ibn 'Amr ibn al-Jamuh.

**Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf.** Al-Bukhari reports in his Sahih (4037) from Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Who will deal with Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, for he has harmed Allah and His Messenger?" Muhammad ibn Maslamah stood and asked whether he would like him to kill him, and he said yes. Muhammad ibn Maslamah asked permission to say something [to deceive Ka'b], and was permitted. He went to Ka'b, complained that "this man" had burdened them with requests for charity, and asked for a loan of one or two loads of dates. Ka'b demanded a pledge, first their women, then their sons, which they refused as a disgrace, and they agreed to pledge their weapons instead. Muhammad ibn Maslamah came at night with Abu Na'ilah, Ka'b's foster brother, and others. Ka'b came down from his fortress despite his wife's warning; Muhammad ibn Maslamah, under the pretext of smelling the perfume in his hair, took hold of his head and told his companions to strike, and they killed him. Then they came to the Prophet ﷺ and informed him.

**Khalid ibn Sufyan al-Hudhali.** Ibn Khuzaymah reports in his Sahih (982) from 'Abd Allah ibn Unays that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, having learned that Khalid ibn Sufyan ibn Nubayh al-Hudhali was gathering forces against him near 'Uranah and 'Arafat, sent him saying, "Go and kill him." 'Abd Allah set out, prayed 'Asr while walking and gesturing so as not to lose the opportunity, reached him among his women, walked with him while presenting himself as an Arab who had come to join him against "this man," and when the chance came, killed him with his sword. On his return the Prophet ﷺ gave him a staff and, when asked why, said: "It is a sign between me and you on the Day of Resurrection, and the fewest of people that day will be those who carry staffs." 'Abd Allah kept it tied to his sword all his life and asked that it be buried with him, and it was placed in his shroud.

**The day of the conquest of Makkah.** Al-Diya' al-Maqdisi reports in *al-Ahadith al-Mukhtarah* (1054) from Mus'ab ibn Sa'd, from his father, that on the day of the conquest of Makkah the Messenger of Allah ﷺ granted safety to the people except four men and two women, saying: "Kill them even if you find them clinging to the curtains of the Ka'bah": 'Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl, 'Abd Allah ibn Khatal, Miqyas ibn Subabah, and 'Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi al-Sarh. Ibn Khatal was caught clinging to the curtains of the Ka'bah and killed by Sa'id ibn Hurayth, who outran 'Ammar ibn Yasir. Miqyas was caught by people in the market and killed. 'Ikrimah took to sea, and when a storm struck and the sailors told the passengers to call on Allah alone because their gods would not avail them, he said that if nothing but sincere devotion to Allah could save him at sea, nothing else could save him on land; he vowed to go to Muhammad ﷺ and place his hand in his, confident he would find him forgiving and generous, and he came and embraced Islam. 'Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi al-Sarh hid with 'Uthman ibn 'Affan, who brought him to the Prophet ﷺ when people were pledging allegiance and asked him to accept his pledge. The Prophet ﷺ looked at him three times, declining each time, then accepted it after the third, and said to his Companions: "Was there not among you a sensible man who would rise against this one when he saw me withhold my hand from his pledge, and kill him?" They said: "How were we to know what was in your mind, O Messenger of Allah?" He said: "It is not fitting for a prophet to have a treacherous eye."

**The blind man's slave-mother.** Al-Diya' al-Maqdisi reports in *al-Ahadith al-Mukhtarah* (177) from Ibn 'Abbas that a blind man had an *umm walad* (a slave woman who had borne him children) who used to revile the Prophet ﷺ and disparage him; he would forbid her and rebuke her, but she would not stop. One night, when she spoke against the Prophet ﷺ again, he took a short blade and killed her. When this was mentioned to the Prophet ﷺ, he gathered the people and adjured whoever had done it to stand. The blind man came forward trembling and explained that she was the mother of his two sons, who were like two pearls, and had been gentle and kind to him, but that she kept reviling the Prophet ﷺ despite his warnings, until the previous night he could not bear it and killed her. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Bear witness that her blood is of no account (*hadar*)."

**The Jewish woman.** Al-Diya' al-Maqdisi reports in *al-Ahadith al-Mukhtarah* (547) from 'Ali that a Jewish woman used to revile and disparage the Prophet ﷺ, and a man strangled her until she died, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ declared her blood to be of no account.

## The Purpose-Based Framework for These Rulings

Before these texts are misunderstood or torn from their Shar'i context, the author sets out the framework the Shari'ah established in this area. When the Shari'ah laid down this ruling for one who harms the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, it did so to protect the religion, preserve the station of prophethood, and block the door to contempt for revelation, not out of bare emotion or personal revenge, for the Prophet ﷺ was the furthest of people from self-interest and the greatest in forbearance and forgiveness. But the Shari'ah, looking to the consequences of words and deeds, knew that laxity regarding his honor opens the door to attacking the message itself, strips revelation of its dignity, and exposes the religion to mockery and contempt. The ruling was thus a safeguard for the Ummah's creed, not vengeance against a person and not a transgression of justice.

For this reason, the article stresses, the ruling is bound by the controls of the Shari'ah and entrusted to those in authority over the Muslims and to their judiciary, not to the whims of individuals, the chaos of anger, or uncontrolled reactions. The same Shari'ah that honored the Prophet ﷺ forbade injustice, blocked the means to strife, and prevented the religion from being used as a cover for disorder or aggression. This legislation thus unites honoring the Prophet ﷺ, preserving the religion, protecting society, and establishing justice, in a way that closes the path of excess as it closes the door of negligence. Given his station in the Book of Allah and the Sunnah, the Ummah is duty-bound to honor him in knowledge, love, following, and support, and to beware of harming him in word or deed or neglecting his honor in the name of leniency or modernity.

## Conclusion

Honoring the station of the Prophet ﷺ in Islam is neither an abstract idea nor a sermon detached from legislation. It is a firm principle on which the texts of the Qur'an concur, shown in its rhetorical styles, and confirmed in the practical Sunnah through clear rulings and disciplined stances that guard the station of prophethood and the sanctity of the message. The rulings concerning one who harms the Prophet ﷺ came within a precise purpose-based framework that balances honoring him, preserving the religion, protecting society, and preventing disorder and aggression, making this the prerogative of the Muslims' authorities and judiciary rather than of individual reactions or uncontrolled passions. In this way the Shari'ah closed the door of excess as well as that of negligence, and established justice as Allah and His Messenger intended.

Keeping this integrated structure in view, the author concludes, recalibrates how many contemporary problems are approached and shows that honoring the Prophet ﷺ is not a slogan or a passing enthusiasm, but a scholarly, legal, and behavioral commitment built on correct understanding of revelation, staying within its limits, and supporting him with knowledge and insight before emotion. The Ummah today most needs to renew this sound understanding of its Prophet's station: one that honors him as Allah honored him, supports him as Allah prescribed, and protects his Sunnah from distortion and corrupt interpretation, so that the message of Islam remains safeguarded, the station of prophethood preserved, and the religion established on justice and guidance until Allah inherits the earth and all upon it.
