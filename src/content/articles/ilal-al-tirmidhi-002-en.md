## The Second Article in the Series Explaining “'Ilal al-Hadith of Imam al-Tirmidhi” by the Hafiz Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali

## Introduction

*Sharh 'Ilal al-Imam al-Tirmidhi* by the hafiz Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali is one of the foundational books of hadith, offering an in-depth explanation of the *'ilal* (hidden defects) of hadiths according to Imam al-Tirmidhi. This article belongs to a continuing series that aims to document and understand this great book, relying on the original texts while carrying out comparisons between manuscripts and printed editions, and on the major scholarly issues they contain relating to the science of hadith and its writing and compilation.

## The Importance of Critical Verification of Hadith Texts

In this part, our study went beyond a traditional reading of *Sharh 'Ilal al-Tirmidhi* to the need to verify the texts and compare them with the original manuscripts. There is a large time gap between the old printed editions and the period in which the work was written, which has made critical verification and scholarly documentation an urgent need, especially given differences in letters, such as a hamza written above or below, which can affect the electronic transmission of hadith.

These discrepancies reflect the fact that the hafiz Ibn Rajab quoted many texts from books, sometimes naming them and sometimes not. This requires confirming the original sources in order to provide accurate documentation for today's students of knowledge and researchers, and to meet the requirements of contemporary graduate studies.

## Narrator Criticism and the Standards of the Scholars

The scholars have made clear that *al-jarh wa al-ta'dil* (the criticism and accreditation of narrators) is legitimate in Islamic law, and it is an important part of the science of hadith narrators. There are examples of narrators who were criticized yet also have accreditations that lessen the effect of that criticism. This underscores the importance of knowing a narrator precisely, so that he is not criticized except after verification, and what is mentioned in the book: that the hadith scholars permitted *al-jarh wa al-ta'dil* in the interest of understanding hadith, according to strict criteria.

## The History of Writing and Compiling Hadith

Investigating the history of writing hadith and compiling it is one of the most prominent themes of this commentary. Ibn Rajab explains that hadith was first recorded through individual writing by the Companions and their Successors, with disagreement over whether memorization or writing was preferable. He also explains that organized, formal compilation (*tadwin*) appeared later as a gathering of scattered material, not merely as scattered writing.

This development from writing to compilation enriched the hadith heritage practically and academically, and led to the emergence of multiple compilations over time, as in the cases of the Companion Zayd ibn Thabit, Imam Malik, al-Zuhri and others.

## Independent Reasoning and Disagreement in Hadith Criticism

The article pointed out how scholars dealt with independent reasoning (*ijtihad*) in hadith criticism, especially in interpreting the terms applied to hadith narrators, with scholars differing in their classifications such as “weak,” “reliable,” and “disputed.” It also mentioned that some jurists took a position against writing down hadith and preferred to preserve it orally, while others permitted writing in order to ease its reception and memorization.

## Technical Challenges in Editing Old Texts

The article also addressed the technical problems facing the critical editing of old hadith texts using computers, such as the problem of differing letter marks, which affects search results. This requires ongoing technical effort and scholarly responsibility in reviewing the manuscripts.

## The Role of Scholars and Hadith Compilations

The article presented the role of a number of hadith scholars and their works, such as Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Abi 'Aruba, Ibn Rajab and others, and the way in which hadiths and their defects were arranged and gathered, which led to the completion of the picture of the science of hadith in both text and method.

It also explained the shift from a communal obligation (*fard kifaya*) to an individual obligation (*fard 'ayn*) in memorizing and compiling hadith, justified by the decline in the level of the knowledge of the early generations (*al-salaf*) and its necessity for the Muslim community.

## Conclusion

This part of the commentary highlights the need to grasp the scholarly and historical dimensions of *Sharh 'Ilal al-Tirmidhi* by the hafiz Ibn Rajab. It is not merely a legal or textual commentary, but an important work in documenting the hadith sciences and explaining the defects of narrations. It combines precise religious knowledge with historical method, and is an indispensable scholarly legacy for students of hadith and researchers in the sciences of the Sunnah.
