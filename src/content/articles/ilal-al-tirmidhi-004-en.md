The integrated scholarly article: Explaining the narrator's condition and the methodology of Imam Ibn Rajab in the book *'Ilal al-Tirmidhi*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Introduction

In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to Allah, and blessings and peace be upon our master Muhammad, and upon his family and all his Companions. This article presents, in an integrated scholarly style, the lecture of the fourth session of the commentary on *'Ilal al-Tirmidhi* by Imam Zayn al-Din Ahmad ibn 'Abd al-Rahman Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali, may Allah have mercy on him. It focuses on the concept of "the narrator's condition" in hadith science and on Ibn Rajab's method in arranging and researching the issues of *jarh* and *ta'dil*, while dealing with the topic of narrating from people of innovation, the importance of the *isnad*, and the rules of *ijtihad* in establishing or weakening a hadith, citing the scholarly formulations and applied examples that Shaykh Ibn Rajab presented.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Narrator's Condition and Its Meaning in Hadith Science

## Defining the Condition and Its Effect on Using a Hadith as Proof

"The narrator's condition" is the set of qualities a narrator possesses that make his narration usable as proof, or qualities that make it unusable as proof. It is an extension of the concept of "accreditation" (*ta'dil*) that the early generations spoke about, especially the Successors. Ibn Rajab, may Allah have mercy on him, treats this question comprehensively, with an extensive and unique organization that reveals the precision with which hadith narrators were dealt with, and he shows that this condition is not fixed but varies in its details.

## The Effect of the Narrator's Condition on Accepting a Narration

- The presence of qualities of probity or of a defect directly affects the acceptance or rejection of a hadith.
- Ibn Rajab provides a detailed survey of many issues of hadith terminology concerning what is said about the narrator's condition.
- The detailing of these matters in his book is considered a qualitative addition to the conventional books of terminology, as it organizes the studies and arranges them in a scholarly order, giving students of knowledge a clearer understanding.
- The aim is to reach an objective, systematic comprehension that helps teacher and student alike understand *jarh* and *ta'dil* in depth.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Narrating from a Narrator Associated with Innovation

## Types of Innovation and Their Effect on the Narrator's Probity

- Ibn Rajab reviews the types of innovation as they appear in the books of creed, such as those mentioned by al-Suyuti, arranged according to their effect on the narrator's probity.
- Ibn Rajab gathers this classification and applies it within the limits of *jarh* and *ta'dil* without entering into the details of creed, which are studied in the field of creed.
- He draws lines between grave and minor innovations and their effect on accepting a narration, with careful observation of whether the narrator is an innovator or not and how this affects trust in the narration.

## Ibn Rajab's Method in Arranging the Question of Narration from People of Innovation

- A sequential arrangement focused on application, making clear that the aim is not mere theoretical statement but practical application.
- His use of the books of hadith terminology with a new arrangement and organization that makes the issues easier to understand and links them to the narrator's conduct.
- The modern trend's benefiting from this arrangement to improve the teaching of hadith terminology and to help students grasp the scholarly reality.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Disagreements Between the Early and Later Scholars on Accepting the Narration of Innovators

- Ibn Rajab clarifies that the disagreement between the early and later scholars on this question is "a disagreement in application," not "a fundamental disagreement" or a divergence.
- He explains that the resulting differences are the product of differences in time and circumstance, but the principle is agreed upon.
- He warns against making the disagreements bear more than their due, and recommends regarding them as changeable, human-made scholarly *ijtihad*.
- He calls for taking scholarly reality and practical application into account when evaluating the narration of innovators, without severity or prior rejection.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Importance of the Isnad and Its Role in Preserving the Sunnah

- Ibn Rajab affirms that the *isnad* is part of the religion and a means of preserving the Prophetic Sunnah.
- He responds to orientalists who claimed that the chains are fabricated and invented, affirming the soundness of their preservation across the generations of the Companions and the Successors.
- He stresses the condition of studying the *isnad* and examining the connection of the chain in order to preserve trust in the narration.
- He draws attention to the need for deliberation and verification before rejecting narrations, noting that the connection of the chain is a basic condition for a controlled probability, and he gives examples of differences and methods of evaluating chains.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Ijtihad in Jarh and Ta'dil and Its Rules

- Imam Ibn Rajab sets out that *ijtihad* in *jarh* and *ta'dil* has strict rules and conditions derived from the principles of jurisprudence (*usul al-fiqh*).
- He points out that not every *ijtihad* is accepted, nor is every opinion rejected; rather, what counts is the one whose *ijtihad* is supported by practice according to a consistent method.
- He regards *ijtihad* in *jarh* and *ta'dil* as an inductive work based on reality and the narrator's apparent conduct, without speculating about intentions.
- He advises not to invalidate one *ijtihad* with another except with evidence that strengthens the second *ijtihad*.
- He mentions that Imam al-Tirmidhi built his entire book on practice (*'amal*), drawing on the practice of the Companions and the Successors, so the actual situation matters.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Standing of the Musnad of Imam Abu Hanifah Among the Books of Sunnah

- The *Musnad* attributed to Imam Abu Hanifah was not authored by him; rather, others compiled it after him.
- There are several *Musnads* that go back to Imam Abu Hanifah, and the grades of the hadiths in them vary, some being *hasan* (good) and some weaker.
- It is not permissible to disparage Imam Abu Hanifah without evidence. He is a man of recognized standing; others disagreed with him on some issues, but he remained an important scholarly reference.
- The *Musnad* of Imam Abu Hanifah contains accepted and rejected hadiths, and this must be understood in light of the body of narrations and the sciences related to chains.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conclusion

The fourth session of Imam Ibn Rajab's commentary on *'Ilal al-Tirmidhi* presents a systematic scholarly framework for studying the narrator's condition and narration from people of innovation, dealing with the issues from cognitive and practical angles while taking into account *ijtihad* and actual practice in evaluating hadiths. It also stresses the importance of the *isnad* and its pivotal role in preserving the Prophetic Sunnah, while adhering to the rules of *ijtihad* in *jarh* and *ta'dil*.

This commentary has a clear impact on the development of the hadith sciences. It arranges the topics of terminology in a way that helps students understand and analyze, and it facilitates grounding the scholarly understanding of the issues of the hadith *isnad* and the conditions for its acceptance and rejection.
