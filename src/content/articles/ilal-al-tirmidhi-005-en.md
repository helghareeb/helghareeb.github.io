The fifth article in the series explaining the book *'Ilal al-Tirmidhi*: the topic of *al-jarh wa al-ta'dil* (narrator criticism and accreditation) according to the method of the people of hadith

Introduction\
The topic of *jarh* and *ta'dil* is among the most important topics in the science of hadith, because of its profound effect on the acceptance or rejection of hadiths, and hence on determining the soundness and acceptability of a chain of transmission. In this fifth article of the commentary series, an extensive explanation is presented based on the words of the great scholars, foremost among them Imam Ibn Rajab in his collection of the statements of the scholars of *jarh* and *ta'dil*, and Imam al-Tirmidhi, who with great effort transmitted important statements from Imam 'Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak, on which he relied in evaluating narrators.

The Role of Imam Ibn Rajab and Imam al-Tirmidhi\
Imam Ibn Rajab, may Allah have mercy on him, collected the statements of the scholars on *jarh* and *ta'dil*, among them al-Tirmidhi's transmissions from Imam 'Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak, who belongs to the generation of early scholars who narrated abundantly. In accreditation he follows the principle of accreditation by practice; that is, he narrates only from those he trusts. Ibn Rajab confirmed this, saying that everyone from whom 'Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak narrated is trustworthy in his view, and this accreditation constitutes a collective accreditation that strengthens the credibility of the narrators he examined.

Challenges in Accrediting Narrators\
The text addressed major challenges in accrediting certain narrators, especially the figure of al-Hakam al-Ayli, who appears under different names in the sources, which led to confusion in determining his *kunyah* (patronymic) and his son. This problem showed the importance of precise verification of a hadith's chain, and of not relying on texts that are corrupted or confused between scholars. It also pointed to the role of the verifying scholars, such as Ibn 'Adi and al-Khatib al-Baghdadi, in correcting some of these errors, and explained the rules of manuscript verification.

Jarh and Ta'dil Between Ijtihad and Disagreement\
The article mentioned scholarly *ijtihad* (independent reasoning) in the field of *jarh* and *ta'dil*, where scholars differed in evaluating narrators, which is natural, just as in the sciences of jurisprudence. General rules were clarified, among them that the narrators from whom 'Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak narrated were necessarily trustworthy, and that being alone in a transmission (*tafarrud*) may lead to a hadith being weak or not accepted, in line with the views of Imam Ahmad and Ibn Hajar.

The Effect of Lost Books and Multiple Copies\
The article touched on the effect of strife and wars, especially the burning of books that befell Shaykh Ibn Rajab's commentary on al-Tirmidhi's *Jami'*, and the effect of this on the preservation of the scholarly heritage, and the importance of having multiple copies and different manuscripts that aid precise scholarly verification. The explanation of this issue showed how the multiplicity of copies is an important resource that helps in correcting texts and determining their soundness.

The Difference Between Collective and Individual Accreditation\
The text clarified the difference between collective accreditation, which is based on a narrator relying on a group of trustworthy narrators who narrate from him, and individual accreditation, which depends on the trust of one particular narrator only. Imam 'Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak adopted the principle of narrating only from those he trusted, which makes accreditation in his case of a coherent collective kind, and this raises the standing of the hadiths he details.

Conclusion\
The topic of *jarh* and *ta'dil* stands out as a pillar that the hadith sciences cannot do without. By taking in the statements of great scholars such as Ibn Rajab, al-Tirmidhi, Ibn 'Adi and al-Khatib al-Baghdadi, the importance of scholarly precision and scrutiny of a hadith's chain becomes clear, especially in light of the historical circumstances that affected the preservation of the heritage. This study affirms the necessity of returning to multiple sources and discussing the question of disagreement and *ijtihad* in evaluating narrators.

Main references used:

- The texts and statements of Imam Ibn Rajab on the science of *jarh* and *ta'dil*.
- Imam al-Tirmidhi's explanations concerning the statements of Imam 'Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak.
- The investigations of Ibn 'Adi and al-Khatib al-Baghdadi in the field of hadith criticism.
- Field studies on the effect of the loss of manuscripts and books on the preservation of narrations.

This article is a scholarly summary and extensive analysis of the fifth topic of the commentary on *'Ilal al-Tirmidhi*, and it is a useful step in the series explaining this great book.
