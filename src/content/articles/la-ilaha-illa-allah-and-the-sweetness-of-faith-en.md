## La Ilaha Illa Allah and the Sweetness of Faith

## The First Sermon

All praise belongs to Allah. We praise Him, seek His help and forgiveness, and seek refuge in Him from the evil within ourselves and our bad deeds. Whoever Allah guides, none can lead astray; whoever He lets go astray, none can guide. I bear witness that none is worthy of worship except Allah alone, without partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger. Allah says, in meaning: “O you who believe, be mindful of Allah as He deserves, and do not die except as Muslims” [Al ‘Imran 3:102].

“O mankind, be mindful of your Lord, who created you from a single soul, created its mate from it, and spread from them many men and women. Be mindful of Allah, in whose name you ask one another, and of ties of kinship. Allah is ever watching over you” [Al-Nisa’ 4:1].

“O you who believe, be mindful of Allah and speak upright words. He will put your deeds right for you and forgive your sins. Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger has attained a great triumph” [Al-Ahzab 33:70–71]. To proceed: the best speech is the Book of Allah, the best guidance is the guidance of Muhammad, blessings and peace be upon him, the worst matters are those newly introduced into religion, and every religious innovation is misguidance.[^1]

## The Greatest Legacy We Leave Our Children

Servants of Allah, the greatest thing a Muslim should cherish for himself and his family is the oneness of Allah in worship. It is the foundation of religion and the key to salvation. Sincere concern for our children includes teaching them the meaning of the two testimonies of faith and nurturing them in love and obedience to Allah, while also attending to what improves their livelihoods and benefits them in this world. The earliest covenant taken from the children of Adam was the covenant of servitude to Allah. He says, in meaning: “When your Lord took from the children of Adam—from their loins—their descendants and made them testify concerning themselves: ‘Am I not your Lord?’ They said, ‘Yes, we testify,’ lest you say on the Day of Resurrection, ‘We were unaware of this’” [Al-A‘raf 7:172].

Allah established the proof by sending His messengers and revealing His books. He says: “We would not punish until We had sent a messenger” [Al-Isra’ 17:15]. The messengers came with one call: “We sent no messenger before you except that We revealed to him: ‘None is worthy of worship except Me, so worship Me’” [Al-Anbiya’ 21:25]. This is the purpose for which Allah created jinn and mankind: “I did not create jinn and mankind except to worship Me” [Al-Dhariyat 51:56].

## The Messenger Is Responsible Only for Conveying the Message

The messenger’s task is to convey the message clearly. One who calls others to Allah must explain the truth with wisdom and gentleness. Allah says: “Your duty is only to convey, and Ours is the reckoning” [Al-Ra‘d 13:40]. He also says: “There is no compulsion in religion. Right guidance has become distinct from error. Whoever rejects false objects of worship and believes in Allah has grasped the firmest handhold, which never breaks. Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing” [Al-Baqarah 2:256].

Among the foundations of calling people to Allah’s oneness is His statement: “Say: ‘People of the Scripture, come to a common word between us and you: that we worship none but Allah, associate nothing with Him, and do not take one another as lords besides Allah.’ If they turn away, say: ‘Bear witness that we are Muslims’” [Al ‘Imran 3:64].

This verse calls to a clear foundation, establishes the proof, and teaches steadfastness in religion when an opponent turns away. Our duty is to explain and invite well; the reckoning of creation belongs to Allah.

## There Is a Great Difference Between Resolving a Doubt and Turning Away

Someone who asks in order to learn is not the same as someone who arrogantly rejects the truth after it becomes clear to him.

The first person has a troubling doubt that he wants resolved, a question awaiting an answer, and a heart open to the truth if it becomes clear. He is entitled to have his doubt addressed with patience and gentleness. Allah commanded His Prophet to grant even a polytheist protection so that he could hear the proof: “If any of the polytheists seeks your protection, grant him protection until he hears the words of Allah; then convey him to his place of safety. That is because they are people who do not know” [Al-Tawbah 9:6]. He also says: “Call to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good admonition, and debate with them in the best manner. Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His way, and He knows best those who are guided” [Al-Nahl 16:125].

Abraham, peace be upon him, asked his Lord to show him how He gives life to the dead, saying: “Yes, but so that my heart may be reassured” [Al-Baqarah 2:260]. His question sought greater reassurance, not an expression of doubt in Allah’s power. Let anyone seeking guidance find patience and clear explanation with us.

Turning away from the truth, however, is a dangerous matter. Allah condemns it: “Who is more unjust than one who is reminded of his Lord’s signs, then turns away from them and forgets what his hands have sent ahead?” [Al-Kahf 18:57].

The Sunnah teaches the same lesson. Abu Waqid al-Laythi, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that while the Messenger of Allah, blessings and peace be upon him, was sitting in the mosque with people, three men approached. One saw a gap in the circle and sat in it; another sat behind them; the third turned and left. When the Messenger finished, he said: “Shall I tell you about the three men? One sought shelter with Allah, and Allah sheltered him. Another felt shy, and Allah showed him gracious restraint. The other turned away, and Allah turned away from him.” Al-Bukhari and Muslim both report it.[^2]

This warns against abandoning gatherings of knowledge and guidance out of aversion to them. Let us pursue what benefits us and ask about what we do not understand.

Even more explicit is the Prophet’s statement in Abu Hurayrah’s report: “All of my community will enter Paradise except those who refuse.” They asked, “Messenger of Allah, who would refuse?” He said: “Whoever obeys me enters Paradise, and whoever disobeys me has refused.” Al-Bukhari reports it.[^3]

A Muslim must not deliberately remain ignorant of what he needs to know of his religion when he is able to learn. Allah says: “Ask the people of knowledge if you do not know” [Al-Nahl 16:43]. Judging whether the proof has been established against a particular person, or whether his excuse no longer applies, involves conditions and detail referred to qualified scholars. Mere conjecture does not permit violating anyone’s honor, life, or property. The purpose here is to hold ourselves accountable for seeking and acting on the truth, and to be gentle with those who need instruction.

## Noah’s Counsel, Peace Be upon Him

Servants of Allah, let us reflect on Noah’s counsel to his son: how it combines reverence for Allah’s oneness, encouragement to remember Him, and warning against associating partners with Him and against arrogance.

Imam Ahmad in the *Musnad* and al-Bukhari in *Al-Adab al-Mufrad* report from ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Amr ibn al-‘As, may Allah be pleased with them both, that the Messenger of Allah said: “When Allah’s Prophet Noah approached death, he said to his son: ‘I shall relate my counsel to you. I command you to do two things and forbid you two things. I command you to say: “None is worthy of worship except Allah.” If the seven heavens and the seven earths were placed in one pan of a balance, and “None is worthy of worship except Allah” in the other, it would outweigh them. If the seven heavens and the seven earths were a closed ring, “None is worthy of worship except Allah” would break them apart. And [I command you to say]: “Glory and praise be to Allah,” for this is the prayer of everything, and through it creation is provided for. I forbid you associating partners with Allah and arrogance.’”[^4]

How tremendous this declaration is, and how tremendous its affirmation that Allah alone deserves worship!

It is for this blessed declaration that the books were revealed, the messengers sent, and the balances of justice established, and by it people are distinguished as wretched or blessed. Allah says to His Prophet: “Know that none is worthy of worship except Allah, and seek forgiveness for your sin and for believing men and women. Allah knows your movements and your resting place” [Muhammad 47:19]. He thus began with knowledge of it before acting upon it.

It was Abraham’s legacy to his descendants: “He made it an enduring word among his descendants, so that they might return” [Al-Zukhruf 43:28]. It was the final matter about which Jacob sought reassurance as death approached him: “Were you present when death approached Jacob, when he said to his sons: ‘What will you worship after me?’ They said: ‘We will worship your God and the God of your fathers Abraham, Ishmael, and Isaac—one God—and to Him we submit’” [Al-Baqarah 2:133].

Consider this: a prophet was dying. He did not ask about wealth to be divided or status to be inherited. He asked: “What will you worship after me?” Whoever wishes to understand Jacob’s life should read his final question.

## Belief in Allah and Disavowal of Associating Partners with Him

We have just heard Allah’s statement concerning Abraham: “He made it an enduring word among his descendants.” What was this word that Allah preserved among his offspring? Read the two preceding verses, and the answer becomes clear: “When Abraham said to his father and his people: ‘I am free of what you worship, except the One who created me; He will guide me.’ And he made it an enduring word among his descendants, so that they might return” [Al-Zukhruf 43:26–28].

The enduring word among Abraham’s descendants is precisely this: disavowal followed by affirmation. “I am free of what you worship” expresses “no deity”; “except the One who created me” expresses “except Allah.” The declaration of Allah’s oneness is not founded on affirmation alone, but on two pillars: negation and affirmation, disavowal and allegiance. Someone who affirms without negating has not affirmed Allah’s exclusive right to worship.

Allah therefore mentioned rejecting false objects of worship before believing in Him: “Whoever rejects false objects of worship and believes in Allah has grasped the firmest handhold, which never breaks. Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing” [Al-Baqarah 2:256]. Removal precedes adornment: one does not build on uncleared ground. This is the substance of every messenger’s call: “We sent a messenger to every community: ‘Worship Allah and avoid false objects of worship’” [Al-Nahl 16:36]. Command and prohibition; worship and avoidance.

Allah made Abraham and those with him a particular example in this matter: “You have a good example in Abraham and those with him, when they said to their people: ‘We are free of you and of what you worship besides Allah. We reject you, and enmity and hatred have become manifest between us and you forever, until you believe in Allah alone’” [Al-Mumtahanah 60:4].

Pay attention to two precise qualifications in this verse, which many people misunderstand through either excess or neglect.

First, disavowal of polytheists concerns their religion and their worship of others besides Allah. It is not permission to wrong them or diminish their rights. The same Allah who revealed this verse revealed, a few verses later in the same surah: “Allah does not forbid you from treating kindly and justly those who have not fought you because of religion or expelled you from your homes. Allah loves those who act justly” [Al-Mumtahanah 60:8]. It is separation in religion, coupled with justice and kindness toward those who have not fought us.

Second, the endpoint named in the verse is belief in Allah alone. Someone’s disagreement must not prevent us from inviting him to guidance wisely. Abraham sought forgiveness for his father because of a promise he had made to him; then he disavowed him when it became clear that he was an enemy of Allah, as Al-Tawbah 9:114 explains.

Disavowal therefore does not mean a harsh disposition or coarse manners. It means separation in belief together with mercy toward creation. This has already appeared in the verse: “Say: ‘People of the Scripture, come to a common word…’” [Al ‘Imran 3:64]. Its conclusion, “Bear witness that we are Muslims” [Al ‘Imran 3:64], is itself a declaration of disavowal and allegiance.

Allah states this sincerity in its clearest form: “Say: ‘My prayer, my sacrifice, my life, and my death are for Allah, Lord of the worlds, who has no partner. This I have been commanded, and I am the first of those who submit’” [Al-An‘am 6:162–163]. He also says: “The places of worship belong to Allah, so do not invoke anyone along with Allah” [Al-Jinn 72:18].

## The Reality of the Testimony and What It Requires

The Prophet explained that the declaration of Allah’s oneness includes worshipping Allah alone and rejecting worship of all others. Tariq ibn Ashyam al-Ashja‘i, may Allah be pleased with him, heard the Messenger of Allah say: “Whoever says, ‘None is worthy of worship except Allah,’ and rejects what is worshipped besides Allah, his property and life are inviolable, and his reckoning is with Allah.”[^5]

The testimony is an acknowledgment, a meaning, and a commitment—not syllables contradicted by worship of someone else. Anyone who outwardly professes Islam is treated according to its rulings; his inner reality belongs to Allah. Muslims do not probe hearts or hasten to declare someone outside Islam.

The Prophet told Mu‘adh ibn Jabal, may Allah be pleased with him: “Allah’s right over His servants is that they worship Him and associate nothing with Him. The servants’ right granted by Allah is that He not punish those who associate nothing with Him.” Al-Bukhari and Muslim report it.[^6] In a single statement he joined worship to the negation of association; sincere worship of Allah is fundamental to religion.

‘Abd Allah ibn Mas‘ud, may Allah be pleased with him, said: “The Prophet said one statement, and I said another. The Prophet said: ‘Whoever dies while invoking a rival besides Allah enters the Fire.’ I said: ‘Whoever dies without invoking a rival to Allah enters Paradise.’” Al-Bukhari and Muslim report it.[^7]

## Faith Is Not Pure While Tainted by Association

Allah censures those who acknowledge His lordship yet direct worship elsewhere: “Most of them do not believe in Allah without associating others with Him” [Yusuf 12:106]. Acknowledging Allah as Creator does not suffice while invoking someone else in worship or associating another with Him. Major association nullifies deeds: “It has been revealed to you and to those before you: ‘If you associate others with Allah, your work will surely be rendered worthless, and you will surely be among the losers’” [Al-Zumar 39:65].

Not all association consists of erected idols that people worship. Some of it creeps invisibly within the heart. The Prophet said: “What I fear most for you is minor association.” They asked, “What is minor association, Messenger of Allah?” He said: “Showing off.” Ahmad reports it, and al-Albani authenticated it.[^8]

Showing off corrupts the sincerity of the deed it affects. It is minor association, and is not equated with major association in every ruling. A servant must watch his intention and resist seeking people’s praise. Among the transmitted supplications is: “O Allah, I seek refuge in You from knowingly associating anything with You, and I seek Your forgiveness for what I do not know.”[^9]

Shortly, in the hadith about the sweetness of faith, you will hear the third of three qualities: “To hate returning to disbelief as one would hate being thrown into the Fire.” This is disavowal transformed into an experience of the heart rather than merely a spoken claim.

## Faith Is Speech, Action, and Belief

Servants of Allah, here is a dangerous pitfall that we must address. Someone may say: “Since this declaration carries such weight, it is enough for me to say it and take my ease!”

In the hadith of the card, ‘Abd Allah ibn ‘Amr reports that a man from the Prophet’s community will have ninety-nine scrolls laid out before him on the Day of Resurrection, each extending as far as the eye can see. A card is then brought out bearing: “I testify that none is worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger.” The scrolls are placed in one pan and the card in the other: “The scrolls rose and the card outweighed them, for nothing has weight against the name of Allah.” Al-Tirmidhi reports it and graded it *hasan*; Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim authenticated it.[^10]

We must not make this hadith an argument for abandoning obligations and persisting in sins, for scriptural texts explain one another. The Prophet said: “The people most fortunate to receive my intercession on the Day of Resurrection are those who say, ‘None is worthy of worship except Allah,’ sincerely from their hearts—or souls.”[^11] Sincerity and truthfulness are part of the testimony’s reality and affect the weight and acceptance of deeds.

The belief of Ahl al-Sunnah is that faith comprises speech of the tongue, belief and action of the heart, and action of the limbs. It increases through obedience and decreases through disobedience. Sins and acts of obedience are not equal; nor does every sin, merely by occurring, expel a Muslim from Islam. Texts of promise and warning must be understood together.[^12]

## Its Being Speech, Action, and Belief

Listen to Allah describing the qualities of true believers: “The believers are only those whose hearts tremble when Allah is mentioned, whose faith increases when His verses are recited to them, and who put their trust in their Lord; those who establish prayer and spend from what We have provided them. Those are the true believers. They have ranks with their Lord, forgiveness, and a generous provision” [Al-Anfal 8:2–4].

These verses bring together actions of the heart—fear and reliance—and actions of the limbs—prayer and spending—and praise those who fulfill them. This describes the completeness of their faith; it does not pronounce everyone who falls short in any such deed a disbeliever.

The Prophet said: “Faith has seventy-odd—or sixty-odd—branches. The highest is saying, ‘None is worthy of worship except Allah,’ and the lowest is removing harm from the road. Modesty is a branch of faith.” Muslim reports this wording; its basis also occurs in al-Bukhari.[^13]

Consider: saying “None is worthy of worship except Allah” is a branch; removing harm, an action of the limbs, is a branch; and modesty, an action of the heart, is a branch. Faith embraces all three.

Regarding its increase and decrease, Allah says: “He is the One who sent tranquility into the hearts of the believers so that they would increase in faith along with their faith. To Allah belong the hosts of the heavens and earth, and Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise” [Al-Fath 48:4]. The increase of faith calls believers to nurture it through remembrance and obedience and by avoiding sin.

The Prophet said: “The adulterer is not a believer while committing adultery; the thief is not a believer while stealing; and the drinker of wine is not a believer while drinking it.”[^14] These sins greatly diminish faith. What is negated is its obligatory completeness; the hadith alone does not mean that someone committing a major sin has left Islam.

## Hope Governed by Sound Principles

Despite all this, no sinner should despair of Allah’s mercy. In Anas’s report, the Prophet said: “Whoever says, ‘None is worthy of worship except Allah,’ with goodness equal to a barley grain’s weight in his heart will leave the Fire… Whoever says, ‘None is worthy of worship except Allah,’ with goodness equal to an atom’s weight in his heart will leave the Fire.” Al-Bukhari and Muslim report it.[^15]

These texts combine hope and fear. A believer hopes for Allah’s favor without feeling secure from His punishment. Someone who dies affirming Allah’s oneness, free from major association, is subject to Allah’s will regarding lesser sins: He may pardon him or punish him, but his ultimate destination is Paradise.

## One’s Ending Is the Fruit of What Came Before

Among the blessings of this declaration is that someone whose life ends with it has succeeded. The Prophet said: “Whoever’s last words are ‘None is worthy of worship except Allah’ will enter Paradise.” Abu Dawud reports it from Mu‘adh, and al-Albani authenticated it.[^16]

A good ending is a favor from Allah. Among the grounds for hoping for it are steadfastness, repentance, and sincere refuge in Him. We do not judge someone’s ultimate fate merely because he cannot speak at death: illness may prevent him from speaking. The Prophet said: “Deeds are judged by their endings.”[^17] Let us therefore act well and ask Allah for steadfastness.

The Prophet gathered this whole subject in two brief instructions. Sufyan ibn ‘Abd Allah al-Thaqafi said: “Messenger of Allah, tell me something about Islam concerning which I need ask no one after you.” He said: “Say, ‘I believe in Allah,’ then remain steadfast.” Muslim reports it.[^18]

He combined the foundation of faith with adherence to obedience: counsel brief in wording and immense in meaning.

## The Meaning and Effect of the Two Testimonies

A Muslim’s saying “I testify that none is worthy of worship except Allah” is an acknowledgment based on knowledge and certainty that no one deserves worship except Allah. Saying “I testify that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah” includes believing what he reported, obeying what he commanded, avoiding what he prohibited, and worshipping Allah as He prescribed.

Among the fruits of this knowledge is awareness of Allah in private and in public. The Prophet explained excellence in worship: “To worship Allah as though you see Him; if you do not see Him, He sees you.”[^19] Let your knowledge that Allah sees you affect your prayer, speech, and dealings. Do not make personal desire the judge over what revelation has established.

## From Knowing Allah’s Oneness to the Sweetness of Faith

Servants of Allah, Allah’s oneness is not merely information to memorize. It is a taste experienced and a sweetness found in the heart. The Prophet brought this together in two great hadiths that are the key to the whole subject.

## Being Content with Allah, His Messenger, and His Religion

Al-‘Abbas ibn ‘Abd al-Muttalib heard the Messenger of Allah say: “He has tasted faith who is content with Allah as Lord, Islam as religion, and Muhammad as Messenger.” Muslim reports it.[^20]

Notice the Prophet’s expression “tasted.” Taste cannot be conveyed by description or apprehended merely by hearing about it. It is known by the person who experiences it directly; one who has not tasted has not known it in that way.

The hadith identifies three foundations of contentment, combining sincere worship, adherence to religion, and belief in the Messenger:

“Content with Allah as Lord”: worshipping Him sincerely, relying on Him, believing in His wisdom, patiently enduring His trials, and not objecting to His law.

“With Islam as religion”: accepting Islam, submitting to it, and seeking guidance in what Allah has revealed.

“With Muhammad as Messenger”: believing him, following his Sunnah, and preferring his guidance to personal desire.

Someone who realizes this contentment experiences the taste of faith to the extent Allah grants him. A person’s attentiveness of heart may weaken for various reasons; a Muslim should not take temporary spiritual fatigue as proof that his faith is invalid. Rather, he should treat his heedlessness and seek his Lord’s help.

## Love of Allah and His Messenger, and Love for Allah’s Sake

Anas ibn Malik reports that the Prophet said: “Three qualities, whoever possesses them will find the sweetness of faith: that Allah and His Messenger be dearer to him than everything else; that he love a person only for Allah’s sake; and that he hate returning to disbelief as he would hate being thrown into the Fire.” Al-Bukhari and Muslim report it.[^21]

These qualities revolve around sincere love: love of Allah and His Messenger, love for Allah’s sake, and hatred of returning to disbelief. This love appears in preferring Allah’s pleasure when it conflicts with personal desire.

**First:** that Allah and His Messenger be dearer than everything else. This is precisely the substance of “None is worthy of worship except Allah”: the deity is the one loved and worshipped. Allah says: “Among people are those who take rivals besides Allah, loving them as they should love Allah. But those who believe are stronger in love for Allah. If only the wrongdoers could see, when they see the punishment, that all power belongs to Allah and that Allah is severe in punishment” [Al-Baqarah 2:165]. He thus identifies association with love directed where it does not belong. He also says: “Say: ‘If your fathers, sons, brothers, spouses, clan, wealth you have acquired, trade whose decline you fear, and homes you delight in are dearer to you than Allah, His Messenger, and striving in His cause, then wait until Allah brings His command. Allah does not guide defiantly disobedient people’” [Al-Tawbah 9:24].

The Prophet said: “None of you truly believes until I am dearer to him than his father, his child, and all people.” Al-Bukhari and Muslim report it.[^22] ‘Abd Allah ibn Hisham said: “We were with the Prophet as he held ‘Umar ibn al-Khattab’s hand. ‘Umar said: ‘Messenger of Allah, you are dearer to me than everything except myself.’ The Prophet said: ‘No, by the One in whose hand is my soul, until I am dearer to you than yourself.’ ‘Umar said: ‘Now, by Allah, you are dearer to me than myself.’ The Prophet said: ‘Now, ‘Umar.’” Al-Bukhari reports it.[^23]

**Second:** to love someone only for Allah’s sake. A believer loves his brother for his faith and righteousness, sincerely advises him, and helps him obey Allah. That love does not end when an immediate benefit disappears. This does not conflict with natural love for family and children or with kindness, justice, and good treatment toward people.

**Third:** to hate returning to disbelief as one would hate being thrown into the Fire. This realizes the pillar of negation—“no deity.” Someone affirming Allah’s oneness does not merely refrain from association; he recoils from it as someone recoils from being pushed into blazing fire. This is a sign of someone whose heart Allah has adorned with faith: “Know that the Messenger of Allah is among you. If he were to obey you in many matters, you would suffer hardship. But Allah has made faith beloved to you and adorned it in your hearts, and has made disbelief, defiance, and disobedience hateful to you. Those are the rightly guided” [Al-Hujurat 49:7]. Contentment with Allah and love of Him come together and produce obedience of heart and limbs. Allah says: “Those who believe and do not mix their faith with wrongdoing will have security, and they are guided” [Al-An‘am 6:82]. The Prophet explained “wrongdoing” here as association.[^24]

You who hope for the sweetness of faith: set your intention right, maintain your obligations, repent to your Lord, remember Him continually, and accompany those who help you obey Him. Do not despair when your soul tires. A servant strives against it and asks Allah to make faith beloved and beautiful in his heart.

May Allah bless me and you through the magnificent Quran and benefit us through its verses and wise reminder. I say these words and seek Allah’s forgiveness for myself, you, and all Muslims for every sin. Seek His forgiveness: He is the All-Forgiving, the Most Merciful.

## The Second Sermon

Praise be to Allah—good and blessed praise, as our Lord loves and is pleased with. I testify that none is worthy of worship except Allah alone, without partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger. May Allah grant abundant blessings and peace to him, his family, and his Companions. To proceed:

The other half of Noah’s counsel remains: he commanded two things and forbade two things.

## The Merit of Glorifying Allah

He said: “‘Glory and praise be to Allah,’ for this is the prayer of everything, and through it creation is provided for.” Allah’s statement confirms this: “The seven heavens, the earth, and everyone within them glorify Him. There is nothing that does not glorify Him with praise, but you do not understand their glorification. He is ever Forbearing, All-Forgiving” [Al-Isra’ 17:44].

The Arabic particle *in* here is negative, meaning “not.” “Thing” is indefinite within a negative construction, giving a universal meaning: everything in existence glorifies Him.

The verse concludes with Allah’s names “the Forbearing” and “the All-Forgiving,” opening the door of repentance. He grants His servants respite and forgives those who repent. Let us feel ashamed of our shortcomings and frequently glorify and praise Him.

The Prophet said: “Two phrases are beloved to the Most Merciful, light on the tongue, and heavy in the balance: ‘Glory and praise be to Allah; glory be to Allah the Magnificent.’” Al-Bukhari and Muslim report it.[^25] He also said: “Whoever says ‘Glory and praise be to Allah’ one hundred times in a day will have his sins erased, even if they are like the foam of the sea.” Al-Bukhari and Muslim report it.[^26]

## Beware of Association and Arrogance

Noah then forbade association and arrogance, both of which corrupt the heart’s servitude to Allah.

Major association is not forgiven for someone who dies upon it without repentance. Allah says: “Allah does not forgive associating anything with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills. Whoever associates anything with Allah has devised a tremendous sin” [Al-Nisa’ 4:48]. As for someone who sincerely repents, Allah accepts his repentance. No one should despair of His mercy.

Concerning arrogance, the continuation of Noah’s counsel itself relates that the Companions asked about it, thinking it might lie in fine sandals, an outfit a man wears, an animal he rides, or companions who sit with him. He rejected all of these explanations and then said: “Disdaining the truth and belittling people.” In *Sahih Muslim*: “Arrogance is rejecting the truth and despising people.” The meaning is rejecting truth and looking down on creation. The Prophet said: “No one with an atom’s weight of arrogance in his heart will enter Paradise.” Muslim reports it.[^27] Allah says: “That home of the Hereafter We assign to those who desire neither exaltation on earth nor corruption. The good outcome belongs to the mindful of Allah” [Al-Qasas 28:83].

Arrogantly rejecting truth and despising people contradict the humility Allah commands. Not every instance of turning away arises from arrogance, but arrogance is among the greatest obstacles to accepting truth. Let the servant humble himself before his Lord and return to what is right when it becomes clear.

## Practical Counsel

Servants of Allah, let us take five practical lessons from this sermon:

1. Frequently say: “None is worthy of worship except Allah alone, without partner. His is the dominion and His is all praise, and He has power over everything,” while keeping its meaning present and acting according to it.[^28]
2. Ask about what you do not know, and seek answers to what troubles you from people of knowledge. Do not let shyness or personal desire prevent learning.
3. Renew your intention in worship and work, and give truth priority over your own desires.
4. Nurture love of Allah and His Messenger by following their guidance, and nurture love for Allah’s sake through sincere counsel and kindness.
5. Set aside a regular portion of glorification and seeking forgiveness, and maintain the established remembrances.

Know that Allah has commanded you to do something He first attributed to Himself and then to His angels who glorify Him: “Allah and His angels send blessings upon the Prophet. O you who believe, send blessings upon him and greet him with peace” [Al-Ahzab 33:56].

O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You sent blessings upon the family of Abraham. Bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You blessed the family of Abraham among all peoples. You are Praiseworthy, Glorious.[^29]

## Supplication

O Allah, forgive us, our parents, and believing men and women. Set our hearts and deeds right, and grant us sincerity, truthfulness, and steadfastness. Help us remember You, thank You, and worship You well. Purify our hearts of association, showing off, and arrogance.

O Allah, provide us with lawful, wholesome sustenance. Help us fulfill our trusts, improve our character, and make us steadfast with the firm word in this life and the Hereafter.

O Allah, have mercy on our dead, heal our sick, relieve the distressed, discharge the debts of those in debt, improve Muslims’ circumstances, and lift oppression from the oppressed.

“Our Lord, grant us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire” [Al-Baqarah 2:201].

Servants of Allah: “Allah commands justice, excellence, and giving to relatives, and forbids indecency, wrongdoing, and aggression. He admonishes you so that you may take heed” [Al-Nahl 16:90].

Remember Allah the Magnificent, and He will remember you. Thank Him for His blessings, and He will increase you. The remembrance of Allah is greater, and Allah knows what you do.
