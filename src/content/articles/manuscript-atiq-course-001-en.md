## Summary of Course 2 - Day One

### Title: Reading Ancient Manuscripts - Absorbing the Phenomena and Applying Them in Practice

### Introduction

The lecture opened with praise of Allah and prayers upon the Prophet, and with a reminder to supplicate that these sessions be sincerely for the sake of Allah the Generous. Participants were urged to make the best use of the lectures and to develop their abilities in reading and analyzing manuscripts (makhtutat).

### Objectives

- **Consolidating writing phenomena:** reinforcing the theoretical understanding of what was studied in the first course through repeated practical exposure.
- **Distinguishing different patterns:** recognizing the shapes of letters and symbols in different manuscripts according to the type of pen and the writer's style.
- **Noting exceptions and new phenomena:** documenting exceptions and rare practices among copyists (nussakh).
- **Deepening analytical skills:** understanding why copyists chose particular methods and connecting the text with its historical context.

### Main Themes

### 1. Linking the First and Second Courses

- Reaffirming the basic writing phenomena such as omission (hadhf) and substitution (ibdal), joining what is normally written separately, and distinguishing between unpointed (muhmal) and pointed (mu'jam) letters.
- Focusing on the importance of “self-practice” by redrawing words and symbols in order to acquire the skill.

### 2. Applied Examples from Manuscripts of *Tahdhib al-Kamal*

- A close study of selected passages from *Tahdhib al-Kamal* by the hadith master (hafiz) al-Mizzi.
- Highlighting the beauty and precision of the script (khatt), with a comparison of the different patterns within the manuscripts.

### 3. Understanding Correction Marks in Texts

- Analysis of correction symbols such as:
  - **The dabba (“clasp” mark):** to indicate a defect in the text.
  - **The mark “صح” (sahh, “correct”):** to dispel doubts or possible errors.
- Clarifying the difference between annotation (tahshiya, a marginal addition) and correction (tashih, a clarification or emendation).

### 4. Challenges Facing the Editor

- Dealing with texts that are unpointed or that omit vowels or letters.
- Inferring from the context and consulting references to establish the reading of obscure words.
- The importance of the editor's commitment to transmitting texts as they are, without change, while noting the differences in the footnotes.

### 5. Detailed Examples and Models

- Explanation of examples of expected errors, such as:
  - Confusion between letters (such as jim and ha').
  - Correcting repeated or omitted words.
  - Dealing with the names of narrators, whose spelling may differ between copies.

### 6. Methodology in Editing

- Clarifying the steps for verifying the soundness of texts by comparing different copies.
- Training participants in critical thinking and in exercising methodical doubt when analyzing texts.

### Advice and Guidance

- **Full concentration during the lectures.**
- **Restudying the phenomena and documenting them through practical exercise.**
- **Engaging with the Shaykh and asking questions to ensure deeper understanding.**
- **Benefiting from the examples of al-Mizzi to learn the skills of close checking.**

### Conclusion

The Shaykh stressed the importance of making use of the coming six days for intensive training, emphasizing the role of self-practice in consolidating the rules and gaining experience. He also called for refining editorial skills by focusing on fine details and learning the methodology of earlier scholars.

**This lesson serves as a practical introduction to developing the skills needed to read and edit ancient manuscripts accurately and professionally.**
