### Lecture Title: Methodology for Dealing with Marginal Notes and Corrections in Ancient Manuscripts

### Summary of the Lecture

The second lecture of the course “Reading Ancient Manuscripts” offers a practical explanation of a precious copy (nuskha nafisa) of *al-Sharh al-Mutawassit 'ala al-Alfiyya* (the intermediate commentary on the *Alfiyya*) by Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Husayn al-'Iraqi. Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari focused on several phenomena in manuscripts, clarifying common errors and the techniques copyists used to document and correct texts.

### Main Themes

### 1. Marginal Notes and Corrections: The Difference Between Them

- **The marginal note (hashiya):** its sign is placed above the word to indicate an external comment or clarification.
- **The correction (tashih):** a mark is placed between two words to indicate where something has dropped out of the text and is to be restored to the original text.
- The importance of the mark “صح” (sahh, “correct”) in confirming that the text in the margin is to be returned to the main text.

### 2. Copyists' Marks and Their Meanings

- **The nail-paring (qulamat al-zufr):** a special form of the sukun, sometimes used as a clarifying mark and interpreted according to the context.
- **A small 'ayn under the letter:** indicates that the letter is unpointed.
- **A small ha' beneath the letter:** to indicate that it is unpointed (muhmal), carrying no dots.
- **Linking the pronoun to its referent:** marks copyists used to show what a pronoun refers to; these are common in copies made by non-Arabs.

### 3. Copyists' Methods of Laying Out Texts

- **Justifying the lines:**
  - If a word is long, it is divided between the line and its end, to avoid the page looking uneven.
  - If a line is short, marks such as “=” are added to fill the gap.
- **Stretching words:** to fit the spaces while preserving the visual balance of the page.

### 4. Corrections in the Margins

- If a word or sentence has dropped out of the text, the copyist writes it in the margin with the mark “صح” (sahh).
- If a word in the margin is not accompanied by the mark “صح”, it is not incorporated into the main text.

### 5. Possible Errors and How to Deal with Them

- **Similarity between words:** such as the words “حرب” (harb, “war”) and “حزب” (hizb, “party”); the copyist clarifies the difference using special marks.
- **Grammatical or linguistic errors:**
  - The copyist leaves the text as it is if the error was in the exemplar he was copying from, placing a “ضبة” (dabba) mark over the text to indicate the error.
  - Sometimes the word “كذا” (kadha, “thus [in the source]”) is used in place of correcting the text.

### 6. The Importance of Studying Copyists' Marks

- These marks show the extent of the copyist's understanding of the text and his ability to transmit it accurately.
- Errors in understanding these marks can lead to inaccurate results when editing texts.

### Lessons Drawn

- The importance of knowing copyists' methods in order to understand manuscript texts.
- The need for caution when incorporating marginal notes into the main text.
- Strengthening the understanding of context by analyzing marks and symbols.

### Recommendations

- Concentrating on studying the earlier lectures to understand recurring copying phenomena.
- Using copyists' marks as a reference for understanding and accurately editing texts.
- Applying the methodology studied to other copies in order to practice the skills acquired.
