### Lesson Title: Uncovering Forgery and Correction in Ancient Manuscripts

### Summary of the Lesson

### The Third Lecture: Reading Ancient Manuscripts

In this lecture, Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari dealt with the phenomena of forgery (tazwir) and correction in ancient manuscripts, presenting practical examples and precise observations on manuscript copies. The lecture focused on the following points.

### Main Elements

### 1. Forgery in Manuscripts

- Shaykh Salih pointed out that deliberate forgery is rare in doctrinal texts compared with names and dates.
- He explained that accusations of forgery are often bound up with sectarian or cultural disputes, such as Sufis accusing Salafis of tampering with certain texts.
- The Shaykh showed how the editor can distinguish forged from unforged texts by comparing different copies and noting the various routes (makharij) through which the texts have come down.

### 2. Distinctive Marks of Copying

- The Shaykh reviewed copyists' use of specific marks to distinguish letters:
  - **A small sad** under the sad to indicate that it is unpointed (muhmal).
  - **A small ha'** under the ha'.
  - **The nail-paring (qulamat al-zufr)** above the ra'.
  - **A dot** under the ta' and the dal.
- The Shaykh pointed to the development of abbreviations such as “مد” (madd) and “صل” (sal), and how they moved from fully written words to shorter, more frequently used symbols.

### 3. Dealing with Doubtful Texts

- The Shaykh explained how copyists dealt with texts whose soundness was doubtful, using symbols such as “صح” (sahh) to confirm that a phrase is correct, or the tatbib (the dabba mark) to correct a text where something has dropped out.
- He stressed that old copies show the development of the use of corrections and symbols, and that the editor must be aware of this development in order to understand manuscripts correctly.

### 4. Evident Techniques of Forgery

- The Shaykh explained that forgery in manuscripts usually appears where there has been scraping or deletion, with new text inserted in a different hand.
- He noted that it is easy for a trained editor to detect forgery by observing the differences in the type of script and by comparing texts.

### 5. The Importance of Reflection and Comparison

- The Shaykh stressed the importance of careful reflection on and scrutiny of texts together with a command of the theoretical principles, emphasizing that the study of manuscripts requires both theoretical understanding and practical experience.
- He presented the example of a rare manuscript containing critical marginal notes that established the soundness of its texts and their historical value.

### Summary of the Lesson

The lecture focused on the importance of a deep understanding of the various phenomena in manuscripts, and highlighted the importance of theoretical and hands-on scrutiny in distinguishing sound texts from forged ones. The Shaykh also called for comparison between copies and reflection on copying phenomena in order to understand the different contexts of texts.
