## Introduction

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened the lecture by praising and extolling Allah, stressing the need for a pure orientation in scholarly research and for making it sincerely for Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He. He began by explaining the place of the sixth course within a series of specialized courses that aim to strengthen precise skills in manuscript studies. This course focuses on understanding the historical and methodological depth involved in identifying the name of the copyist (nasikh) and the date of copying. The Shaykh pointed out that this process is not merely a documentary step; it is a core element in establishing the identity of texts and documenting the time and place to which they belong.

## Themes of the Lecture

### The Importance of the Copyist's Name and the Copying Date

- Manuscripts (makhtutat), like individuals, have a unique identity that depends on the copyist's name and the copying date. This makes it possible to trace the line of their development over time and their position in the chain of copies.
- This information plays a pivotal role in assessing the authenticity of a manuscript and how close it is to the mother text. It is a decisive factor relied upon in the disciplines concerned with critically edited texts.
- An accurate identity helps in analyzing the cultural and social contexts surrounding the manuscript, opening the way to a deeper understanding of the intellectual environments in which it arose.
- This information is a vital tool in textual analysis, since it enables the editor (muhaqqiq) to identify textual overlaps and transformations across generations.

### A Gradual Methodology in Studying Manuscript Sciences

- The Shaykh emphasized the need to follow a gradual approach in learning manuscript studies. Mastery of this discipline, he noted, requires patience and a firm methodology that begins with the fundamentals and rises step by step toward proficiency.
- He stressed a common mistake: trying to move into practical work without sufficient grasp of the theoretical concepts. He explained that this approach often leads to inaccurate results.
- He pointed to the importance of organized practice that reinforces understanding of the theoretical aspects, noting that such practice is the decisive factor in turning knowledge into deep analytical skills.
- The Shaykh proposed creating integrated educational programs that take the researcher by the hand from the stage of theoretical grounding to advanced levels of application.

### Where the Copyist's Name and the Copying Date Are Found in Manuscripts

- The copyist's name and the copying date are most often found at the end of the text, in the place known as the "closing note" (qayd al-khitam), that is, the colophon.
- This information may, however, appear in other places, including:
  - The end of each part, if the copy consists of several parts, which requires examining each section carefully on its own.
  - The title page, which may contain references to the place of copying and the name of the copyist.
  - Recorded audition certificates (sama'at) or ownership notes (quyud al-tamalluk), which sometimes provide indirect information about the copyist's name and the copying date.
- The Shaykh stressed that the absence of this information in one place does not mean it is entirely lost, underlining the importance of searching the copy as a whole methodically.

### The Importance of Verifying the Accuracy of the Information

- The Shaykh addressed the issue of hasty judgment about manuscripts, considering it one of the methodological errors into which some editors fall.
- He pointed to the importance of adopting a precise analytical methodology that takes into account all the possible places where this data may be found.
- He presented practical examples showing how this information can become a key to determining the place of a copy and its importance.
- He stressed using textual and historical clues (qara'in) to understand copies and evaluate them accurately.

### Patterns of Dating in Manuscripts

- The Shaykh discussed four basic patterns that copyists used to date their copying:
  - **Dating in explicit phrases:** the most common and most precise, in which copyists use clear sentences that specify the date.
  - **Dating in numerals:** writing the numbers alone, often used alongside the explicit phrases.
  - **Dating by the abjad reckoning (hisab al-jummal):** a system based on converting letters into numbers, which requires special skill to decode.
  - **Dating with decimal fractions:** a pattern that developed in later periods and reflects an innovative use of symbols.
- The Shaykh pointed out the importance of understanding these different patterns, since failing to grasp them can lead to misinterpreting the texts.

### Challenges in Reading Dates

- The Shaykh highlighted some of the challenges researchers face when trying to read handwritten dates, such as:
  - Obscure letter forms resulting from some copyists' use of abbreviated styles.
  - Joining words or letters in a way that hinders correct reading.
  - Omitting letters or drawing them unclearly.
- The Shaykh explained the importance of practiced experience in reading manuscripts and of absorbing the different writing styles, stressing the need to keep the historical contexts of texts in mind for a deeper understanding.

### The Importance of Practical Work in Editing Manuscripts

- The Shaykh considered practical work to be the backbone of acquiring skills in editing manuscripts.
- He gave examples of how manuscript texts can be used as training tools to improve researchers' analytical abilities.
- He stressed the importance of building collaborative networks among researchers to exchange experience and strengthen collective understanding.
- He pointed to the role of these practices in consolidating the relationship between academic theory and practical application.

## Conclusion

The Shaykh concluded the lecture by emphasizing the importance of discipline in studying manuscript sciences, noting that this field requires a combination of theoretical precision and practical skill. He called for adopting integrated methodologies that link scholarly research with actual practice. He also explained that the next stage will see distinguished teams of researchers selected to work on editing manuscripts of high scholarly value, which will contribute to developing the field and preserving the Arabic heritage.

## Overall Aim

The course aims to enable researchers to identify accurately the copyist's name and the copying date in manuscripts, while strengthening their ability to analyze texts methodically. It also seeks to prepare a generation of distinguished editors capable of safeguarding the Arabic-Islamic heritage in an integrated scholarly manner.
