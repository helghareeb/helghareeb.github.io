**Lesson title: A Critical Analysis of Copying Notes in the Arabic Manuscript**

**Introduction:** Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened the lecture by praising and extolling Allah and invoking blessings on the noble Prophet, praying that these lectures be sincerely for the noble Face of Allah and lead to beneficial knowledge and righteous action. The Shaykh reviewed the importance of studying and analyzing copying notes (quyud al-naskh) in Arabic manuscripts, as an essential element in the field of textual editing (tahqiq) and the preservation of the Islamic intellectual heritage.

**Main themes of the lesson:**

- **What copying notes are and why they matter:**
  - Copying notes, also called *hard al-matn* or the "colophon," are the additional texts that the copyist (nasikh) inserts at the end of the manuscript.
  - These notes usually contain the copyist's details, such as his name and the date on which he finished copying, and sometimes the purpose of the copy.
  - The Shaykh pointed to the temporal and geographical differences in how these notes were worded, stressing that understanding them requires familiarity with copying traditions across different eras.
- **Distinguishing the author's voice from the copyist's:**
  - The Shaykh discussed the importance of distinguishing between the author's words and the copyist's interventions.
  - He stressed the need for precision in analyzing texts to avoid errors, such as taking the copying note to be the date of composition.
  - Illustrative examples were presented showing how textual context can be used to determine precisely the source of a statement.
- **Patterns of dating a copy:**
  - **Verbal dating:**
    - This pattern relies on explicit phrases such as "The completion of this book took place…" (*waqa'a al-faragh min hadha al-kitab…*).
    - The Shaykh explained the difficulties of understanding such phrases when they contain errors or ambiguity caused by haste in writing.
  - **Numerical dating:**
    - This consists of using numerals to indicate dates.
    - The Shaykh went into detail on the development of how numerals were written, pointing to the differences between older and modern forms, such as drawing the zero as an open circle.
  - **Dating by the abjad reckoning (hisab al-jummal):**
    - This pattern uses letters as symbols for numbers according to the *abjad hawwaz* order.
    - Analytical examples of this type were shown, with attention to its importance in dating manuscripts.
- **Where copying notes are located:**
  - These notes are usually placed at the close of texts, but they may also appear on title pages or within the parts of a volume.
  - The Shaykh stressed the importance of examining the whole manuscript so as not to overlook notes found in unexpected places.
  - Practical examples were reviewed showing how to find hidden copying data.
- **Challenges in cataloguing manuscripts:**
  - The Shaykh discussed common mistakes such as ignoring data found on pages other than the last ones.
  - He explained the importance of distinguishing between the copying date and the date of composition by understanding the context.
  - He offered examples showing how to avoid these mistakes through critical analysis.
- **Applied studies:**
  - The Shaykh presented examples of manuscripts containing different dates, analyzing how these dates should be read and interpreted.
  - He discussed common copyists' errors such as misreading of dots (tashif) and unjustified additions, and how to deal with them from a scholarly perspective.
- **Building critical skills:**
  - The Shaykh called for developing skills through continuous practice under the supervision of specialists.
  - He stressed that wide exposure to manuscripts strengthens the ability to analyze texts and to handle problems in a scholarly way.
- **Integrating texts and scholarly editing:**
  - The Shaykh discussed the importance of keeping historical contexts in mind when comparing copies.
  - He explained how copying notes are used as tools to verify the authenticity of texts and their attribution.

**Conclusion:**

- The Shaykh closed the lecture by pointing to the coming themes, which will address further patterns of copying dates and their analysis.
- He invited the attendees to prepare with more exercises and engagement with the examples presented.
- He ended with a prayer that this work be beneficial and blessed.
