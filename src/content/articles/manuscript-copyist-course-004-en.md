**Lesson title: Distinguishing Copying Notes from Authorial Notes in Arabic Manuscripts**

**Introduction:**

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened the lecture with a prayer and a warm welcome to the participants, stressing the importance of this lesson as the conclusion of a specialized course that aims to equip researchers with techniques for distinguishing between copying notes (quyud al-naskh) and authorial notes (quyud al-ta'lif) in Arabic manuscripts. The Shaykh emphasized that this topic is a cornerstone of textual editing (tahqiq), since accurately distinguishing between these notes helps avoid serious methodological errors that damage the credibility of scholarly work.

### Main Themes

### 1. Patterns of Dating in Manuscripts

The Shaykh addressed the common patterns of dating in Arabic manuscripts, highlighting their importance in determining the origins of texts:

- **Dating with letters:** relies on converting the letters of the alphabet into numerical values according to the abjad (jummal) system, a pattern common especially in old manuscripts.
- **Dating with words:** consists of stating the date in explicit verbal formulas, a less common but precise method.
- **Dating with numerals:** the most common and simplest pattern, used in most Islamic manuscripts.
- **The abjad reckoning and dating by fractions:** rarely used and requiring special mathematical knowledge. It is noted that dating by fractions began to appear with Ibn Kamal Pasha in later periods.

### 2. Challenges in Determining the Date of a Copy

The Shaykh noted that determining the date of a copy faces several challenges, most notably:

- **The transferred date:** sometimes the date recorded in the original exemplar is carried over without being updated in the present copy.
- **Missing copyist data:** some copyists neglected to add their personal details or the date of their copying.
- **Contradiction between material and script:** a contradiction may appear between the type of paper or script and the recorded date, which requires in-depth examination to determine how authentic the text is.

**Illustrative examples:**

- A copy of the book *Ma'rifat al-Majruhin* bore a date in the fourth century AH, but analysis showed that it actually belongs to the twelfth century, based on the type of paper and the script used.
- The Shaykh's experience helped him infer the date of a manuscript through having studied a large number of dated copies.

### 3. Distinguishing the Authorial Note from the Copying Note

The Shaykh highlighted the importance of distinguishing between authorial notes and copying notes:

- **The authorial note (qayd al-ta'lif):** the text the author himself records upon completing the writing. This note represents the author's own intellectual documentation.
- **The copying note (qayd al-naskh):** added by the copyist (nasikh) to indicate the details of the copying process, such as the date and his name.
- **The common confusion:** confusion arises when the copying note is mistakenly believed to be the authorial note, which changes the understanding of the copy entirely.

**Case study:**

- At the end of the book *al-Mahsul*, the author wrote a clear authorial note, but the copyist neglected to add his own note, which caused confusion about the copy.
- Resolving such a problem requires careful analysis of the texts and linking the notes to the historical and material context of the copy.

### 4. The Importance of Practical Experience in Analyzing Manuscripts

The Shaykh explained that practical experience is the most important factor in acquiring editing skills:

- **Continuous exposure:** reading a large number of manuscripts strengthens the ability to distinguish between scripts and different chronological patterns.
- **Studying dated copies:** this makes it possible to build a knowledge base that helps in inferring the date of undated copies.
- **Learning under the supervision of experts:** working directly with specialists is an invaluable opportunity to understand advanced tools and methods.

### 5. Methodical Steps for Dealing with Copying Notes

- **Distinguishing original notes from transferred ones:**
  - Original notes refer to the present copy, whereas transferred notes are taken from earlier copies.
- **Analyzing texts carefully:**
  - Examining the texts to make sure they are consistent with the material, the script and the date.
- **Comparing scripts:**
  - Studying the characteristics of the hands of well-known copyists to determine the source of the copy.
- **Consulting specialists:**
  - When facing complications, it is advisable to turn to those with experience.

### 6. Guidance for Researchers in Textual Editing

- **Patience:** one should be deliberate in issuing judgments to ensure conclusions supported by evidence.
- **Broadening general knowledge:** familiarity with jurisprudence (fiqh), language and history makes the process of verification easier.
- **Repeated training:** practical work is the key to developing the researcher's skills.

### Conclusion

Shaykh Salih concluded the lecture by stressing that studying manuscripts requires a careful and comprehensive review of earlier sources. He explained that the next course will deal with the critical analysis of manuscript copies in greater detail, with a focus on practical work.

### Recommendations

- **Perseverance:** maintaining continuity in the study of manuscripts.
- **Documenting observations:** recording every detail related to the copy during editing.
- **Learning from experience:** benefiting from the experiences of others strengthens the researcher's understanding and analytical ability.

### Closing Notes

The Shaykh stressed that distinguishing between copying notes and the authorial note is not merely a skill but an integrated discipline that requires a deep understanding of a range of auxiliary sciences. Success in this field depends on combining theoretical knowledge with continuous practical experience.
