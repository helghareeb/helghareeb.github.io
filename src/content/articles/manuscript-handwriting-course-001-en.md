### Lecture title: Analyzing Lacunae in Arabic Manuscripts - Causes, Types and a Methodical Approach

### Introduction

This lecture offers a methodical analysis of the concept of lacunae (naqs) in Arabic manuscripts, shedding light on the causes that lead to such gaps, whether material or textual, and the challenges that result from them. The lecture also explains the pivotal role of the editor (muhaqqiq) in restoring incomplete texts, with a focus on the tools and methods used in editing manuscripts. In his presentation, Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari relied on a variety of practical examples that highlight the importance of precision and scholarly integrity in this field.

### Main Elements

### 1. The Concept of Lacunae and Their Causes

### 1.1 Defining a Lacuna

A lacuna in a manuscript is the partial or total loss of elements of the written text, affecting its completeness and soundness. A lacuna may appear in various forms, such as missing volumes or leaves, or even missing sentences and words.

### 1.2 The Main Causes of Lacunae

- **External factors:**
  - Wars, natural disasters and neglect.
  - The effect of insects and humidity on paper and ink.
- **Internal factors:**
  - The author or copyist (nasikh) leaving gaps in the text to be filled in later.
  - Errors during copying or photographing.
- **Technical factors:**
  - Poor organization in numbering the leaves or quires.
  - Parts of the text being replaced or lost through lack of knowledge.

### 2. Patterns of Textual Lacunae

### 2.1 Missing Volumes

- These are detected by reviewing the texts of endowment notes (waqfiyyat) and audition certificates (sama'at).
- **Applied example:** a manuscript copy that, according to its endowment note, comprises 19 volumes, of which only 18 are available.

### 2.2 Missing Leaves

- These are noticed by analyzing the textual sequence of the leaves.
- The extent of the loss can be estimated by comparing the foliation with the quires.

### 2.3 Blank Spaces Within Texts

- **Intentional blanks:** leaving an empty space to be filled in later.
- **Unintentional blanks:** resulting from damage to the text or its illegibility.

### 3. The Role of Quires in Analyzing Lacunae

### 3.1 Defining the Quire

The quire (kurrasa) is a physical unit usually consisting of 8 to 10 connected leaves. The quire is the structural basis of manuscripts.

### 3.2 The Importance of Numbering Quires

- It makes it easier to trace and analyze texts.
- It helps detect lacunae quickly and accurately.
- It contributes to ordering the leaves and recovering lost texts.

### 3.3 Applied Examples

- If the second quire begins with leaf number 11, this indicates that the first quire contains leaves 1 to 10. Where this is contradicted, the lacuna can be identified.

### 4. Audition Certificates and Endowment Notes as Tools for Editing

### 4.1 The Audition Certificate

The audition certificate (sama') is the text that records the reading of the book or part of it. It is used to determine which parts were read and which are missing.

### 4.2 Endowment Notes

Endowment notes (waqfiyyat) are the texts that document the endowment (waqf) of a book. They often contain information about the number of volumes, which makes it easier to determine what is missing.

### 5. Analyzing Textual Blanks

### 5.1 Causes of Blanks

- **Deliberate omission:**
  - The author or copyist leaves a space to add content later.
- **Unintentional omission:**
  - Texts that are unclear or damaged.
  - The absence of the ink needed to fill the gaps.

### 5.2 Classifying Blanks

- **Blanks with a notice:** flagged with a phrase such as "blank in the original" (*bayad fi al-asl*).
- **Blanks without a notice:** the text is left empty with no warning, which makes the editing process more difficult.

### 6. How Copyists Handled Blanks

### 6.1 Filling the Blank

- Some copyists completed the texts on the basis of their personal knowledge or other sources.
- This practice may distort the original texts.

### 6.2 Flagging the Blank

- Copyists write notes in the margins, such as "blank in the original," as evidence of faithful transmission.

### 6.3 The Effect of These Practices on Editing

- Copyists' practices have a major effect on the accuracy of an edition. This requires editors to distinguish between original material and material added later.

### 7. Applied Examples

### 7.1 A Study of the Manuscript of *al-Hujja fi al-Qira'at*

- A lacuna in the copy was detected through the audition texts, which indicate the original number of parts.

### 7.2 A Study of the Manuscript of *Kashf al-Zunun*

- The analysis showed recurring blanks, with marginal notes confirming that the blanks were present in the original texts.

### 8. Editorial Challenges Resulting from Lacunae

### 8.1 Merging Unconnected Texts

- Failing to notice a lacuna leads to merging texts that are not connected, which changes the meaning.

### 8.2 Passing Over a Lacuna Without Documenting It

- Failing to flag a lacuna weakens the credibility of the edition and exposes texts to distortion.

### Advice for Editors

### 9.1 Editing Methodology

- **Careful analysis of the texts of endowment notes and audition certificates.**
- **Comparing the different copies** to discover the differences.
- **Recording notes carefully** about every lacuna or blank.

### 9.2 The Editor's Tools

- Using the foliation and the quires as a primary reference.
- Engaging with texts in their context to understand their original purposes.

### Conclusion

This lecture highlighted the importance of methodical editorial treatment of lacunae in handwritten texts. It also offered practical tools that help strengthen the accuracy of editorial work and ensure the faithfulness of texts. This approach is a vital step toward restoring handwritten texts to their original form.
