### Lesson Title

The Different Stages in the Development of Texts in Arabic Manuscripts: Drafts, Fair Copies and Recensions

### Elements of the Lesson

- **Introduction and opening prayer**
  - Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened the session by stressing the importance of sincerity in seeking knowledge, praying that these lessons would be a source of beneficial knowledge and righteous action.
  - He pointed to the importance of studying manuscripts as an integrated discipline that includes analyzing texts and understanding how they developed over time.
  - He cautioned that this discipline requires patience and readiness to face the challenges that confront researchers in it.
- **Aims of the lecture**
  - To clarify the differences between drafts and fair copies in manuscript texts.
  - To explain the concept of the different recensions (ibrazat) through which texts pass.
  - To analyze the methodology for dealing with texts across their various stages.
  - To strengthen critics' and researchers' understanding of the complexities involved in transmission and revision.
- **The concept of drafts and fair copies**
  - **The draft (musawwada):** the initial copy written by the author, marked by crossings-out, comments and marginal additions that reflect raw thinking.
  - **The fair copy (mubayyada):** the revised copy on which the author makes final improvements, including rearranging the texts and correcting grammatical and stylistic errors.
  - The Shaykh explained that some works remain in draft form because of circumstances such as the author's death or the work never being completed.
- **The importance of drafts and fair copies**
  - Drafts reveal the development of the author's ideas, which makes them a basic reference for understanding the stages through which texts evolved.
  - Fair copies represent the final copy prepared for circulation, but they may suffer from copying errors or unintended changes.
  - The Shaykh stressed that studying drafts enriches critical analysis and helps in understanding the intellectual paths the author followed.
- **The different recensions of books**
  - **The first recension (al-ibraz al-awwal):** the initial text, full of notes and additions, reflecting the author's first conception.
  - **The second recension:** includes the revisions and additions the author made after reviewing the first text.
  - **The third recension:** reflects an advanced stage of revision and restructuring, though it may include substantial changes.
  - The multiplicity of recensions reflects the dynamism of scholarly work and highlights the different stages in the development of the text.
- **The role of copyists in manuscripts**
  - The Shaykh pointed to the role of copyists (nussakh) as partners in transmitting texts, though their intervention may lead to errors or distortions (tahrif).
  - In some cases, copyists produce the fair copy on the author's behalf, which adds complexity to identifying the original copy.
  - Studying copying techniques and copyists' hands is necessary to understand the development of texts and the context in which they were written.
- **Problems in studying manuscript texts**
  - The multiplicity of copies and the differing stages of recension pose a major challenge for editors.
  - Texts that were never put into fair copy may contain precise information, but they require greater effort to resolve their obscurities.
  - The psychological and physical condition of copyists and its effect on the accuracy of copying is one of the factors affecting the quality of texts.
  - The Shaykh stressed the importance of studying the available copies and analyzing the differences between them to determine the text closest to the original.
- **Applied examples**
  - The Shaykh reviewed examples from manuscripts such as *Kashf al-Zunun* and the books of Ibn Hajar, explaining how to analyze drafts and fair copies.
  - Examples were presented that highlight the changes between the different copies and clarify the stages through which the texts passed.
  - The Shaykh explained that practical examples help researchers develop precise methodologies for studying texts.
- **The importance of ranking the copies**
  - Ranking the copies (tartib manazil al-nusakh) requires a deep understanding of the textual and chronological clues that reveal the sequence of copies.
  - Texts containing dated corrections or additions are a key to understanding the historical development of texts.
  - Ranking copies in a scholarly way helps present a final text that accurately reflects the author's vision.
- **Recommendations for researchers and editors**
  - Adopting a comprehensive methodology that includes comparing texts and analyzing internal and external clues.
  - Using digital technologies to document manuscript copies and analyze them methodically.
  - Taking into account the historical circumstances of the copyists and understanding their effect on the quality of texts.
  - Cooperation among editors and the exchange of experience to develop unified, scholarly methodologies.
- **Questions and inquiries**
  - The Shaykh discussed how to deal with texts that were never put into fair copy, explaining that the editor should regard them as the copy closest to the author's vision.
  - The questions addressed the differences between marginal notes (hawashi) and the original texts, and how to deal with additions.
  - The Shaykh explained how to classify copies on the basis of their contents and the clues found in them.

### Summary of the Lesson

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari presented an in-depth analysis of the stages in the development of manuscript texts, focusing on drafts, fair copies and the different recensions. He explained the importance of studying textual and historical clues in order to understand and edit texts accurately. He also stressed sincerity in scholarly work and the investment of time in understanding texts from a critical perspective.

The Shaykh emphasized that manuscript studies is not merely a theoretical discipline; it requires analytical and practical skills to understand the complexities surrounding texts and their history. He pointed out that progress in this field calls for careful study of all available copies, taking into account the historical contexts of the copyists and the texts.

The Shaykh concluded by calling for the adoption of a rigorous scholarly methodology in editing texts, stressing the need to prepare a generation of distinguished researchers capable of preserving the Islamic heritage and editing texts to a high standard.
