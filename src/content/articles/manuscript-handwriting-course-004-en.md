## Introduction

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened this lecture by setting out an in-depth vision of textual editing (tahqiq), stressing that editing is not merely collecting texts or superficial revision but a specialized scholarly practice that aims to present texts in the form closest to what the author left. The Shaykh explained the importance of studying manuscript copies from a methodical critical perspective concerned with analyzing how texts developed across their different stages.

## Main Elements

### 1. The Aim of Editing

- Restoring the text to the state closest to what the author intended in its final stage, taking into account all the factors that may affect that text.
- Editing is the link between authors and contemporary researchers, which makes it a great responsibility demanding precision and scholarly integrity.
- Distinguishing between the factual reality of what the author wrote and what textual editors suppose to be correct: what the author wrote is preserved regardless of how far it matches reality.

### 2. The Different Stages of Manuscript Texts

- **The draft (musawwada):** the initial stage, containing repeated revisions, which makes it an important source for understanding the author's thought process.
- **The fair copy (mubayyada):** the copy that has been reworked in a more organized and methodical way, and is often closer to the final text.
- **Recensions (ibrazat):** the multiple versions the author may have issued at different stages, whether because of his revisions or changes in his views.
- **Intermediate copies:** those that represent transcriptions of texts from the drafts or fair copies, which are liable to additions or corrections by copyists (nussakh).

### 3. The Editor's Tasks

- Presenting the original text as the author left it, while documenting any differences or revisions that appeared across the multiple copies.
- Using the tools of critical research to analyze the manuscript copies and determine the copy closest to the original text.
- Refraining from "correcting" texts except in exceptional cases, such as clear errors in Qur'anic texts or Prophetic hadiths, while indicating this in the footnotes.
- Dealing cautiously with copies that contain notes or corrections by scholars or copyists.

### 4. The Editor's Position on Textual Differences

- The editor is not charged with correcting the author's errors but with presenting accurately what he wrote.
- Relying on the footnotes to clarify possible errors or additions without affecting the main text.
- Understanding the scholarly and cultural context of the text is necessary for interpreting the differences that may appear in the various copies.

### 5. Ranking Manuscript Copies

- **Main considerations:**
  - Giving precedence to copies that contain indications that the author relied on them, or that he wrote in his own hand.
  - Comparing the manuscript texts to determine the most accurate copy, the one representing the author's final stage.
  - Dealing with newer and older copies on the basis of their quality and source, not merely their age.
- **Refuting conventional criteria:**
  - Shaykh al-Azhari criticized the rules that consider complete copies always better than incomplete ones, pointing out that accuracy matters more than formal completeness.
  - He stressed the importance of moving beyond rigid templates that may lead to inaccurate results.
- **Advanced concepts:**
  - Ranking the copies requires a critical methodology that takes account of their differing sources, such as copies written by scholars, copies containing additions or corrections, and intermediate copies between the author and the reader.

### 6. Practical Examples and Lessons Learned

- The Shaykh gave applied examples of authors' errors that appeared in their manuscript copies, such as mistakes in transmitting names or information.
- He reviewed cases that showed the influence of copyists on texts, which confirmed the importance of returning to the copy closest to the author's own hand.
- He touched on the challenges of dealing with copies written by scholars or skilled copyists, noting that beautiful handwriting does not necessarily mean an accurate text.

### 7. Lessons Learned from the Editing Process

- Editing is not merely a technical process; it requires critical skills and a deep understanding of manuscript copies and their historical contexts.
- The study of manuscript copies should be comprehensive, covering the title, the attribution to the author, the copying date and possible additions.
- The editor's critical faculty (malaka) is developed through intensive practice and continuous exposure to texts.

## Conclusion

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari concluded the lecture by stressing that commitment to scholarly integrity is the foundation on which any successful editing project is built. He emphasized the importance of developing editors' critical and intellectual tools through continuous training on real texts, and he called for holding applied workshops that present examples of manuscript copies so that the theories can be applied in practice.
