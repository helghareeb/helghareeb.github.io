### Lesson Title: Course on Mastering Manuscript Studies

### Introduction

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened the lecture by explaining the importance of manuscript studies (*'ilm al-makhtutat*) as an essential tributary for studying the Islamic heritage and analyzing its components. The Shaykh stressed the pressing need to move students from a state of passive learning to one of critical engagement and methodical analysis. He pointed out that the present course is part of an integrated series that aims to enable participants to acquire the scholarly skills needed to deal with manuscripts (*makhtutat*) professionally and with insight.

### The Main Tracks of the Course

### Track One: Critical Reading and Self-Review

- Motivating students to read the foundational works and references in manuscript studies independently.
- Applying analytical methods to understand texts and to identify points that are obscure or controversial.
- Encouraging the formulation of critical, methodical questions to raise in group discussions.
- Developing the ability to evaluate written texts by precise scholarly standards.

### Track Two: Analyzing Manuscripts

- Training students to study manuscript texts in detail and to draw out the distinctive phenomena related to orthography (*rasm*) and vocalization (*dabt*).
- Analyzing the accompanying texts, such as ownership notes (*tamalluk*) and endowment notes (*waqfiyyat*), with a focus on their role in building the identity of the manuscript.
- Grasping the methods of the copyists (*nussakh*, sing. *nasikh*) and understanding their effect on the soundness of texts.

### Track Three: Evaluating Editions and Printed Texts

- Applying the foundations of scholarly criticism to the various editions of manuscripts.
- Developing the skill of comparing editions in order to discover errors and offer methodical solutions.
- Promoting balanced criticism that aims at improvement without excess or deficiency.

### General Aims of the Course

- Enabling students to reach the stage of scholarly independence in reading and analyzing texts.
- Strengthening the faculty of scholarly criticism through practical exercises and constructive discussions.
- Establishing a learning environment that stimulates academic innovation and the exchange of experience.
- Preparing a generation of seasoned researchers capable of making sound scholarly contributions.

### A Critical Review of the Books Written on Manuscript Studies

Shaykh Salih explained the challenges facing the current works on manuscript studies, highlighting the following problems:

- **Absence of methodical sequence:** many books lack a logical organization of ideas, which leaves the reader scattered.
- **Informational gaps:** some works do not cover their subjects comprehensively.
- **Tedious repetition:** the inclusion of repeated ideas without offering new insights.
- **Absence of practical application:** reliance on abstract theorizing without practical examples to support the theoretical points.
- **Methodological errors:** inaccurate conclusions that negatively affect the credibility of the books.

The Shaykh pointed to the need to reconsider these works by offering a critical evaluation that enhances their scholarly value.

### The Main Topics of the Course's Core Book

The book *Nahw 'Ilm Makhtutat 'Arabi* (Toward an Arabic Codicology) by Dr. Abd al-Sattar al-Hilwaji was chosen as a model for critical study. The book covers the following topics:

- **History of the manuscript:**
  - Studying the development of the Arabic manuscript across the ages, from its beginnings to the age of printing.
  - Analyzing the reports related to the earliest manuscripts, such as the *Mu'allaqat* and the Noble Mushaf.
  - Discussing the stages of flourishing and decline through which the manufacture of manuscripts passed.
- **Making of the manuscript:**
  - Reviewing the raw materials used in writing manuscripts, such as parchment (*raqq*), paper (*waraq*), and papyrus.
  - Studying writing tools and binding methods, with an analysis of their effect on the durability of texts.
  - Researching the ornaments used and their importance in bringing out the aesthetic value of the manuscript.
- **Documentation and evaluation:**
  - Analyzing accompanying texts such as audition certificates (*sama'at*, sing. *sama'*) and ownership notes, and their role in documenting the manuscript.
  - Presenting scholarly methods for employing these texts in editing the original texts.
  - Offering practical examples to show how accompanying texts can be put to use in documentation.
- **Cataloguing:**
  - Clarifying the basic principles of scholarly cataloguing.
  - Presenting sample cataloguing forms and analyzing their strengths and weaknesses.
  - Reviewing the relationship between cataloguing and bibliographic control.
- **Editing and publication:**
  - Highlighting the principles of the scholarly critical editing (*tahqiq*) of texts.
  - Addressing the problems of publication, with solutions offered to improve the quality of editions.
  - A critical study of publishing processes with the aim of raising the level of scholarly output.

### Course Assignments

- Reading the book *Nahw 'Ilm Makhtutat 'Arabi* carefully and recording notes while reading.
- Analyzing the issues raised in the book and putting them forward for discussion in the coming sessions.
- Participating actively in the discussions to deepen understanding and exchange experience.

### Concluding Guidance from the Shaykh

- The Shaykh stressed the importance of gradual progression in learning, with a focus on deep comprehension of concepts.
- He called on students to engage in continuous practical work to achieve the desired benefit.
- He affirmed that success in this field requires commitment and patience.
- He urged students to engage seriously with the course content and to make use of all available resources.

### Close of the Lesson

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari concluded the lecture by affirming that this course represents the cornerstone in preparing researchers specialized in manuscript studies. He called on the students to persevere in learning and research, stressing that this long path requires patience and continuous work to achieve the hoped-for contributions in the service of the Islamic heritage.
