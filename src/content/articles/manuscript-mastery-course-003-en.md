## Introduction

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened this lecture with a greeting of peace, followed by praise and glorification of Allah the Exalted and blessings upon the Prophet Muhammad, framing the session as an advanced stage of learning within the training series devoted to understanding manuscript studies. The Shaykh clearly defined the lecture's aims: to strengthen the faculty of deep analysis of manuscript texts and to equip participants with precise methodological tools for understanding those texts in terms of context and content. This is the third lecture in a program that extends toward a comprehensive methodological grounding in this field.

## Main Topics

### 1. The Importance of Methodical Reading and Analytical Engagement with Manuscripts

- The Shaykh stressed that deep reading of manuscript texts is not merely a process of surveying content, but a means of building a critical understanding of the writing methods of earlier authors.
- He affirmed the need to set aside regular daily time for reading, even for short periods, explaining that keeping such a habit builds up knowledge methodically.
- He explained that the texts chosen for study are among the most prominent sources in this field and are valued as basic references for studying methods of recording and the Islamic heritage.
- He pointed out that methodical reading gives the researcher the ability to handle old texts flexibly and precisely, enabling him to benefit from them to the fullest.

### 2. Reconsidering the Definition of Manuscripts and the Limits of the Concept

- The Shaykh discussed the definition of manuscripts (*makhtutat*), defining a manuscript as "a book written by hand before the age of printing," but he pointed to the problems that may arise from adopting this literal definition.
- He addressed the age of printing as a temporal boundary, explaining that this boundary differs from one culture to another, though it is usually linked to the beginning of the nineteenth century CE.
- He affirmed that the scholarly value of a text is tied less to the time it was written than to its content, pointing to texts written after the age of printing that carry distinctive research value.
- He stressed the importance of distinguishing between handwritten and printed texts in order to understand how patterns of documentation and editing developed over time.

### 3. Methodological Applications of the Definition of Manuscripts in the Context of Editing

- The Shaykh highlighted the limitations of relying on rigid definitions, pointing to the need to deal flexibly with texts in their various contexts.
- He affirmed that definitions that are "comprehensive and exclusive" (*jami' mani'*) may not always be practical, explaining the importance of adopting living examples as a basis for understanding texts.
- The Shaykh addressed examples showing how texts can go beyond the bounds of traditional definitions, which strengthens their capacity to offer new intellectual insights.
- He pointed out that scholarly critical editing (*tahqiq*) requires awareness of the temporal and social contexts of texts, combining theoretical knowledge with analytical skills.

### 4. Collation and Comparison Between Copies: Basic Tools of Editing

- The Shaykh reviewed the concept of collation (*muqabala*) between copies (*nusakh*, sing. *nuskha*), pointing to its importance as a vital tool in editing texts and ensuring their accuracy.
- He explained the difference between the processes of presentation (*'ard*), audition (*sama'*), and collation, affirming that each of them has its role in improving the quality of the edited text.
- He discussed the symbols used in texts (such as circles and dots) as means of clarifying how texts passed between copies, highlighting their role in analyzing the stages of recording.
- He affirmed that collation is not a mere formality, but represents a deep scholarly effort aimed at uncovering the differences between copies and correcting texts on that basis.

### 5. The Importance of Critical and Contextual Analysis of Texts

- The Shaykh stressed the importance of critical analysis for understanding manuscript texts, explaining that many errors arise from superficial reading.
- He pointed to the need to grasp the intellectual and temporal contexts in which texts were produced, and to distinguish between copyists' errors and editorial modifications.
- He offered practical examples showing how to derive the correct meanings from texts by analyzing context and textual marks.
- He affirmed that criticism does not mean excessive doubt, but is a means of reaching a deeper and more coherent understanding.

### 6. Theoretical and Practical Issues Related to the Rules of Editing

- The Shaykh addressed the theoretical and practical rules of editing, pointing to the need to deal with texts with precise awareness and knowledge of the tools of editing.
- He affirmed the importance of using the margins to record corrections and observations in a way that reflects precise scholarly method.
- He explained the importance of handling corrections with caution, distinguishing between corrections made by the copyists and others that reflect later editing.
- He stressed that the rules of editing require continual adaptation to the nature of different texts.

### 7. Recommended Texts and Future Projects

- The Shaykh announced that the next book set for study is *Manahij al-'Ulama' fi Kitabat al-Makhtutat* (The Methods of Scholars in Writing Manuscripts), pointing to its value as a comprehensive reference in this field.
- He explained that this book is distinguished by its scholarly style and precise methodology, which makes it a rich source for specialists.
- He affirmed that the book presents a set of research issues that form a foundation for a deeper understanding of manuscripts.
- The Shaykh called on the trainees to invest their time in reading this book carefully in order to gain abundant scholarly benefit.

## Conclusion

The Shaykh concluded the lecture by stressing the need to commit to reading the set book and reviewing it carefully, noting that the coming discussions would address the issues it raises in depth. He also stressed the importance of upholding scholarly integrity in handling the texts sent to participants and of ensuring that they are not published except with the permission of their authors. The Shaykh ended by reminding the participants that developing their research skills depends on their serious engagement in analyzing texts.

## Recommendations

- Commit to a daily schedule of regular reading.
- Develop critical ability in dealing with texts.
- Make use of the recommended texts to strengthen understanding of editing methods.
- Observe scholarly integrity in dealing with texts.
- Apply the methodological rules of editing with a focus on context and content.
