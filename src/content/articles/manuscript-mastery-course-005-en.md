**Lesson Title:** The Methods of Scholars and Copyists in Writing and Establishing Arabic Manuscripts

**Lecture Summary:**

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened the lecture with the greeting of Islam, affirming the importance of this meeting as a vital part of a lecture series aimed at deepening specialized researchers' critical understanding of manuscript studies. The lecture reviewed in depth the methods adopted by scholars and copyists (*nussakh*, sing. *nasikh*) in writing manuscripts (*makhtutat*) and establishing their texts (*dabt*), focusing on the organic relationship between theoretical analysis and the practical application of texts.

### Main Elements of the Lecture

- **Aims of the lecture and the course:**
  - Establishing skills of critical analysis and intellectual independence among the participants, moving beyond the traditional models of rote instruction.
  - Developing a methodological vision that allows researchers to analyze manuscript texts on solid scholarly foundations.
  - Encouraging the best use of modern scholarly research tools, while taking into account the historical and cultural dimensions of the texts.
- **Analysis of Dr. al-Hilwaji's book:**
  - The lecture addressed critical remarks on the book *Nahw 'Ilm Makhtutat 'Arabi* (Toward an Arabic Codicology), highlighting the importance of distinguishing between original texts and digital images.
  - It discussed the role of technical developments in reshaping the ways manuscripts are studied and in making more precise results easier to reach.
- **Digital images as an advanced research tool:**
  - High-quality digital images stand out as an important analytical tool, allowing texts to be magnified and fine details to be made clear.
  - They play an essential role in protecting the original copies of manuscripts from damage, which makes them a pivotal tool in research.
- **Challenges related to the study of manuscripts:**
  - The importance of understanding the physical structure of manuscripts, including the order of the quires (*karrasat*) and the number of leaves, to ensure the integrity of the texts.
  - Addressing the limitations that digital images may impose, especially in cases that require inspecting the original copies to assess their physical condition.
- **The problem of forgery in manuscripts:**
  - It was stressed that cases of forgery are rare, with a call to avoid the exaggeration that may harm the objectivity of research.
  - The Shaykh offered a rational approach to analyzing this phenomenon based on the methods of scholarly criticism.
- **Ideal cataloguing:**
  - The Shaykh stressed that cataloguing must cover all textual and physical phenomena, taking care to document every detail.
  - He affirmed the importance of the scholarly qualification of cataloguers, pointing to the dangers that result from a lack of competence.
- **Classifying manuscript copies:**
  - He explained the method of classifying copies (*nusakh*, sing. *nuskha*) into groups on the basis of the textual "mother" (*umm*, the archetype), which contributes to a deeper understanding of textual relationships.
  - He discussed how variants between copies can be used to determine their textual standing (*manazil*) and to order the relationships among them.
- **Dealing with textual errors:**
  - Textual errors are not necessarily an indication of negligence or forgery; they may represent part of the natural development of texts.
  - The Shaykh explained how errors can be put to use in understanding historical contexts and textual development.
- **Marginal notes and margins as a source of knowledge:**
  - Marginal notes (*hawashi*) are a basic element in documenting texts, as they help in comparing copies and clarifying contexts.
  - The Shaykh called for documenting marginal notes precisely and linking them to their original sources in order to analyze them better.
- **Critical reading of the recommended book:**
  - He encouraged the participants to engage critically with the texts and samples contained in the book.
  - He suggested summarizing the book by hand to deepen understanding and consolidate concepts.
- **The importance of knowledge in preserving the heritage:**
  - The lecture closed by affirming the role of knowledge in safeguarding the cultural and religious heritage.
  - The Shaykh affirmed that a scholarly legacy is a means of preserving a person's remembrance through time, stressing the importance of documentation and authorship.

### Lecture Assignments

- Reading the book carefully, with a focus on the texts and samples.
- Preparing a handwritten analytical summary that includes the main points and critical questions.
- Preparing for the next discussion by reviewing the texts and drawing critical conclusions.

**Comment:** Shaykh Salih focused on developing skills of critical engagement with manuscript texts, pointing to the importance of methodical analysis of contexts and concepts. He affirmed that the next discussion would be devoted to reviewing the findings the participants had reached, which helps consolidate understanding and strengthen research abilities.
