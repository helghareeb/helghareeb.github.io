### Analyzing Naskh Script and Its Importance in Editing Arabic Manuscripts

### Introduction

Analyzing and reading Arabic manuscripts (*makhtutat*) is a lofty field of knowledge that requires precise skills and broad familiarity with linguistic and historical methods. Texts written in old naskh scripts are a precious source for understanding the cultural and intellectual heritage of the Islamic Ummah. This academic work highlights the importance of the interaction between the analytical method and historical contexts in achieving a precise and comprehensive reading of heritage texts, which helps uncover the intellectual and social values that shaped their contents.

### Main Elements

- **Methods of reading manuscripts:**
  - Focusing on distinguishing between the original texts and later additions by copyists (*nussakh*, sing. *nasikh*).
  - Employing the linguistic and historical context to understand ambiguous expressions.
  - Using advanced technical tools to analyze texts.
- **Types of symbols in manuscripts:**
  - **General symbols:** shared among copyists, such as marks indicating corrections.
  - **Technical symbols:** specific to particular fields, such as the hadith sciences and jurisprudence (*fiqh*).
  - **Individual symbols:** used by the scribe or author in a personal way.
  - **Rational-science symbols:** these appear in the philosophical and rational disciplines and require specialized interpretation.
- **Context and its importance in interpreting texts:**
  - Relying on the linguistic context to understand words that are entangled with one another.
  - Studying parallel texts to understand the ideas put forward.
  - Analyzing the structures of texts to avoid errors.
- **Analyzing manuscript copies:**
  - Determining the chronological characteristics of texts.
  - Comparing different copies (*nusakh*, sing. *nuskha*) to uncover textual variants.
  - Analyzing the structure of texts to understand the development of ideas.
- **Skills of the editor:**
  - Command of the linguistic sciences and the heritage disciplines.
  - The capacity for long-term practice in analyzing scripts (*khutut*, sing. *khatt*) and symbols.
  - Collaborating with specialists when facing complex texts.
- **Using technology in editing:**
  - Digital imaging to analyze damaged texts.
  - Databases to examine different copies.
  - Artificial intelligence programs to decipher symbols.

### Practical Examples

- **Understanding the words "انتفاء" (*intifa'*, "absence") and "استيفاء" (*istifa'*, "fulfillment"):**
  - The example shows the importance of precision in analyzing similar-looking words.
  - Using different copies to determine the correct reading.
  - Focusing on the ambiguous letters and the symbols used in the copies.
- **Classifying and interpreting symbols:**
  - Such as the use of "ص" (*sad*) to refer to Ibn al-Salah and "ر" (*ra'*) for al-Shirazi.
  - Interpreting symbols by means of the related texts.
- **The influence of non-Arab ('*ajami*) scripts:**
  - Showing the influence of the scripts used in Persia on the texts.
  - Analyzing methods of abbreviation to understand what the texts signify.

### Conclusions

- Editing manuscripts requires advanced linguistic and analytical skills.
- The symbols in texts are decisive tools for understanding heritage texts.
- Using context and comparing copies are a foundation for editing texts.
- Modern technologies offer enormous possibilities for speeding up the process and improving its precision.
- Understanding the author's history and intellectual context enhances the precision of analysis.

### Recommendations

- Providing specialized training courses in manuscript studies.
- Intensifying practical training in reading and analyzing texts.
- Strengthening cooperation among researchers to exchange experience.
- Using technology to improve the efficiency of editing.
- Publishing the results of editing so that they are available to researchers and interested readers.

This text represents an academic model to support researchers in the field of manuscript editing. Combining traditional tools with modern technologies opens new horizons for heritage studies and deepens the understanding of texts, which contributes to preserving the cultural heritage and developing the humanities.
