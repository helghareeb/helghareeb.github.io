## Introduction

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari began the lecture by affirming the importance of the lessons in manuscript studies, pointing out that absorbing them strengthens the researcher's critical ability and enables him to deal with manuscript texts with a high degree of professionalism, while understanding their linguistic and historical dimensions within their contexts.

## Main Elements

### 1. Copying Methods and the Difference Between Terms

### Distinguishing Between Rubbing Out, Erasing, and Scraping

An analysis of the fine differences between these terms:

- **Scraping (*kasht*):** using sharp tools such as knives to remove the ink clearly.
- **Erasing (*mahw*):** removing the text by less harsh means, such as a damp piece of cloth.
- **Rubbing out (*hakk*):** used to make fine adjustments without greatly affecting the integrity of the text.

### Historical Applications

A review of applied examples from old manuscripts to explain the effect of these techniques on the quality of the text and the ease of reading it, highlighting the role of the copyist's (*nasikh*) skill.

### 2. Establishing Texts and Distinguishing Copies

### Dealing with Symbols and Their Meanings

- Symbols such as "ظ" (*za'*, standing for *al-zahir kadha*, "apparently it is thus") and "خ" (*kha'*, indicating a variant between copies) represent a challenge that must be handled precisely.
- Misunderstanding the symbols leads to a mistaken reading and to the distortion of texts.

### Editors' Errors

- The Shaykh presented examples of mistaken interpretations, stressing the importance of critical depth in reading and understanding the symbols.

### Colors as a Tool of Textual Control

- The use of colors in manuscripts is a means of distinguishing texts and key words, which enhances the clarity of meanings.

### 3. Applied Problems in Editing Manuscripts

### A Practical Model

An analysis of a copy (*nuskha*) of the book *al-Kamil* by al-Mubarrad, which was established (*dabt*) using symbols and colors, reflecting the precision and creativity of the copyists.

### The Importance of Precision in Copying

- Highlighting common errors, such as dropping letters or substituting words, and their effect on meaning.

### 4. The Difference Between Variants of Copies and Variants of Transmissions

### Analyzing the Differences in Meaning

- Variants between copies (*ikhtilaf al-nusakh*) often result from the copyists' own judgment, whereas variants between transmissions (*ikhtilaf al-riwayat*) arise from the sources of the texts themselves.

### The Science of Chains of Transmission and Manuscript Studies

- Linking the critical method of the science of chains of transmission (*'ilm al-asanid*, sing. *isnad*) with the methods of editing manuscript texts.

### 5. Tools for Editing Texts

### Documentation as a Necessity

- Documenting symbols precisely is indispensable to ensure that texts are not misinterpreted.

### Balance Between Theory and Practice

- The importance of combining theoretical study with fieldwork to arrive at comprehensive and precise editing.

### 6. Future Recommendations

### Strengthening Practical Skills

- A call to launch training programs that focus on developing editors' practical skills.

### Rare Texts as a Priority

- Focusing on editing rare texts that have not received their share of academic study.

## Conclusion

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari concluded the lecture with a recommendation to reread the book, stressing that editing manuscripts requires patience, deep reflection, and continuous practice. He affirmed that this science is not merely an academic activity, but a responsibility that aims to preserve the cultural heritage and transmit it accurately to coming generations.
