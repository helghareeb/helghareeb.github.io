### The Scholarly Methodology for Analyzing the Manuscripts of Ibn al-Subki: Tools of Verification and Advanced Documentation

### Introduction

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened his lecture with praise and glorification of Allah and blessings upon the Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), paving the way to speak about the depth of knowledge that the science of editing manuscripts requires. The Shaykh affirmed that correct reading is not a passing skill but an essential pillar on which all stages of academic editing (*tahqiq*) rest. He also pointed out that neglecting this skill has led to major deviations in the results of editing, which has weakened the reliability of edited texts.

### Pivotal Elements

### 1. The Strategic Importance of Precise Reading

- Correct reading of manuscripts is the foundation on which the understanding of texts, and the achievement of their purposes, is built.
- An error in reading leads to the distortion of texts and the misinterpretation of meanings, which negatively affects their scholarly value.
- The Shaykh addressed the challenges facing specialists in this field, pointing to shortcomings in training programs.

### 2. Analyzing the Paratexts

- The paratexts (*khawarij al-nusus*, the material outside the main text) include: marginal notes (*hawashi*), endowment notes (*waqfiyyat*), ownership notes (*tamallukat*, sing. *tamalluk*), audition certificates (*sama'at*, sing. *sama'*), and identifying data.
- These elements play a decisive role in understanding the historical context of texts.
- The Shaykh affirmed the need to analyze these components carefully in order to distinguish the original among them from what was added, and to adopt them as supporting evidence for the texts.

### 3. The Difference Between the Author's Colophon and the Copyist's Colophon

- The author's colophon (*qayd al-mu'allif*): refers to the closing statements that the author places at the end of his book.
- The copyist's colophon (*qayd al-nasikh*): reflects the information the copyist writes about his copying of the text.
- The Shaykh explained the danger of confusing the two, as this may lead to texts being wrongly attributed.

### 4. Methods of Verifying the Authenticity of a Copy

- Comparing the script (*khatt*) of the copy (*nuskha*) with reliable samples of the author's handwriting is a main tool for confirming the correctness of the attribution.
- Reviewing endowment and ownership notes as means that support the authenticity of texts.
- The Shaykh reviewed applied examples of analyzing suspect texts.

### 5. Rhymed Prose and Punctuation as Two Literary Elements

- Rhymed prose (*saj'*) reflects the author's style and personality, and plays a pivotal role in establishing edited texts.
- The Shaykh stressed the importance of respecting the rhymed structure of texts, to prevent them from deviating from the original.
- He addressed the challenges editors face in this context and offered practical examples.

### 6. Studying the Notes of Manuscripts

- *Hard al-matn* (the closing formula of the text), as a model of the closing notes, reflects the importance of in-depth analysis of manuscript texts.
- The Shaykh explained how to deal with the various notes (*quyud*, sing. *qayd*) and the importance of determining their source precisely.

### 7. Analyzing Scripts as a Means of Documentation

- Comparing scripts across multiple samples is among the most important means of strengthening the credibility of a copy.
- The Shaykh addressed the distinctive features of scripts and how to employ them as supporting evidence.

### 8. The Role of Audition Certificates in Strengthening Documentation

- Audition certificates are a pivotal tool in establishing the soundness of texts and their attribution.
- The Shaykh explained how missed portions (*fawat*) are dealt with through licenses (*ijazat*, sing. *ijaza*) or accompanying comments.
- He pointed to the use of audition certificates as a central element supporting the credibility of the copy.

### Conclusion

The Shaykh affirmed the importance of adopting organized and precise methods in studying manuscript copies, with a focus on the details that bring out the historical context and the accompanying texts. He called for practicing editing as a science that requires patience, precision, and long-term practice, pointing to the importance of methodical work in building reliable scholarly editions.

### Recommendations

- Focusing on reading the accompanying texts carefully and analyzing them with advanced tools.
- Scrutinizing the notes and distinguishing between the author's colophon and the copyist's colophon.
- Using multi-sample comparison to document scripts and strengthen reliability.
- Intensifying practical training in reading difficult manuscripts to develop skills.
- Regarding audition certificates as an integral part of the process of documenting texts in a scholarly way.
