### Lesson Title: Analyzing Manuscript Copies and Naming Books According to the Method of Shaykh Salih al-Azhari

#### The Tenth Lecture: Discussing Manuscript Copies and the Naming of the Book

**Introduction:**

The lecture opened with an analytical overview of the methodological errors that researchers and editors fall into when studying manuscript copies (al-nusakh al-khattiyya). Shaykh Salih al-Azhari stressed the importance of distinguishing between the words of the author and the words of the copyist (nasikh), pointing out that a deep understanding of the contexts of texts and hands (khutut) requires precise skills and long practical experience.

### Core Elements of the Lecture

### 1. The Closing Note and Its Importance in Editing Manuscript Copies

- **Identifying the primary source of the text:** the main challenge in studying manuscript copies lies in distinguishing between notes (quyud) recorded by the author and those added by the copyist. Copyists often transmit such notes without indicating their source, which causes problems in dating texts and determining their origin.
- **Methodical verification of notes:** this includes comparing the recorded notes with other reliable texts, while taking into account the possibility that some copies were transcribed long after the author's death.
- **Indicators used to determine the source:**
  - Comparing the hands with the author's known hand.
  - Relying on the texts of audition certificates (sama'at) and attestations.
  - Analyzing the accompanying texts to verify that they agree with the general context of the text.

### 2. Corrections and Their Role in Revealing the Original Source

- **The analytical character of corrections:** the corrections recorded in texts are a mirror of the thoughts and revisions the author made while writing the text. If a copy contains substantive changes, such as rewording or the addition of new information, this is taken as an indicator of its direct connection to the author.
- **Applied examples:** examples were shown from texts of Ibn al-Subki, where the corrections showed how they were used to improve or modify the texts.
- **Inductive study of corrections:** the study of corrections is not limited to establishing that they belong to the author; it also includes understanding the intellectual and technical dimension behind these changes.

### 3. The Problem of Naming Books

- **Precisely determining the title:** the correct title of a book is the one the author himself used. When this element is absent, one can rely on other places such as the title page, the introduction, or the accompanying notes.
- **Distinguishing between description and naming:** a description may reflect the content of the book, but it does not count as a formal name. For example, describing a book as “a fine abridgment on the history of the jurists” clarifies its subject but is not considered a real title.
- **Multiple names and how to deal with them:**
  - Giving priority to the oldest and most widely circulated names.
  - Analyzing the historical contexts to determine the most appropriate one.

### 4. The Methodological Framework for Editing Manuscript Copies

- **Analyzing the copy:**
  - Verifying the date of the copy and comparing it with other copies.
  - Studying the practices of copyists inductively to determine their degree of accuracy.
- **Analyzing the accompanying texts:**
  - Studying corrections to understand their role in the text.
  - Distinguishing endowment notes (waqfiyyat) and ownership notes (tamallukat) as part of the accompanying texts that help establish the source.
- **Using textual indicators:**
  - Establishing the differences between copies through textual analysis.
  - Studying corrected words and the extent of their effect on the text.

### 5. Case Study: Ibn al-Subki's Copy

- **Textual analysis of the copy:** a copy in the hand of Ibn al-Subki was analyzed and carefully compared with other copies, to show how methodical techniques are used to verify the soundness of texts.
- **Establishing the title through indicators:** multiple indicators, such as audition certificates and accompanying texts, were used to determine the correct title. The Shaykh warned against relying on copyists' names for a book without strong supporting indicators.

### 6. Recommended Practical Training for Students

- **Preparing a comparison table:**
  - Selecting specific words from the text.
  - Analyzing the differences between the author's copy and the other copies.
  - Exploring how copyists and editors acted in dealing with the texts.
- **Analyzing the differences between copies:**
  - Studying words marked with vowel signs (dabt) and assessing how accurately they were handled.
  - Evaluating the marginal insertions (ilhaqat) and the extent of their effect on the texts.

### 7. Conclusion and Findings

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari stressed that editing texts is a precise discipline that requires a critical methodology and great patience. He also stressed the importance of the editor possessing advanced analytical skills in order to understand the nature of texts and study the differences between them in depth. He called for benefiting from contemporary methodological tools while remaining committed to the authentic traditional methodology.

### Recommendations

- Reading *Ma'rifat Khutut al-A'lam* (Recognizing the Hands of Notable Scholars) by Dr. Muhammad al-Suray', because of the important applied rules it contains.
- Applying the methods explained to a variety of manuscript copies in order to develop one's skills.
- Focusing on analyzing differences between copies in order to understand the methods of copyists.

### Assignment

- Preparing a comparison table covering words and corrections, as explained.
- Studying the book *Anmat al-Tawthiq fi al-Makhtut al-'Arabi* (Patterns of Documentation in the Arabic Manuscript) up to the third chapter, analyzing the rules it presents.
- Delving into the study of textual corrections and marginal insertions to assess their accuracy and effect.

**In closing:**

This lecture represents an advanced academic reference in the discipline of manuscript editing. Shaykh Salih al-Azhari highlighted the importance of practical training in studying and analyzing manuscript copies. He stressed that success in this field depends on a combination of advanced theoretical and applied skills, affirming that preserving the heritage and editing texts with scholarly precision is a responsibility that demands deep awareness and a high level of professionalism.
