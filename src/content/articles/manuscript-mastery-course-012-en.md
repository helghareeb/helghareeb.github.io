**Lesson title:** Advanced Methodological Rules in Editing Manuscript Texts

**Introduction to the lesson:** In this lesson, Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari dealt with advanced and specialized topics in the field of editing manuscript texts (tahqiq al-nusus al-khattiyya), focusing on the methodological rules that enable researchers to deal with old texts with advanced scholarly awareness. He also dealt with the contemporary challenges facing researchers in this field, including historical changes in the names of libraries, the influence of copyists' dialects, and differences in styles of writing.

**Main Elements of the Lesson:**

### First: Investing Time in Scholarly Research

- The Shaykh stressed that devoting oneself completely to knowledge is rare, pointing out the need to balance life's occupations with scholarly work.
- He stressed the importance of organizing time and investing it in scholarly activities, explaining that a few minutes used effectively can lead to great achievements.
- He discussed the importance of avoiding daily distractions, such as social media, and replacing them with activities that increase scholarly productivity.

### Second: Techniques for Recording the Differences Between Copies

- The Shaykh pointed out that recording the differences between copies (nusakh) is a fundamental pillar of work on manuscripts, since it helps build the researcher's faculty of precise observation.
- He explained the importance of these exercises in improving the researcher's understanding of texts and of the style of copyists (nussakh).
- He advised documenting the differences using precise, organized tables that show each variant clearly, which makes review and analysis easier.

### Third: The Development of Library Names and Its Bearing on Editing

- The Shaykh dealt with how the names of libraries have changed over time, pointing out that failing to realize these changes can lead to serious documentation errors.
- He called for consulting historical catalogues in order to understand the different names of the same place.
- He reviewed precise examples from the history of Egyptian libraries, such as the development of Dar al-Kutub from the “Kutubkhana” to “Dar al-Kutub al-Misriyya” (the Egyptian National Library), and then to the “General Egyptian Book Organization for the National Library and Archives” (al-Hay'a al-'Amma li-Dar al-Kutub wa-al-Watha'iq al-Qawmiyya).

### Fourth: Principles of Precise Citation of Texts

- The Shaykh explained that citations of texts must be made precisely to the folio, recto and verso (wajh and zahr), to ensure that they are easy to consult during review.
- He warned against using the term “page” loosely, stressing that this can cause confusion in tracing texts.
- He stressed the need to adhere to a unified method of citation, recording the folio and line numbers precisely.

### Fifth: The Influence of Qur'anic Orthography on Manuscript Texts

- The Shaykh reviewed how manuscript texts were influenced by the orthography (rasm) of the Mushaf, which led to differences in spelling between texts.
- He gave examples of this, such as writing words with imala, as in “أحداهما” (ahdahuma / ihdahuma, “one of the two”), and the effect of this on understanding the texts.
- He called on editors to distinguish between the orthography of the Mushaf and standard spelling (al-rasm al-imla'i), and to determine the effect that orthography may have on texts.

### Sixth: Dealing with Revisions and Additions in Texts

- The Shaykh discussed the phenomenon of additions (ilhaqat) that authors insert at later stages, explaining how these additions can cause differences between copies.
- He reviewed examples such as the additions made by al-Sakhawi in his own hand in copies of *Fath al-Mughith*.
- He stressed the importance of documenting these additions precisely, indicating their original positions and distinguishing them from the base texts.

### Seventh: The Influence of Dialects and the Cultural Idiom of Copyists

- The Shaykh dealt with the influence of copyists' local dialects on written texts, pointing out that dialects may be reflected in the choice of vocabulary or the use of particular letters.
- He gave examples from texts that reflect dialectal differences, and called for studying the historical and cultural context of copyists in order to understand these phenomena.

### Eighth: The Method for Dealing with Obscure Words

- The Shaykh stressed that obscure words in original texts must not be changed except with conclusive evidence supporting the interpretation.
- He called for presenting the different possible meanings of words in the footnotes, giving logical justification for each interpretation.
- He stressed the importance of adopting a careful method in interpreting obscure texts, which strengthens the precision and credibility of the edition.

**Close of the lesson:** The Shaykh concluded by stressing that editing texts is work that requires patience and perseverance, as well as attention to fine details that may seem marginal but are essential for understanding texts and reproducing them accurately. He called on researchers to adhere to a rigorous scholarly method at every stage of the work.

**Lessons Learned:**

- The importance of investing time efficiently to achieve scholarly progress.
- Understanding the development of library names and its effect on documenting texts.
- Adhering to a precise method of citation to ensure accurate checking.
- Recognizing the influence of Qur'anic orthography and copyists' dialects on manuscript texts.
- Developing the ability to deal with obscure texts in a scholarly way.
- Using the differences between copies as a tool for a deeper understanding of texts and for enriching the edition.
