### Lecture Title: Collation and the Rules of Copying in Manuscript Studies

#### Introduction

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari opened the lecture with an introduction that included supplication and glorification of Allah, stressing the importance of seeking knowledge and of preserving manuscripts as a repository of the scholarly and religious heritage. He pointed out that this thirteenth lecture in the series “Adhkar 'Ulum al-Makhtutat” focuses on a methodical and in-depth treatment of the process of collation (muqabala), which is one of the fundamental pillars for establishing edited texts, especially in the Islamic heritage.

### Main Themes

### 1. The Importance of Collation in Manuscript Studies

- Collation is a necessary process for ensuring the faithful transmission of texts from their original sources to later generations, and it is an innate human practice concerned with accuracy of documentation.
- The Shaykh drew attention to the fact that Muslims paid great attention to collation because of its close connection with the preservation of religious texts, citing the Noble Mushaf as the first model of precise verification, where review was carried out between the written texts and what was preserved in the hearts of those who had memorized it.
- The importance of collation increases with the value of the text being studied, especially if it relates to the religious sciences or to fundamental scholarly references.

### 2. Collation in the Science of Hadith

- The Shaykh stressed the centrality of collation in the science of hadith, since it ensures the accuracy of written narrations.
- He explained the difference between collation and presentation to the shaykh ('ard): the former aims to verify the written text, while the latter is concerned with direct oral transmission.
- He mentioned that the scholars stipulated that the narrator must collate his book to make sure it is free of errors and distortions (tahrif), which lends credibility to the preserved texts.

### 3. The Forms That Indicate Collation

- The indicators that a text has undergone collation include expressions such as “balagha” (“it reached [here]”, the collation mark or balagh) and “sahh” (“correct”), which appear in the margins and point to careful revision.
- The Shaykh warned against relying excessively on these indicators without a complete examination of the text, stressing that partial collation is not enough to guarantee that a text is free of errors.
- The importance of understanding the symbols used in manuscripts, which may carry a variety of meanings that require advanced awareness.

### 4. Problems Related to Collation

- The Shaykh stressed that collation reduces errors but does not eliminate them completely; it must therefore be accompanied by careful individual checking.
- Among the common errors: incorporating marginal notes (hawashi) into the base text without distinction, which leads to a change in meaning and weakens the credibility of the text.
- The Shaykh urged combining collation with personal checking, to make sure the texts are sound and to avoid adopting corrections that may be misleading.

### 5. Transposition in Texts

- The Shaykh dealt with the phenomenon of transposition (taqdim wa-ta'khir, moving material forward or back) that editors face, especially in historical texts, explaining that this phenomenon may be an indicator that the copy is in the author's own hand.
- He stressed the need to respect the author's vision in arranging the texts, affirming that this is part of the trust (amana) of editing.

### 6. Types of Errors in Copying

- The Shaykh pointed out that errors may arise from:
  - Inserting marginal notes into the texts without clear distinction.
  - Using colloquial language instead of classical Arabic (fusha).
  - Errors in numbers resulting from not following the grammatical rules.
- He explained that these errors mostly reflect the copyist's (nasikh's) inattention or inadequate training.

### 7. The Samples Used in Editing

- The Shaykh pointed out the importance of examining rare samples in manuscripts, since they provide living, applied examples that help strengthen the editor's skills.
- He criticized some writers for not following a methodical sequence in presenting ideas, which makes it harder for the reader to benefit from the texts.
- He stressed that studying samples is a fundamental tool for understanding the various patterns in manuscript copies, which strengthens the editor's competence.

### Recommendations and Advice

- The Shaykh called for patience and precision in the study of manuscripts, pointing out the importance of scholarly responsibility in this field.
- He recommended reading the reference works in manuscript studies, such as the works of Dr. Ayman Fu'ad Sayyid and Dr. Qasim al-Samarra'i, because of the practical and theoretical expertise they contain.
- He stressed the importance of being familiar with the terms and symbols used in manuscripts, considering this a foundation for understanding and editing texts accurately.
- He advised against assuming that collated texts are free of errors, pointing out the need for continuous verification to ensure their soundness.

- Analyzing the samples included in these books, with a focus on understanding the symbols and signs.

### Conclusion

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari concluded the lecture with supplication, beseeching Allah to make these scholarly gatherings sincerely for His Noble Face. He encouraged those present to make use of these meetings to strengthen scholarly ties and consolidate the information they had acquired. He stressed the importance of adopting a sound scholarly approach aimed at editing texts accurately and preserving the heritage according to the highest standards of scholarly integrity.
