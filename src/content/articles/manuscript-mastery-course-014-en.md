## A Fitting Title for the Lecture

“The Methodology of Analyzing and Studying Arabic Manuscripts: Between Samples and Academic Concepts”

### Introduction

The lecture opened with greetings and supplication, highlighting the importance of studying Arabic manuscripts as a specialized discipline that enriches heritage research. Shaykh Salih al-Azhari offered a scholarly analysis of the book *al-Makhtut al-'Arabi* (The Arabic Manuscript) by Dr. Ayman Fu'ad Sayyid, showing its value as a central source for the study of manuscripts in the academic context. The Shaykh stressed the need to understand the theoretical and applied frameworks the book puts forward, since it represents a cornerstone of knowledge in this field.

## Main Elements

### 1. The Importance of *al-Makhtut al-'Arabi* as a Pivotal Source

- **A comprehensive academic reference:** the book is one of the most important academic references in the study of manuscripts, since it combines theory and application and presents physical and documentary samples in a masterly way.
- **Highlighting historical samples:** the book offers rare examples of manuscripts preserved in libraries around the world, many of which have not been published on the internet, which makes them highly valuable to researchers.
- **Critical observations:** Shaykh al-Azhari pointed out the author's clear influence by the Orientalist school, while stressing that this does not diminish the book's great scholarly value.

### 2. The Conceptual Methodology of Codicology

- **A methodical definition:** codicology is concerned with studying the physical elements that make up the manuscript (makhtut), such as paper, ink, binding and catchwords (ta'qibat). This study provides a precise understanding of the structure of the manuscript.
- **Accompanying texts:** the accompanying texts include audition certificates (sama'at), licenses (ijazat), reading certificates (qira'at), ownership notes (tamallukat) and endowment notes (waqfiyyat). These elements provide a rich context for understanding the original texts.
- **The concept of “khawarij al-nusus”:** the Shaykh preferred to use the expression “accompanying texts” (al-nusus al-musahiba) rather than “texts outside the text” (khawarij al-nusus), as it is more precise in describing texts that are not directly connected to the main text.

### 3. Research Ideas and the Methodology of the Book's Introduction

- **Rich research ideas:** the introduction contains a number of research ideas that can be developed into in-depth academic projects. The Shaykh encouraged the participants to make use of these ideas in developing their own studies.
- **Earlier sources:** the author presented a list of the most important sources that dealt with the study of manuscripts before him, stressing their importance for understanding methodological developments in this field.
- **Critical analysis:** the Shaykh called for reading the introduction carefully in order to understand the theoretical background the author relied on, with a focus on the importance of terms and concepts.

### 4. The Technical Study of Manuscripts: Catchwords and Quires

- **Catchwords (ta'qibat):**
  - **Definition:** recording the last word on the right-hand page at the bottom of the following page, to ensure the sequence of the text.
  - **Use:** catchwords are used to ensure that the quires (kurrasat) are arranged accurately.
  - **Variations:** the numbering methods included Arabic numerals, abjad letters and Coptic numerals.
- **Quires (kurrasat):** the Shaykh dealt with the techniques for arranging quires, such as numbering only the first half to save time and effort.

### 5. Drafts and Fair Copies: An Academic Approach

- **Distinguishing between them:**
  - **Drafts (musawwadat):** represent the initial stage, in which the author's preliminary notes are gathered.
  - **Fair copies (mubayyadat):** the final, revised copies, which may contain major changes, especially if they were prepared by someone other than the author.
- **Analyzing texts:** the Shaykh presented examples of texts from drafts and fair copies, explaining the importance of understanding terms such as “al-dasatir” (rough working copies) and “al-tabyid” (making a fair copy) in order to develop critical analytical skills.

### 6. Methodological Notes on Research Work

- **Research guidance:**
  - Paying attention to heritage texts as a tool for strengthening academic understanding.
  - Starting to produce research works even if they are preliminary, and improving them over time.
- **Inspiring experiences:** the Shaykh reviewed the experiences of eminent scholars in editing manuscripts, stressing the importance of perseverance and diligence.

### 7. Suggested Assignments

- **Extended reading:**
  - Studying the section on “Dated Manuscripts” (pp. 399–419).
  - Reading the section on “Audition Licenses” (ijazat al-sama', pp. 473–507) carefully.
- **Review and analysis:**
  - Reviewing the previous sections to ensure an integrated understanding.
  - Applying the conceptual methods in analyzing manuscripts.

## Conclusion

The Shaykh stressed the importance of *al-Makhtut al-'Arabi* as an indispensable scholarly source in the field of text editing. He called on the participants to make use of the book's contents in preparing their studies and research projects, stressing the importance of planning and dividing scholarly goals in order to achieve them effectively.

## Additional Recommendations

- **Managing research:** devoting regular daily hours to academic work.
- **Scholarly cooperation:** building networks of cooperation with researchers and exchanging expertise.
- **Continuity:** benefiting from recent studies and updating one's knowledge periodically.

## Closing

The lecture ended with encouragement toward critical reading and perseverance in seeking knowledge. The Shaykh mentioned future plans for scholarly sessions aimed at studying new works and strengthening a lasting relationship with scholarly research, so as to help develop the participants' academic skills and achieve distinctive accomplishments.
