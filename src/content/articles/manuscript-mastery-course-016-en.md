**Lesson title: An In-Depth Analysis of the Development of Manuscript Copies and the Role of the Author and the Copyists**

**Introduction**

The lecture opened with supplication, asking Allah for success in making these scholarly gatherings sincerely for His Noble Face. The session focused on advanced issues in manuscript studies, dealing with the analysis of how texts developed during their composition and circulation, and exploring the decisive role played by authors and copyists (nussakh). The technical terms related to classifying texts were discussed, with precise historical examples offered to clarify these complex issues.

**Main Elements**

### 1. The Precise Distinction Between Audition and Reading to the Shaykh

- **Audition (sama'):** consists of the student listening to the text as the shaykh delivers it directly; according to the majority of scholars, it is the most reliable method of transmitting knowledge.
- **Reading (qira'a):** takes place when the student reads the text while the shaykh listens and corrects; it ranks below audition but remains reliable within its own framework.
- The importance of understanding the historical and scholarly context of each of the two methods was pointed out.

### 2. Terms Related to the Development of Texts

- **The draft (musawwada):** the initial copy, characterized by erasures and revisions, which is regarded as a direct reflection of the author's thought process.
- **The fair copy (mubayyada):** the revised copy produced after reviewing the draft, which is considered ready for publication or circulation.
- **The first and second versions (al-ibrazat al-ula wa-al-thaniya):** refer to the restructuring and revision of texts in line with new intellectual developments or corrections.

### 3. Copies in the Author's Hand and Their Importance

- Copies in the author's hand represent the most reliable original, since they reflect the author's explicit intentions.
- Copies presented to the author or read to him occupy second rank, especially when they contain direct corrections or comments.
- Copies transcribed from the author's hand may sometimes surpass them if they were copied by expert hands with a high degree of accuracy.

### 4. Catchwords and Their Role in Organizing Texts

- Catchwords (ta'qibat) are used as a tool for ordering the quires (kurrasat) and ensuring the continuity of texts.
- The session discussed the possible errors resulting from non-original catchwords, which may affect the accuracy of texts.
- It was stressed that catchwords must be dealt with critically and analyzed as part of the study of texts.

### 5. Challenges in Dealing with Drafts and Fair Copies

- Many drafts have not reached us because of the initial circumstances of their production or because of neglect.
- Spelling errors resulting from weak copyists or poor copying conditions represent a prominent challenge.
- The need to apply a rigorous critical method to analyze and understand texts was highlighted.

### 6. The Role of Copyists Between Improvement and Distortion

- Copyists have great freedom in dealing with texts, which makes it necessary to understand their intentions and motives.
- Studying the copyist's (nasikh's) style is a pivotal tool for evaluating the quality of a text.
- The importance of distinguishing between constructive corrections and modifications that lead to distortion (tahrif) of the text was discussed.

### 7. Problems with the Rules of Evaluation

- The session discussed the problems of over-reliance on theoretical rules that may not be sufficiently flexible.
- It was stressed that theory must be combined with practical application in order to understand texts better.
- Examples were reviewed showing how rules can serve as flexible guidance rather than being rigid and coercive.

### 8. The Importance of Documentation and Methodical Study

- The editor must rely on clear evidence and indicators to determine the original copy.
- Careful reading of texts and analysis of indicators are the first step in building a critical faculty.
- A practical strategy combining theoretical study and practical work was put forward to improve research competence.

**Conclusion**

The session concluded by stressing the importance of making use of the remainder of the course to deepen the skills acquired. The book *Khutut al-'Ulama'* (The Hands of the Scholars) was highlighted as an essential tool for understanding manuscripts. The participants were encouraged to adopt a practical and critical approach in order to gain the greatest benefit.
