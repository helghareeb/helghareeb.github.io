**Lecture title:** Analyzing Marginal Notes and Annotations in Manuscripts: Methodology and Significance

**Introduction:**

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened his seventeenth lecture with supplication and praise of Allah, explaining that this lecture belongs to the series "Mastering the Sciences of Manuscripts." The lecture focused on the study of marginal notes (hawashi) and annotations (taqyidat) in manuscript copies (nusakh khattiyya), reviewing the methodological approaches to analyzing them and using them in the critical editing (tahqiq) of texts. The Shaykh also pointed to the decisive role these marginal notes play in enriching textual studies and developing the tools of critical analysis.

**Main elements:**

### The Importance of Marginal Notes in Manuscripts

- **Approximate dating of copies:**
  - Marginal notes are considered a principal means of estimating when manuscript copies were written, especially in the absence of explicit dates.
  - For example, if a copy contains a marginal note in the hand of a scholar who died in the seventh century AH, one can infer that the copy was written before that date.
- **Strengthening the reliability of the copy:**
  - Marginal notes add further scholarly value to texts, particularly when they are written in the hands of famous scholars or contain precise scholarly comments.
  - Marginal notes often contain additions and explanations that help the editor complete the texts and understand them more deeply.
- **Completing the original texts:**
  - Marginal notes fill textual gaps and add quotations from lost sources, which makes them an indispensable tool in recovering heritage texts.

### Method for Identifying the Writer of a Marginal Note

- **Explicit statements:**
  - Sometimes the name of the writer of the marginal note is mentioned at the beginning or end of the copy, or within the marginal notes themselves.
  - For example: "Written by so-and-so," or abbreviated signatures indicating the writer's identity.
- **Comparing hands:**
  - By comparing the hand (khatt) of the marginal notes with the hands of other texts, such as ownership notes (tamalluk) or endowment notes (waqfiyyat), the writer can be identified.
- **Expertise in scholars' hands:**
  - Expertise in reading scholars' handwriting is an effective tool for determining the writer's identity, especially when explicit statements are absent.

### Classifications of Marginal Notes

- **By their writer:**
  - Marginal notes are classified into those written by the author himself and those added by copyists (nussakh) or other readers.
  - The author's own marginal notes are of special importance, since they reflect his view and interpretation of the text.
- **By their content:**
  - **Explanations and comments:** they interpret the text and clarify its meanings.
  - **Quotations from other sources:** they add to the text by including additional information.

### The Research Value of Marginal Notes

- **Documenting lost sources:**
  - Marginal notes reveal lost or rare books, which makes them a rich source of information.
  - Example: the project "Taqyidat al-'Ulama' al-Kibar" (Annotations of the Great Scholars) uses marginal notes to recover the heritage.
- **Highlighting scholars' views:**
  - Marginal notes shed light on positions and ideas of scholars that were not recorded in their own works.
- **Enriching textual studies:**
  - Marginal notes contribute to carrying out textual comparisons and offering in-depth critical insights.

### Applied Examples

- The Shaykh presented examples from *Tabaqat al-Subki* that highlight the importance of analyzing marginal notes and linking them to sources and copyists.
- He discussed the marginal note of Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Manufi as a model illustrating the value of marginal notes in offering useful criticisms and quotations.
- He pointed to the need to trace marginal notes in order to understand the accompanying texts and the symbols that distinguish them.

### Texts Accompanying the Copy

- **Ownership notes:**
  - They show the copy's passage between individuals and can be arranged chronologically to clarify the context of its ownership.
- **Endowment and reading notes (waqfiyyat and mutala'at):**
  - They highlight the scholarly activity connected with the copy and reveal the names of the scholars who acquired it.
- **Marks of precision and correction (dabt and tahrir):**
  - They show how copyists dealt with the texts, including additions and modifications.

### Conclusion and Closing Remarks

- Shaykh Salih al-Azhari stressed the importance of studying marginal notes and annotations in depth, emphasizing their role in the critical editing of texts.
- He urged the trainees to develop their skills in analyzing marginal notes, pointing out that this process is a fundamental pillar for producing original research.

- Analyze the accompanying texts, such as ownership notes and endowment notes.
- Prepare detailed analytical reports on the findings.
- Apply the methodologies discussed to practical examples.
