### Article Title: Methods of Critical Editing and Handling Manuscripts: Lessons Learned

#### Introduction

In this lecture Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari addressed a number of pivotal issues relating to the science of critically editing (tahqiq) manuscripts (makhtutat), highlighting the importance of precise scholarly methods in reading and analyzing manuscript texts. The Shaykh also presented a set of practical models confirming the need to distinguish between marginal notes (hawashi), main texts (mutun) and comments. He shed light on practical experiences and common errors in the field of manuscript editing, offering practical insights aimed at raising researchers' competence.

#### Main Themes

- The Shaykh pointed to the logical order of the books covered in the course, explaining that the choice of books is not random but based on a sequence that serves gradual understanding, so that basic concepts are built before moving on to more complex issues.
- Examples of the selected books:
  - The book "Manhaj Kitabat al-Makhtutat" (The Method of Writing Manuscripts): helps in understanding how to read correctly and decipher old hands.
  - The book "Anmat al-Tawthiq fi al-Makhtut" (Patterns of Documentation in the Manuscript): highlights methods of documentation and teaches how to extract information from accompanying texts.
  - Basic works on editing such as *Tahqiq al-Nusus* (Editing Texts) by Abd al-Salam Harun and *Tahqiq al-Nuskha al-Farida* (Editing the Unique Copy) by Dr. Bashshar Awwad, which are considered important references in this field.
- The Shaykh emphasized the need to be acquainted with a diverse range of sources for a broader and more comprehensive understanding of the science of editing.

- The Shaykh explained that many copyists (nussakh) used to insert comments and marginal notes into the main texts, which led to great confusion in understanding and analyzing the texts.
- He called for distinguishing between the original texts and additions that may come from copyists or later editors. The Shaykh stressed the importance of extremely careful scrutiny in determining the source of texts.
- He presented practical examples showing how these additions can lead to distortion (tahrif) of the meaning or mislead the reader.

- The Shaykh addressed the importance of verifying the attribution of texts to their authors, explaining the means that can be followed:
  - Comparing the hands with other hands of the author.
  - Studying external evidence such as the audition certificates (sama') and licenses (ijazat) found on the manuscripts.
  - Relying on primary sources, such as books written in the author's hand, and citing quotations that match them.
- The Shaykh emphasized that relying on weak evidence may lead to inaccurate results, stressing the importance of multiple proofs to document the attribution of texts precisely.
- He pointed to examples from the Islamic heritage in which the attribution of texts was misjudged, which later harmed the scholarly material.

- The Shaykh stressed the need to reread edited texts more than once using the existing rules, which helps consolidate information and correct errors.
- He gave examples of common errors in attributing books, pointing to some scholars who fell into these errors despite their wide fame.
- He emphasized the importance of analyzing texts critically to know the historical and social context of the texts, which helps toward a deeper understanding and greater accuracy.

- The Shaykh responded to the trainees' questions, including:
  - The difference between the draft (musawwada) and the fair copy (mubayyada), and the importance of knowing the type of copy in determining its scholarly value.
  - How to deal with books containing additions of unclear origin, and the steps to follow to determine the origin of the additions.
- He addressed the phenomenon of change in handwriting due to age or health conditions, emphasizing the importance of verification when comparing hands, and mentioning examples from the heritage of changes in handwriting and their effect on editing.
- The Shaykh added illustrative examples from his own experience in dealing with rare manuscripts and the problems he faced.

#### Practical Recommendations

- **Continuous training**: the Shaykh emphasized the importance of practice in editing manuscripts through reading and comparing texts regularly. He recommended periodic exercises to improve editing skills.
- **Reliable sources**: one should rely on primary sources and the authors' original hands as far as possible, and avoid relying on doubtful copies without verifying their soundness.
- **Methodical documentation**: the Shaykh called for setting strict rules when documenting and transmitting texts, taking care to be precise and clear. He recommended using modern technological tools to facilitate the documentation process.
- **Rereading and analysis**: the Shaykh advised editors to reread and analyze texts in light of new information that may appear during editing.
- **Teamwork**: the Shaykh stressed the importance of teamwork among editors to exchange experience and solve shared problems.

#### Conclusion

The lecture highlighted the importance of methodology and precision in editing manuscripts, with specialists committed to scholarly integrity and a tireless search for the truth. The Shaykh also confirmed that periodic sessions would continue to be held to discuss and analyze texts, helping to refine the trainees' skills and develop their understanding of this science. He called for continued research and learning, pointing out that the science of editing is an ever-developing field that requires patience and continuous effort.
