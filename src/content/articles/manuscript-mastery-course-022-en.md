### Analyzing the Title and the Hand in Arabic Manuscripts

#### An Entry Point to Understanding Arabic Manuscripts

The critical editing (tahqiq) of manuscripts (makhtutat) is one of the fundamental sciences that enable the researcher to explore the depths of the Arab-Islamic heritage. This science requires a deep understanding of texts and their historical contexts, in addition to experience in dealing with old hands and different forms of recording. In this context, this article discusses several core themes relating to the analysis of the title and the hand (khatt) in manuscripts.

#### 1. The Theoretical Framework for Analyzing the Title in Manuscripts

- The title is the face of the manuscript, and one must verify whether the written text represents the original title or merely an explanatory description. This step is a way into understanding the historical and intellectual context of the text.
- Analyzing the title relies on a careful study of the hand used and a comparison of it with the hand of the author or the copyist (nasikh). This helps confirm the attribution of the title to its original writer.
- Inferences can be drawn from accompanying phrases such as "rahimahu Allah" (may Allah have mercy on him) or "'afa Allah 'anhu" (may Allah pardon him), since these phrases sometimes carry chronological indications that help determine the time of writing.

#### 2. Techniques for Analyzing Hands and Their Role in Editing Texts

- The hand is a fundamental element in the study of manuscripts, since it reflects the identity of the writer and the circumstances of copying. Distinguishing between original and added hands requires a precise understanding of writing techniques and tools.
- The study includes analyzing the size of the script, the type of ink and the style of writing. These factors allow conclusions about the historical and geographical circumstances of the copying process.
- Comparing recurring words and the writing pattern helps reveal the possibility of multiple writers or later interventions.

#### 3. The Importance of Notes (Quyud) as Research Tools

- Notes written in the margins or on the first pages are a rich source of information. They can shed light on the stages of the manuscript's passage between different hands.
- Analyzing ownership notes (tamalluk), reading notes (mutala'at) and endowment notes (waqf) provides a temporal and spatial context for the manuscript's journey. This analysis is used to build a historical narrative of the development and transmission of texts.
- Notes are indispensable tools for identifying the owners of a manuscript and where it was located in different periods, which helps in understanding the development of cultural heritage.

#### 4. Applied Studies Following Shaykh Salih al-Azhari

- Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari presents practical models of manuscript analysis, especially in manuscripts of al-Subki such as *Tabaqat al-Shafi'iyya*. His method relies on analyzing hands and determining when texts were written on the basis of the wording used.
- Studying ownership notes such as "malakahu min fadl Allah" (he came to own it by the bounty of Allah) reveals the stages of the manuscript's transmission and its circumstances.
- The Shaykh highlights the importance of comparing the different hands of the author and of the readers (mutali'un) to arrive at a precise edition of the texts.

#### 5. Marginal Additions and Marginal Notes

- Marginal notes (hawashi) carry comments that may add new dimensions to understanding the original text. Studying these marginal notes is a tool for editing texts more deeply.
- Determining the source of marginal notes, whether from the author or later additions, helps in assessing the authenticity of the manuscript.
- Marginal notes and comments sometimes reflect the engagement of the text's contemporaries with its content, adding cultural and social dimensions to the study of the manuscript.

#### 6. Challenges of the Editing Process

- Additions or modifications made during copying are a major challenge in editing texts. This requires analytical skills to identify the original texts.
- Reordering the quires (kurrasat) and understanding their sequence is another obstacle facing researchers. The editor needs a methodical awareness of the order and arrangement of texts.
- Differences in the ink or paper used in the same manuscript may reflect multiple stages of copying or later interventions.

#### 7. Practical Examples and Lessons Learned

- Studying the transfer of manuscripts between libraries, such as the King Saud Library and the Imam library, sheds light on the effect of these processes on their condition.
- Studying the systems of preservation and storage in different libraries clarifies how to deal with old and new shelfmarks.
- Analyzing historical registers and catalogues is a necessary tool for understanding the transfer of manuscripts.

#### 8. Methods of Documentation and Cataloguing

- The study of manuscripts relies on advanced cataloguing systems that ensure easy access to copies.
- Analyzing the old and new numbers of manuscripts makes it possible to trace changes of ownership and understand the relationship between libraries.
- Distinguishing between general and special numbers in different libraries helps in understanding archival systems.

#### Conclusions and Recommendations

- The researcher must commit to continuous training in analyzing manuscripts, since practical experience strengthens the understanding of the complexities of texts.
- Attention to fine details, such as ink, hands and paper size, is an essential part of the editing process.
- Comparing multiple copies of the same manuscript is necessary to obtain a complete picture of the text.
- Researchers should take care to document cataloguing systems, using modern numbers to call up manuscripts easily.
- Marginal notes and comments are a rich source for understanding texts, and they should be studied carefully to understand the developments that texts have undergone over time.

### Conclusion

This text reflects the advanced academic effort offered by Shaykh Salih al-Azhari in the field of studying and analyzing manuscripts. This science is the cornerstone of recovering and understanding the Islamic heritage, and the Shaykh's method is a practical guide for academics and researchers seeking to edit texts and enrich heritage studies.
