### Main Title

**Methods of Verifying Manuscript Titles: Importance and Methodological Means**

### Introduction

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari began the lecture with supplication and by welcoming those present, emphasizing the importance of researchers' commitment to attending scholarly sessions because of the opportunities they offer to enrich understanding and strengthen engagement with the material. The Shaykh stressed that these sessions are not merely a presentation of information but platforms for training in methodical and critical analysis, which is pivotal for excelling in the study and critical editing (tahqiq) of manuscripts (makhtutat).

### The Importance of Determining the Correct Title of a Book

Establishing the correct title of manuscripts is a central issue in heritage studies because of its profound effects, which extend to many scholarly and methodological dimensions, the most prominent of which are:

- **Clarifying the author's conditions and method**:
  - The title is a reflection of the author's method and scholarly aims. For example, the names of al-Bukhari's books show the precision of his conditions and criteria in selecting hadith.
- **Avoiding illusory duplication**:
  - Careful checking helps prevent the phenomenon of multiple titles for a single book, which may lead researchers to imagine that the author wrote different works.
- **Enriching the history of scholarship**:
  - Titles reflect the development of terminology over the ages, which makes them an effective tool for understanding the history of the Islamic sciences.
- **Avoiding documentation errors**:
  - Correct titles help in building accurate biographies and analyzing the book's subjects in depth.
- **Strengthening academic organization**:
  - Accurate titles enable libraries and catalogues to classify books in a way that makes it easy for researchers to access and use them.

### Methodological Means of Identifying the Correct Title

The Shaykh reviewed ten methodological means used to verify the correct title of manuscripts, pointing to the importance of combining critical analysis with material evidence:

- **The title in the author's hand on the title page**:
  - This is the strongest means, but it is rare.
  - The Shaykh pointed to examples such as Ibn Hajar and al-Sakhawi, where the hand of the author or one of his students appears on the copy.
- **Explicit naming in the introduction**:
  - Common among later authors, who tend to state the title of the book explicitly in its introduction.
  - This means is considered a reliable reference when other means are absent.
- **Naming within the body of the book**:
  - Rare, but it sometimes appears when the naming is absent from the introduction.
  - It is considered additional evidence that strengthens other evidence.
- **Naming in an authoritative copy**:
  - This refers to copies that were read before the author or are close to his time.
  - The Shaykh pointed to the need to verify that the copy is free of interventions by the copyist.
- **Naming in another book by the author**:
  - Considered decisive evidence if the author mentions the title of his book in other works.
  - Used to confirm the title and to exclude doubtful names.
- **The title mentioned in the conclusion**:
  - It may have been subject to alteration, but it is a helpful means when stronger evidence is absent.
- **The title in books of catalogues, programs and registers (faharis, baramij and athbat)**:
  - These are documented in sources such as *Fahrasat Ibn Khayr*, where the title is transmitted directly from the author.
  - The Shaykh pointed out that closeness to the author's time increases the reliability of these titles.
- **What is mentioned in biographical works**:
  - Used as a supporting means when the original texts are absent.
- **Sensing the author's style and matching the subject**:
  - Used as a last resort when none of the other means is available.
  - It relies on the researcher's familiarity with the author's style and the book's subject.

### Methodological Challenges in Verifying Titles

The Shaykh discussed the challenges relating to verifying manuscript titles, highlighting the complexity of the process through vivid examples:

- **Illusory multiplication of books**:
  - He pointed to the case of *Kashf al-Zunun*, where a single book is mentioned under multiple titles, such as Ibn al-Qayyim's book "Bada'i' al-Fara'id," which is also called "Badi' al-Fara'id."
- **Changes in titles over time**:
  - Sometimes the author modifies the title of the book during the different stages of composition.
  - Example: al-Dhahabi, who changed the titles of the volumes of *Tarikh al-Islam* while compiling them.
- **Contradiction between the introduction and the title page**:
  - This calls for analyzing the different contexts to determine the most accurate name.
- **Disagreement between copies**:
  - The Shaykh stressed the importance of studying the different copies carefully, focusing on the antiquity and credibility of the copies.

### Applied Examples

The Shaykh presented examples showing how to deal with challenges relating to titles:

- Al-Dhahabi's book *Tarikh al-Islam*, which saw changes in the titles of its volumes.
- *Al-Qawl al-Mu'tabar* by al-Sakhawi, where the author modified the title of the book after writing the introduction.
- The Shaykh emphasized studying these examples carefully to derive the correct applied rules.
