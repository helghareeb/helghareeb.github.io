### Lesson Title: Editing Precious Audition Certificates and Comparing Them with Old Manuscripts

### Main Elements of the Lesson

- **Opening and supplication:**
  - Opening the session with praise of Allah and blessings upon the Prophet, and a supplication that these sessions be sincerely for the sake of Allah, the Most Generous.
- **Presenting the copy under study:**
  - Describing the precious copy belonging to the manuscript *Juz' Muhammad ibn Makhlad al-'Attar*.
  - Stating the date of the copy and its journey from the library of Ali ibn Abis al-Mursili to Egypt.
- **The importance of the copies under study:**
  - Clarifying the difference between the original copy transferred from the Berlin copy and the copy of Ibn Nafis.
  - The importance of verifying audition lists (tabaqat al-sama'at) and their role in determining the credibility of copies.
- **Analyzing the texts of the audition certificates:**
  - A detailed reading of the texts of the audition certificates (sama'at) of the various copies.
  - Distinguishing between descriptions of copies and audition lists.
  - Explaining the differences between copied-over lists and original lists.
- **Identifying research problems:**
  - The difficulty of linking lists together and identifying the elements specific to audition lists.
  - The importance of consulting biographical dictionaries to fix names and lineages accurately.
- **Methods of copying and writing:**
  - Explaining concepts such as the first and second release (ibraz) of a book.
  - The importance of carefully reviewing copies and the revisions authors make.
- **The editor's tools in dealing with manuscripts:**
  - How to document blank spaces (bayadat) in copies and specify their extent to avoid confusion.
  - The importance of drawing up a map of the audition certificates to trace the path of the manuscript.
- **Interactive discussions:**
  - Answering the trainees' questions on fine concepts such as the license by attendance (al-ijaza al-huduriyya) and how it differs from audition.
  - Presenting examples of royal audition sessions.
- **Closing and guidance:**
  - Inviting the trainees to listen to the previous theoretical lectures and to practice reading continuously.
  - Stressing the importance of drawing a clear map for every audition-bearing copy one works on.

### Note

This lesson is distinguished by its depth and its detailed analysis of the manuscript *Juz' Muhammad ibn Makhlad al-'Attar*, linking it to its historical and scholarly context, while highlighting the importance of reading texts carefully and analyzing them within the framework of scholarly editing.
