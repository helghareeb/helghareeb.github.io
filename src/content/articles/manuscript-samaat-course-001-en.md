**Lesson title:** Audition Certificates Recorded on Manuscripts - Structure, Use and Analytical Skills

**Introduction:** This lesson, delivered by Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari, examines the concept of the audition certificates (sama'at) recorded on manuscripts (makhtutat) as a central mechanism for documenting and transmitting Islamic knowledge across generations. The Shaykh focuses on the theoretical and practical analysis of this phenomenon, drawing on precise examples from manuscripts, in order to clarify its importance and strengthen researchers' ability to handle it skillfully and with academic method.

### Main Elements of the Lesson

### 1. Defining Audition Certificates and the Modes of Transmitting Knowledge

- Audition certificates are a precise means used to ensure the transmission of knowledge through written attestations recorded on manuscripts. The most prominent modes of receiving knowledge (turuq al-tahammul) include:
  - **Hearing from the shaykh's own recitation (al-sama' min lafz al-shaykh):** the text is read directly by the shaykh and the students hear it.
  - **Reading to the shaykh (al-qira'a 'ala al-shaykh):** the student reads the text aloud before the shaykh, who corrects and clarifies.
  - **Handing over (munawala):** delivering a copy of the book together with an acknowledgment of its soundness.
  - **License (ijaza):** formal permission from the shaykh to the student to transmit the text, which may be oral or written.
- These modes highlight the importance of documenting knowledge in Islam as a unique heritage marked by precision and completeness.

### 2. The Structural Elements of Audition Certificates

- **Phrases indicating the mode of receiving:**
  - Phrases such as "I heard from the shaykh..." or "I read it to the shaykh..." are used to specify how the text was transmitted.
  - The wording of these phrases differs depending on whether the writer is the shaykh himself or one of his students.
- **Reference to the book that was read:**
  - The book is referred to with phrases such as "I heard this part (juz')", or by naming the full title, such as *Jami' Bayan al-'Ilm wa-Fadlih*, or in abbreviated form, such as *Kitab al-'Ilm*.
  - This variety reflects different periods or terminological preferences among scholars.
- **Mention of the name of the transmitting authority (musmi'):**
  - The name begins with words of praise that highlight the shaykh's standing, such as "the eminent imam...".
  - The shaykh's full name is sometimes followed by a supplication such as "may Allah let us enjoy his continued presence", which reflects the etiquette of documentation.

### 3. The Roles of Those Present at Audition Sessions

- **The shaykh:** responsible for delivery and for ensuring the accuracy of the reading.
- **The reader (qari'):** the student who reads the texts.
- **The controller (dabit):** records attendance and absence and specifies where the reading began and ended.
- **The writer (katib):** documents what took place in the session, including the texts of the audition certificates written on the manuscripts.
- These roles ensure accuracy in scholarly transmission and the documentation of the details needed for the credibility of the process.

### 4. The Importance of Analyzing Audition Certificates

- Careful analysis of audition certificates makes it possible to infer missing information and to understand texts in their historical context.
- Analyzing the wording and terminology reduces errors of understanding and interpretation.
- Among the main benefits:
  - Establishing the attribution of texts to their authors.
  - Documenting the different titles of books across the ages.
  - Building scholarly biographies of scholars.
  - Understanding the historical development of the methods of transmitting knowledge.

### 5. Audition Certificates Across the Ages

- The roots of audition certificates go back to the second century AH, reflecting the depth of documentary practice in Islam.
- The oldest documented audition certificate dates to the year 170 AH.
- Audition certificates were not limited to Prophetic hadith; they extended to jurisprudence (fiqh), medicine, astronomy and history.

### 6. Problems in Understanding Audition Certificates

- Differences in titles between various audition certificates may be wrongly understood as a contradiction, while in reality they reflect the variety of terminological contexts.
- Lacking a precise understanding of how audition certificates are structured can lead to methodological errors when editing texts or attributing them.

### 7. Areas in Which Audition Certificates Are Useful

- Audition certificates are used to study the biographies of scholars and authors with precision.
- They support research in hadith studies, jurisprudence and other applied sciences.
- They document the development of Islamic thought and the stages of the transmission of texts, and they highlight the cultural interaction between scholars and their students.

### Conclusion

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari concluded the lesson by stressing the importance of audition certificates as an indispensable scholarly tool for editing texts (tahqiq) and understanding manuscript studies. He pointed out that they are a key to understanding the development of the Islamic sciences and their transmission across generations.
