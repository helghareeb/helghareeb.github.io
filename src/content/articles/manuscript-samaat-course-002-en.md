### Main Title: The Structure of the Audition Certificate Recorded on Arabic Manuscripts - Analysis and Documentation - Day Two of Course 4

### Introduction

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari opened the second lecture of the fourth course by highlighting the importance of the audition certificate (sama') recorded on manuscripts as an essential part of the hadith sciences, since it is the link between theoretical method and practical application in the study of manuscript heritage. He stressed that these audition certificates are not mere conventional documents; rather, they are decisive elements for understanding how scholarly texts were transmitted across generations. He urged the attendees to review the first lecture in order to grasp the theoretical framework on which the details of this lesson would be built.

### Main Topics

### 1. Classifying the Types of Recorded Audition

- The Shaykh presented four main types of audition, stressing the need to recognize the fine differences between them:
  - **Audition from the shaykh's own recitation:** hearing the texts directly from the shaykh.
  - **Audition through the reading of a student or someone else to the shaykh:** a student or one of those present undertakes the reading while the shaykh checks it.
  - **License (ijaza):** permission to transmit without hearing the whole text.
  - **Handing over (munawala):** delivering a written copy together with a license to transmit it.
- The Shaykh pointed out that these types represent scholarly and methodological foundations that show the precision with which texts were documented in the manuscript heritage.

### 2. Conditions of the Transmitting Authority and the Right to Transmit

- The Shaykh summarized the conditions required to preside over an audition session:
  - The transmitting authority (musmi') must be the author of the book or hold a license connected by a chain of transmission (isnad) to the author.
  - He must possess a documented and precise chain linking the transmitter to the series of narrators all the way to the author.
- The Shaykh discussed the challenges of verifying these conditions and how the right to transmit is established through the complete chain, pointing to the errors some editors fall into through lack of scrutiny.

### 3. The Components of a Recorded Audition List

- The Shaykh identified the basic elements that make up an audition list (tabaqat al-sama'):
  - **The audition formula:** the type of audition (audition, license, handing over).
  - **The title of the book:** given in abbreviated or full form depending on the context.
  - **The name of the transmitting authority:** accompanied by expressions of veneration and praise.
  - **The chain of transmission:** the essential link between the transmitting authority and the author.
  - **The names of those present:** including their agnomens (kuna), honorific titles (alqab) and supplications, with their ages specified, especially for children.
  - **The place and date of the audition:** the location and time are documented precisely.

### 4. Details of the Chain of Transmission and How It Is Documented

- The Shaykh addressed the strategies for documenting the chain:
  - Recording the full chain when space allows.
  - Referring to other places in the text that contain the chain.
  - Using abbreviated expressions such as "with his chain given at the beginning" (bi-sanadihi awwalan) to refer to the chain mentioned at the start of the copy.
- He pointed to the importance of distinguishing between the original chain of the copy and a transmitted (copied-over) chain, explaining how this distinction can affect the accuracy of the edition.

### 5. Recording and Ordering the Names of Those Present

- The Shaykh explained how the names of those present are documented:
  - Grouping names according to family relationships to save space and avoid confusion.
  - Attaching titles and supplications that reflect each person's standing.
  - Indicating ages with expressions such as "was present" (hadara), especially for children below the age of discernment.

### 6. The License: Using It to Reinforce the Audition

- The Shaykh pointed out that the license is used in some cases to make up for absence or to remedy a gap in the audition.
- The license is considered a means of documenting the transmission and repairing any weakness that may result from not having attended the whole text.

### 7. The Roles of the Writer and the Controller of the Audition

- The Shaykh explained the difference between the writer and the controller of the audition (dabit al-sama'):
  - The writer is responsible for composing the texts.
  - The controller of the audition follows attendance and absence and checks the details.
- The Shaykh referred to examples from manuscripts showing how the roles were separated or combined when needed.

### 8. Practical Examples of Audition Lists

- The Shaykh presented practical models showing how audition lists were worded:
  - Identifying where the audition certificates are placed in the margins of manuscripts.
  - Indicating the ages of those present with precise expressions.
  - Accurately documenting texts transferred between different copies.

### 9. Challenges in Editing Audition Lists

- The Shaykh addressed the common errors editors fall into because of mistaken interpretation or inaccurate reading.
- He stressed the importance of practical training to understand the contexts surrounding manuscript texts, insisting on careful analysis to avoid mistaken projections.

### Conclusion

The Shaykh concluded the lecture by inviting the attendees to participate and ask questions to deepen their understanding, promising to discuss in the next lecture the preventive measures against the forgery of audition lists. He stressed the importance of studying practical models in order to deal with and overcome common errors.

### Key Takeaways

- The importance of the audition certificate in documenting heritage texts.
- The role of the chain of transmission in strengthening the soundness and accuracy of transmission.
- The license as a means of reinforcing transmission and covering gaps.
- The importance of combining theoretical analysis with practical work in editing manuscripts.
