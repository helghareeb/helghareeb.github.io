### Course 4: Audition Certificates Recorded on Manuscripts - Day Three

### Lesson Title: "The Benefits and Analysis of Audition Certificates Recorded in Arabic Manuscripts"

### Introduction

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened the third lecture of the fourth course by explaining the importance of the audition certificates (sama'at) recorded in manuscripts as one of the central pillars of textual editing (tahqiq). He stressed that analyzing these certificates and distinguishing original ones from copied-over ones is the very core of the editor's work, not merely a detail. The Shaykh discussed how this information can be used to offer a deep and comprehensive reading of texts, which strengthens the reliability of studies.

### Main Elements of the Lecture

### 1. The Components of Recorded Audition Certificates

- **Opening phrases:** these define the nature of the receiving (audition, reading, license (ijaza), handing over (munawala)) and indicate the mode of learning followed, which helps draw an accurate picture of the session.
- **Mention of the title:** the title may appear in three forms:
  - A simple reference to the book.
  - A short formulation that makes documentation easier.
  - The full title, including all the necessary details.
- **The name of the transmitting authority (musmi'):** accompanied by a title reflecting his scholarly and social standing, which highlights the scholarly value of the session.
- **The shaykh's chain of transmission (isnad):** demonstrates his right to transmit by stating his chain, whether in detail or in abbreviated form.
- **The names of those present:** recorded with additional information such as ages or the number of times attended, reflecting how thoroughly these sessions were documented.
- **The date and place of the audition:** two vital elements that help establish the temporal and spatial contexts framing the texts.

### 2. Benefits Drawn from Audition Certificates

### A. Benefits Relating to the Edited Text

- **Inferring the title of the book:** audition certificates are a decisive means of determining titles lost because the first pages are missing.
- **Bringing out multiple titles:** audition certificates make it possible to trace the historical development of the names a book has carried.
- **Establishing the attribution of the book:** audition certificates signed by the author are reliable evidence that the attribution is sound.
- **Deriving the copyist's name and the copying date:** by comparing the handwriting of the audition certificates with the handwriting of the copy.
- **Tracing the movement of manuscripts:** they help reveal the various stations a copy passed through over time.

### B. Benefits Relating to Other Disciplines

- **Building accurate biographies:** audition certificates provide highly reliable information about authors, their teachers and their students.
- **Correcting errors in biographies:** they help correct contradictions found in biographical dictionaries.
- **Writing the history of scholarly activity:** they highlight the role of different geographical locations in transmitting knowledge.
- **The social dimension:** they reflect the names of those present and the social roles recorded in the certificates.

### 3. Distinguishing Original from Copied-Over Audition Certificates

### A. Explicit Statements

- Phrases such as "in the exemplar from which it was copied" or "from the handwriting of so-and-so" are clear evidence of copying.
- Such statements may be found at the beginning or end of the audition list (tabaqa), or within the text.

### B. Indications of Copying

- **Discrepancy of dates:** if the date of the copy is later than the date of the audition, this indicates copying.
- **Statements indicating transfer:** such as "so-and-so summarized it".
- **Difference of handwriting:** this shows that the audition certificate was copied from another copy.
- **Obvious errors:** these sometimes confirm that the texts are not original.

### 4. Problems Relating to Audition Certificates

- **Unauthorized alterations:** such as scraping (kasht) or additions that may raise suspicion.
- **Unclear texts:** these require experience and practice to interpret.
- **Modern audition certificates:** documentation efforts still need development to ensure consistency.
- **Varied cultural contexts:** these affect how texts are understood and interpreted.

### 5. Practical Applications

- **Specialized training:** focusing on training editors to analyze texts associated with audition certificates.
- **Applied workshops:** organizing training sessions focused on analyzing a selected set of manuscripts.
- **Documenting scholarly chains of transmission:** building maps of scholarly transmission using the extracted data.
- **Methodical comparison:** using precise comparative methods to identify differences.

### Conclusion
