## The Methodological Foundations of Reading the Arabic Manuscript: Grounding and In-Depth Analysis

## Summary of the Opening Lecture of Course 1

In this foundational lecture, Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari reviewed the theoretical and methodological principles for reading Arabic manuscripts (makhtutat). He focused on the importance of analyzing the historical and social contexts that shaped the conventions of earlier copying, and stressed that mastering the reading of manuscripts requires a deep understanding of the cultural structure within which they were produced. The Shaykh also set out a well-grounded approach that enables the researcher to move beyond superficial interpretations, which can distort texts and strip them of their original intent.

## Core Themes

### 1. Reading Manuscript Texts Correctly: Importance and Aims

- The central aim is to read texts in a way that reflects the author's intention and the copyist's (nasikh's) meaning, staying clear of modern interpretations that may contradict the original.
- The Shaykh highlighted that misunderstanding manuscript texts leads to distortions that can change meanings and corrupt historical contexts.
- He stressed that understanding copyists' symbols and abbreviations is an essential skill for deciphering manuscript texts.

### 2. Challenges in Dealing with Copyists' Conventions

- The Shaykh pointed out that modern researchers' lack of precise knowledge of copyists' conventions can lead to serious errors in reading texts.
- He drew attention to the need to analyze manuscript orthography (rasm) in order to understand copyists' methods, noting the influence of the cultural and social environment on their practices.

### 3. Methodological Paths to Mastering the Reading of Manuscripts

- **Orthography (rasm):** a detailed study of copyists' ways of forming letters and words, together with an understanding of the differences between standard spelling and the traditional orthography of manuscripts.
- **Vocalization (dabt):** analysis of the techniques of vowel-marking and diacritical precision that ensure the clarity of texts.
- **Correcting errors:** a review of the methods of correcting errors using the copyists' own symbols and signs.

### 4. The Importance of Acquired Skill (Malaka) and Practice

- The Shaykh explained that reading manuscripts is not merely a skill but a complete craft that requires long practice under the supervision of specialists.
- He affirmed that combining theoretical understanding with practical application helps the researcher develop an analytical faculty that allows him to read texts with confidence and precision.

### 5. Distinctive Orthographic Phenomena in Manuscripts

- **Omission (hadhf):** dropping medial alifs or letters that are not phonetically necessary.
- **Addition (ziyada):** adding letters for greater clarity, as in the word “مائة” (mi'a, “hundred”).
- **Joining (wasl):** merging separate words, as in “بسم الله” (bismillah).
- **Substitution (badal):** replacing letters for stylistic reasons or under the influence of the orthography of the Mushaf, such as “صلوة” (salawa) instead of “الصلاة” (al-salah, “prayer”).
- **Hamzas:** variation between writing the hamza, omitting it, or softening it (tashil).
- **Influence of the Mushaf:** the Shaykh highlighted that the orthography of the Uthmanic Mushaf had a profound influence on copying techniques.

### 6. The Orthography of the Uthmanic Mushaf and Its Influence on Manuscripts

- The Shaykh explained how the orthography of the Uthmanic Mushaf influenced copying techniques, noting that copyists often drew on its rules when writing non-Qur'anic texts.
- He attributed this influence to the copyists' education in the Qur'an schools (katatib), which relied on the Mushaf as the first teaching text.
- He stressed that studying this influence requires careful analysis to identify the overlaps between Qur'anic texts and other texts.

### 7. Practical Guidance for Researchers

- The Shaykh advised researchers to read manuscript texts analytically and patiently, taking into account the stylistic differences among copyists.
- He called for distinguishing between copying errors and deliberate stylistic variations.
- He stressed the need for practical training in analyzing texts, with particular encouragement to benefit from the expertise of specialists.

### Conclusion

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari affirmed that mastery of reading manuscripts requires the integration of theoretical knowledge with practical application. He announced that future courses would include in-depth practical applications covering a variety of manuscript examples, in order to strengthen participants' understanding and give them analytical tools that enable them to deal critically with historical texts accurately and efficiently.
