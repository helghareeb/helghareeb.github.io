## The Science of Vocalization in Reading Manuscripts: Theoretical and Applied Foundations

### Introduction

This lesson, delivered by Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari, deals with the science of vocalization (dabt) as an essential element in editing and understanding manuscript texts. The lesson highlights the importance of vocalization as a means that complements orthography (rasm), making it possible to convey the meanings of texts faithfully and precisely, in keeping with their historical and linguistic contexts. The Shaykh also explained the importance of studying this science in depth in order to understand the differences between the various schools and to address the challenges facing editors working on the manuscript heritage.

### Key Elements of the Lesson

### 1. The Concept of Vocalization and Its Basic Dimensions

- Vocalization is a system of symbols that complements the written orthography, helping to convey meaning precisely.
- It includes the use of the short vowels (fatha, damma, kasra), the sukun, the shadda, the madda, and other marks.
- It is considered a foundation for understanding Qur'anic, literary, and legal texts that demand a high degree of precision.

### 2. The Functions of Vocalization in Manuscripts

- **Marking the grammatical case endings:** case vowels such as the fatha, damma, and kasra are essential for understanding grammatical structure.
- **Distinguishing similar letters:** such as distinguishing ba', ta', and tha' by their dots.
- **Fixing the correct pronunciation:** vocalization allows the reader to pronounce accurately, which strengthens understanding.
- **Resolving textual ambiguity:** it serves as a mechanism for clarifying the differences between spelling and pronunciation.

### 3. Differences Between the Maghribi and Mashriqi Schools

- **The Maghribis (Western Islamic lands):** place the dot beneath letters such as fa' and qaf.
- **The Mashriqis (Eastern Islamic lands):** place the dot above these letters.
- **The shadda:** differs between the schools; some use the traditional form while others adopt innovative symbols, such as a shape resembling the numeral “seven”.
- **The kasra:** in some texts it is written as an upright alif, which highlights the diversity of copyists' techniques.

### 4. Symbols Used in the Science of Vocalization

- **The inverted nail-paring (qulamat al-zufr al-maqluba):** indicates that the letter is unpointed (muhmal) and carries no dots.
- **Writing small letters beneath the letter:** such as writing a small “ح” (ha') under the unpointed letter.
- **Inverting the dots:** changing the position of the dots, for example placing them beneath letters instead of above them.
- These symbols are considered precise tools that preserve the meanings of texts and prevent confusion.

### 5. Multiple Vocalizations of a Single Word

- Some words can be vocalized in more than one way, such as “مذمة” (madhamma / midhamma), which can be read with a fatha or a kasra.
- Symbols such as “معًا” (ma'an, “both together”) are used to indicate that both readings are correct and to record them.
- This multiplicity reflects the richness and flexibility of the Arabic language.

### 6. Applied Examples

- The Shaykh presented practical examples illustrating the copyists' rationale in using these symbols.
- The examples included both common and exceptional cases, to train participants in analyzing and understanding texts.
- These examples aimed to enable participants to read manuscript texts accurately and skillfully.

### Concluding Points

- **The rationale of vocalization:** understanding the reasoning behind the symbols of vocalization makes accurate reading easier.
- **Diversity among schools:** enriches the editor's knowledge of different traditions.
- **The importance of practice:** mastery requires continuous training on a variety of texts.
