### Lesson Title: Copyists' Methods of Writing Numerals in Manuscripts - An In-Depth Scholarly Study and Analysis

### The Specialized Study

### Introduction

The study of copyists' methods of writing numerals in manuscripts occupies a central place in the field of editing heritage texts, and Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari delivered this lecture with a highly precise scholarly method. The Shaykh began by glorifying Allah and affirming the sanctity of knowledge as a means of drawing closer to one's Lord, stressing the importance of this subject, which is concerned with analyzing the phenomena of writing numbers and their effect on the accuracy of editing and of dating.

### Scholarly Themes

- **The problem of interpreting numerals in manuscripts:**
  - The Shaykh discussed the importance of the way numerals are written as an inseparable part of the cultural context of manuscript texts. He explained that any error in interpreting these forms can lead to mistaken dating, which has a negative effect on the results of scholarly research.
  - He stressed the need to absorb and keep in mind copying traditions when editing texts, to ensure scholarly accuracy.
- **Analysis of the written forms of numerals:**
  - The Shaykh reviewed the marked differences between the old forms of numerals and modern ones, citing practical examples:
    - The numeral (4): written in a shape resembling the word “عو” ('w), which requires training to recognize.
    - The numeral (5): resembles an inverted English letter “e”.
    - The numeral (6): appears as a reversed form, which may cause confusion between it and the numeral (2).
    - The numeral (0): was often represented by a circle or by a shape resembling the present-day numeral (5).
  - He pointed out that these differences resulted from copyists' individual choices and the diversity of the cultural environments in which they worked.
- **Techniques for marking numerals in manuscripts:**
  - The Shaykh explained the methods of marking numerals with dots, showing a precise system that indicates the position of a digit in its place value:
    - The units place: left without dots.
    - The tens place: one dot placed above the digit.
    - The hundreds place: two dots.
  - He drew attention to the fact that studying these techniques requires strong analytical ability and comparison among several manuscript copies to determine the method used.
- **The methodological framework of scholarly editing:**
  - The Shaykh offered practical suggestions for avoiding superfluous notes in the footnotes, recommending that a comprehensive description of the copyist's method be placed in the introduction to the edition. He pointed out the importance of this approach in keeping the reader focused on the core content.
  - He reviewed exceptions in which the original forms must be noted if they directly affect the meaning or the reading of the text.
- **Essential reference works:**
  - The Shaykh reviewed a distinguished set of scholarly sources that are essential for every editor:
    - *Fath al-Mughith* by al-Sakhawi and *Tawjih al-Nazar* by al-'Iraqi, which offer in-depth analysis of copying phenomena.
    - *Al-Matali' al-Misriyya* by al-Hurini, which brings out the dimensions of orthography.
    - The introductions of Ahmad Shakir and Mahmud al-Tanahi, which provide deep critical insights.
    - Books on Qur'anic orthography and vocalization, which help in understanding the cultural influence on manuscript texts.

### Practical Applications

- The lecture presented detailed examples drawn from rare manuscripts, with a thorough explanation of the techniques used in writing numerals. The common errors into which editors fall were also analyzed.
- The Shaykh urged participants to practice reading old manuscripts regularly, stressing that this skill is acquired through daily practice.
- He announced that future courses would focus on intensive practical application, strengthening participants' ability to analyze texts and to discern copying conventions for themselves.

### Summary and Scholarly Recommendations

- Emphasizing the importance of building an editorial faculty focused on reading and understanding numerals in old manuscripts.
- Adopting an integrated editing plan that includes a description of the copyist's method in the introduction to the work, which reduces distraction for the reader.
- Turning to specialized classical references to broaden one's understanding and improve the quality of editorial work.
- Emphasizing that the study of manuscripts requires a deep awareness of the copying traditions that form the general framework for the production of texts in earlier eras.

This lecture is a complete academic reference for researchers and graduate students, combining theoretical analysis with practical application. It highlights the importance of understanding copyists' methods of writing numerals as an integral part of the methodology of scholarly editing.
