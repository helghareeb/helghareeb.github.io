### Course 3 - Day Two: An In-Depth Study of Endowment Notes in the Arabic Manuscript

### Introduction

Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari opened the lecture with a supplication, highlighting the essential importance of studying endowment notes (quyud al-waqf) in Arabic manuscripts. He explained that endowment (waqf) is not merely an element accompanying manuscript copies, but reflects social, cultural, and legal dimensions that have had a profound effect on preserving the Islamic heritage. These notes are a mirror of the historical practices that contributed to sustaining knowledge and passing it on between generations.

### Definition of Endowment Notes

The term “endowment notes” refers to the texts or seals that document the dedication of a particular copy of a book to charitable or religious purposes, with strict conditions regulating its use. The Shaykh explained that endowment was, and remains, a central practice in Islamic civilization, contributing greatly to enriching scholarly libraries and sustaining intellectual resources. Endowment notes are marked by a variety of forms, ranging from written texts to seals, which differ in style and content according to period and geographical location.

#### The Basic Elements of Endowment Notes

- **Explicit statement of the endowment:**
  - The texts contain clear formulas declaring that the copy is endowed, using terms such as “وقف” (waqf), “حبس” (habs), or “سبل” (sabbala).
  - The phrases used vary across periods, which makes them an important historical indicator for researchers.
- **The name of the endower:**
  - The name of the person who endowed the copy is given, accompanied by titles that highlight his social or official standing.
  - Consulting biographical dictionaries and histories helps decode these titles and understand what they signify.
- **The title of the book:**
  - The title usually appears in abbreviated form, and the author's name may be added to it.
  - This information is used as a basic reference for identifying the copy, but it needs verification when compared with other copies.
- **The number of parts:**
  - The number of parts or volumes making up the book is indicated.
  - This element is useful for determining how complete the copy is and inferring which parts are missing.
- **The seat of the endowment:**
  - The place where the copy is kept is specified, such as a mosque, a library, or a madrasa.
  - Precise details about the quarter or city are sometimes added for clarity.
- **Conditions of the endowment:**
  - The conditions bring out the endower's vision for how the copy is to be used, such as restricting its use to a particular group or forbidding its removal except under certain conditions.
  - These conditions express a commitment to the purpose of the endowment and protect its legitimate uses.
- **The administrator of the endowment (nazir al-waqf):**
  - The endower appoints a person or body to manage the endowment and ensure that its conditions are carried out.
  - Powers are clearly defined, and instructions may be added concerning the appointment of successors to the administrator upon his death.
- **Date and witnesses:**
  - The date on which the endowment note was drawn up is recorded precisely, which helps in dating and documenting the copy.
  - The document is usually witnessed by scholars or trustworthy figures, which lends it a high degree of credibility.

### Analyzing Endowment Notes

- **Forms of endowment notes:**
  - They range from written texts to seals, reflecting the development of the tools of documentation over the centuries.
  - Seals became increasingly common during the Ottoman period, becoming established as an effective means of documenting endowments.
- **Recurring elements:**
  - The texts contain recurring elements that make them easier to read and analyze, even when they have been damaged.
  - Understanding these recurring patterns helps in deducing unclear meanings and resolving ambiguity.
- **Lost or altered texts:**
  - Some documents show later alterations or scraping, which requires careful analysis to establish the soundness of the texts.
  - Comparative analysis with other copies is used to confirm the authenticity of the texts and to reconstruct them.

### The Importance of Endowment Notes

- **Documenting ownership:**
  - These notes establish the ownership of the copy and identify the bodies responsible for keeping it.
  - They highlight the role of endowments in protecting cultural heritage and ensuring its continuity.
- **Scholarly contribution:**
  - The notes provide information about the historical and social contexts in which the copy was produced.
  - They show the development of the linguistic terminology and styles of writing used in documenting endowments.
- **Usefulness in editing:**
  - They are a principal source for determining the date and place of production of the copy.
  - They help in verifying the attribution of works to their authors through the texts contained in the notes.

### Applied Examples

The Shaykh presented a variety of endowment notes, including complete ones and others with missing parts. He explained how to extract the basic elements and analyze them methodically. The Shaykh stressed that practical exercise is essential for developing researchers' skills in dealing with complex texts.

### Recommendations for Researchers

- **Deepening one's study:**
  - Reading a variety of manuscripts to gain comprehensive knowledge of the types of notes.
  - Recording observations about recurring phrases and their uses.
- **Benefiting from historical sources:**
  - Consulting biographical dictionaries and historical sources to understand titles and terms.
  - Using specialized references in manuscript studies to strengthen understanding.
- **Practical application:**
  - Analyzing notes in real examples to build experience.
  - Comparing different texts to discover patterns and differences.

#### Conclusion

The lecture closed with the Shaykh stressing the importance of studying endowment notes as an essential tool for preserving the scholarly heritage. He indicated that the next lecture would deal with reading notes (quyud al-mutala'a), with applied examples to enrich the participants' knowledge and strengthen their skills. He also emphasized that mastery in analyzing manuscripts depends on intensive practice and engagement with texts.
