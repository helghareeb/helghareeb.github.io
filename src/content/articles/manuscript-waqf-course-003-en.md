**Lesson Title: The Practical Benefits of Ownership, Reading, and Endowment Notes in the Arabic Manuscript**

**Introduction:**

The third lesson in the series “Endowment, Ownership and Reading Notes in the Arabic Manuscript”, delivered by Shaykh Salih Muhammad Abd al-Fattah al-Azhari, began with a focused academic framework on the importance of the notes recorded on manuscripts and their essential role in scholarly editing. The lesson was built around a central question: “How can the editor benefit from the notes recorded on manuscript copies?”, accompanied by scholarly mechanisms and precise methods for studying and interpreting these notes.

### Main Themes of the Lesson

### 1. Analyzing the Dates Recorded in the Notes

### The Importance of Dates in Estimating the Age of a Copy

- When a copy has no specific date, the dates recorded in ownership notes (tamalluk) or endowment notes (qayd al-waqf) become a precise scholarly tool for estimating when the manuscript was copied. This estimate is a basic step toward determining when the text was composed or the copy made.
  - **Example:** if a copy contains an endowment note dating to the year 850 AH, then the copy was certainly made before that year, which gives the study a reliable chronological dimension.

### Making Use of the Historical Figures Named in the Notes

- The dates of the people mentioned in the notes, such as owners or endowers, can be an important indicator for dating. If one of these people is known to have died on a certain date, then the copy must necessarily be older than the date of his death.
  - **Example:** an ownership note of a famous scholar who died in 900 AH clearly establishes that the copy was written before that date.

### 2. Inferring Missing Titles and Authors

### Dealing with a Lost First Page

- If the first page of a manuscript is damaged or lost, endowment or ownership notes may provide decisive evidence of the title and the author's name.
  - **Example:** the title of a book whose opening is lost may be found within an endowment formula on the copy, which helps complete the necessary information.

### Strengthening the Attribution to the Author

- If the notes include the testimony of a trusted scholar such as the hadith master Ibn Hajar, this is regarded as a strong reference confirming the correctness of the text's attribution to its original author.

### 3. Analyzing Marginal Notes and Comments

### Identifying the Writer of a Marginal Note

- A match between the handwriting of a recorded note and that of the marginal notes (hawashi) can reveal the identity of the person who wrote the comments, enriching the study and adding a new dimension to the copy.
  - **Example:** establishing that the marginal notes were written by the hadith master Ibn Hajar by comparing them with his handwriting recorded in a note.

### 4. Estimating What Is Missing from Copies

### How to Calculate Missing Leaves or Parts

- The editor can estimate the number of missing volumes or leaves by comparing the notes with what actually survives.
  - **Example:** if the note indicates that the copy consists of ten volumes, and the present copy contains only seven, the difference clearly indicates that three volumes are missing.

### 5. Tracing a Manuscript's Journey Through Time

### Mapping the History of a Copy Geographically

- The recorded notes help trace the path of a copy from the time it was made until it reached its present location.
  - **Example:** a manuscript that moved from Baghdad to Turkey and then to Egypt clearly shows the effect of travel on the preservation of texts.

### 6. Notes as a Tool for Biographical Study

### Reconstructing the Biographies of Scholars

- The notes provide reliable sources for completing or correcting the biographies of scholars.
  - **Example:** al-Zirikli made use of such notes to establish accurate biographical entries and to correct the errors of earlier books.

### Additional Details

- Establishing full names accurately.
- Determining the periods of scholarly activity.
- Tracing scholars' interests and the fields of their reading.

### 7. Analyzing Cultural and Social Activity

### Studying Cultural Institutions

- The names of libraries and madrasas mentioned in the notes offer penetrating insights into cultural activity.
  - **Example:** endowment notes in Syrian (Shami) libraries show the nature of scholarly life in that period.

### Documenting Places That Have Disappeared

- Some notes bear the names of places that no longer exist, which contributes to the writing of cultural history.

### 8. Reconstructing Lost Libraries

### Reviving Scholarly Libraries

- The editor can use ownership notes to reconstruct the lost libraries of scholars.
  - **Example:** tracing the contents of Ahmad Shakir's library by studying ownership notes.

### Critical Horizons in the Study of Notes

### 1. Ranking Copies for Editing

### Quality of the Copy First

- The copy containing the fewest grammatical and methodological errors is considered the most suitable for editing, regardless of its date or the identity of its copyist.

### 2. Detecting Tampering and Forgery

- Studying signs of tampering, such as scraped-off notes or altered texts, helps in assessing the authenticity of a copy.
  - **Example:** Turkish copies that underwent alterations aimed at concealing previous owners.

### 3. A Method for Choosing the Best Copy

- Comparing copies using multiple samples brings out the copy that best represents the original text.

### Orthographic Developments in Copyists' Methods

### Copyists' Rationale in Orthography

- Copyists' methods appear in variations of orthography (rasm) and spelling across the ages.
  - **Example:** writing “الرحمن” (al-Rahman) without the small (dagger) alif in old texts.
  - Studying these methods strengthens the understanding of texts.

### Conclusion

Shaykh Salih al-Azhari concluded this lesson with an inspiring message calling on participants to delve deeply into the study of manuscripts and to treat the notes as an integral part of the editing process. He stressed that continuous practice, supported by scholarly methods, is the way to master this art. He also encouraged participants to bring examples of notes for joint analysis in the coming sessions, providing a unique opportunity to acquire advanced practical skills.
