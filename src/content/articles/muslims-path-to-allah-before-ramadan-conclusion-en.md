Praise be to Allah, who made for hearts doors that are opened, for souls paths along which they are refined, and for days seasons to be seized; and blessings and peace be upon the master of those who guide, Muhammad ﷺ, and upon his family, his Companions, and all who follow his guidance until the Day of Judgment.

With this series we reach the end of a journey that began with a question and ended with an answer.

It was a journey that was less a sequence of articles than a map of the heart: tracing its borders, showing its diseases, uncovering its path, and placing before the reader a "practical method" for preparing for the greatest season that passes over a Muslim in the year: the blessed month of Ramadan.

This series is based on the lessons of Shaykh Abu Ishaq al-Huwayni, may Allah have mercy on him, in his explanation of *Manazil al-Sa'irin ila Allah* (The Stations of Those Journeying to Allah). I have tried, as far as I was able, to recast them in written form that joins knowledge with purification of the soul, and connects the spiritual heritage with the reality of the contemporary Muslim, given the difference between the style of sermons and lessons on the one hand and the particular nature of articles on the other. Whatever I got right is from the grace of Allah alone, and whatever error occurred is from myself; I ask Allah for pardon, concealment, and success.

This closing article is not a summary of the articles but their crown, gathering their meanings into a single idea: how does a servant enter Ramadan with a new heart?

## Why Does the Heart Need This Series Before Ramadan?

Because most people enter Ramadan with their bodies, not with their hearts.

Homes prepare with food, schedules prepare with appointments, pages prepare with advice…

As for hearts, the seasons descend upon them while they are preoccupied, worn out, exhausted, and full of heedlessness.

And this is the greatest obstacle to truly benefiting from Ramadan.

The series came to say: the gates of Paradise are opened only for one who has first opened his heart.

Wakefulness, self-accountability, repentance, resolve, inner intent, love…

These are not "theoretical stages" but keys to a door that will not be opened in Ramadan for one who did not open it before Ramadan.

## From Wakefulness to Love: The Complete Path the Series Traveled

**Wakefulness**: the moment in which the heart regains its awareness. There is no worship without wakefulness, and no Ramadan for one whose heart is heedless.

**Self-accountability**: not merely a review of deeds, but a review of motives, a review of intentions, and a review of the reality of our relationship with Allah. In it the servant comes to know himself without embellishment, without excuses, and without cover.

**Struggling against weak aspiration, conceit, and delusion**: in it the deed is purified of conceit. A servant does not benefit from an act of obedience that pleases him with himself, and no act of worship is raised up when delusion and showing off corrupt it. When conceit departs, the heart becomes humble and is opened to increase in good. Every act of obedience that made you admire yourself counts against you, and every sin for which you reproached your brother comes back to you.

**Repentance**: the beginning of the path, its middle, and its end.

**Sincere inner intent (*al-hamm*)**: an inner secret that takes shape before any movement, for which the servant is held to account for good and for evil. And **resolve**: it is not enough for a person to know the path; he must move. Resolve is the will that cuts a new road in the heart.

**Love**: the goal of the stations and the jewel in the crown of the path, the fruit that is not given without trial. Whoever loves Allah, everything besides Him becomes light for him. In this article the series reached its peak, when love was tied to patience, trial, striving against the self, and purity of the heart.

**Love in a time of trials and heedlessness:** here appeared the effect of the media, the responsibility of the head of the household, the danger of what one listens to and looks at, and the heart's struggle with the distractions of the age. In it experiences and stories were related to point to a fixed law: heedlessness extinguishes love.

## The Greater Meaning of the Series: That the Servant Enters Ramadan "With a Prepared Heart"

The series does not say to the reader, "Do this and do not do that." Rather it says to him: be another person, build a new heart, purify your vessel before Allah fills it, prepare yourself to receive the opening.

That is why the series was not "an admonition" but "a method." It walked with the reader from the beginning of the path to its end, step after step, until Ramadan stood before him not as an annual ritual but as an appointment between the servant and his Lord.

## What Remains After the Series Ends?

Three things remain:

**That knowledge turns into a habit of the heart:** daily wakefulness, holding oneself to account, renewing resolve at every weakness, purifying the heart at every feeling of conceit, watching one's inner intent at every intention, tending love at every trial. These are not "theoretical stations" but "habits of the heart."

**That Ramadan be the examination of the result… not the beginning of the path:** whoever waits for Ramadan in order to begin usually does not begin; whoever begins before Ramadan enters Ramadan full; and whoever enters full comes out with an opening.

**That these stations be provision for the whole year:** this series is not confined to Ramadan; it is provision for the year, provision for a lifetime, and provision for the path.

## The Close of the Series: The Road Is Still Long… but the Heart Now Knows How to Walk

With this article the series is concluded, not as a closing of the door but as an opening of it.

The road to Allah is long, but walking it is a joy; the heart is weak, but Allah strengthens it; the servant grows slack, but wakefulness brings him back; the soul slows down, but resolve drives it on; and love, if it is found… carries its owner to Allah.

We ask Allah to make this series blessed in its effect and sincere in its aim, to open through it for whoever reads it, and to make it one of the keys of good in his life; and to grant us a living, sound heart that receives Ramadan as a dear guest is received: with love, readiness, sincerity, and longing.

And that He have mercy on the eminent Shaykh, the hadith scholar Abu Ishaq al-Huwayni, place what he offered in the scale of his good deeds, and strengthen the hearts of his family and his students.

And that He protect the lands of the Muslims, and return us to Him in a beautiful return.

Allah is the One whose help is sought, upon Him is our reliance, and there is no might nor power except through Him.
