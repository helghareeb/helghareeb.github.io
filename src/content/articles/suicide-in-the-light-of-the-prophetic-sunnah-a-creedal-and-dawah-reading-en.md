## Suicide in the Light of the Prophetic Sunnah: A Creedal and Da'wah Reading

The article begins by noting that the protection of life is one of the greatest objectives of the Shari'ah, and that among the most serious issues touching a person's creed and conduct is the violation of life, especially in the form of suicide, which has become a worrying phenomenon in some contemporary settings. This calls for a grounded scholarly treatment that relies on the texts of revelation, draws on the understanding of the Salaf, and addresses present reality with a wise da'wah spirit.

## The Hadith of al-Tufayl ibn 'Amr al-Dawsi

The article takes as its central text the hadith of the Companion al-Tufayl ibn 'Amr al-Dawsi, may Allah be pleased with him, in *Sahih Muslim*. Jabir ibn 'Abdullah narrates the story of a man who emigrated with al-Tufayl. He could not bear an illness that struck him, so he cut the joints of his fingers until he died. Al-Tufayl then saw him in a dream in a good state, but with his hands covered. The man told him that Allah had forgiven him because of his emigration, but that he had been told his hands would not be mended, since he himself had ruined them. When al-Tufayl related this to the Prophet ﷺ, he prayed for him, saying: "O Allah, and forgive his hands too." (Reported by Muslim, 116.)

This authentic hadith, the article explains, contains several precise meanings: the great status of emigration and righteous deeds in expiating sins; that the effect of a sin can remain even with general forgiveness; the affirmation of the Prophet's intercession and his supplication for his Ummah; and that suicide is a grave sin that does not take a person out of the religion.

## The Prohibition of Suicide in the Texts

The prohibition of killing oneself is stated explicitly in the Book of Allah: "And do not kill yourselves [or one another]. Indeed, Allah is to you ever Merciful" (al-Nisa' 4:29). The hadiths stressing this are numerous. Among the best known is the report of Abu Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever kills himself with something in this world will be punished with it on the Day of Resurrection" (reported by al-Bukhari, 6047, and Muslim, 110). In another hadith: "Among those before you was a man who had a wound and became distressed. He took a knife and cut his hand with it, and the blood did not stop until he died. Allah said: My servant hastened to Me with his own life, so I have forbidden Paradise for him" (reported by al-Bukhari, 3463).

These texts clearly show that suicide is one of the major sins, that it is an assault on Allah's right over the soul, and that its recompense corresponds in kind to the deed, which is one of the greatest principles of divine justice.

## The Creedal Dimension

Some people think that the texts of warning, such as the Prophet's words "abiding eternally therein forever," indicate that the one who commits suicide is a disbeliever. The article states that this is an unsound understanding according to Ahl al-Sunnah. The narration referred to is the hadith of Abu Hurayrah: "Whoever throws himself from a mountain and kills himself will be in the Fire of Hell, falling into it, abiding eternally therein forever; whoever drinks poison and kills himself, his poison will be in his hand and he will drink it in the Fire of Hell, abiding eternally therein forever; and whoever kills himself with a piece of iron, his iron will be in his hand, stabbing his stomach with it in the Fire of Hell, abiding eternally therein forever" (reported by al-Bukhari, 5778, and Muslim, 109).

The imams of creed have established that the one who commits suicide is not declared a disbeliever unless he held it to be permissible; that he is under the will of Allah, who may punish him if He wills or forgive him if He wills; and that the eternity mentioned in the texts is to be understood in one of several ways, among them that it is said for emphasis and deterrence, that it refers to a long stay, or that it applies to one who deemed the act permissible and thereby became a disbeliever. This agrees with the general rule taken from the verse: "Indeed, Allah does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills. And he who associates others with Allah has certainly fabricated a tremendous sin" (al-Nisa' 4:48).

## A Psychological and Da'wah Analysis

Reflecting on the story of the man who could not bear his illness, the article finds that the underlying causes were distress, loss of patience, and weak certainty in the wisdom behind trial. These same factors recur in our time, when some people are driven to suicide by psychological pressures, economic crises, and disconnection from the meanings of faith. The prophetic method treats this at its roots by establishing the meaning of trial, as in the hadith "The people most severely tested are the prophets"; by linking pain to reward; and by instilling patience and contentment with Allah's decree. The article cites the fuller hadith of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, who asked which people are most severely tested, and the Prophet ﷺ answered that it is the prophets, then the righteous, then the next best and the next best; a man is tested according to his religion, so that if his religion is firm his trial is increased, and if it is weak his trial is lightened, and trial continues to afflict the believer until he walks on the earth with no sin upon him (reported by al-Tirmidhi, 2398, Ibn Majah, 4023, and Ahmad, 1481, with slight variation). From this it becomes clear that the problem is not purely material; at root it is a problem of faith and upbringing.

## Between Divine Justice and Divine Mercy

The hadith of al-Tufayl combines two great meanings. Justice appears in the fact that what the servant ruined with his own hands was not mended. Mercy appears in the forgiveness of his sin because of his emigration, and then through the supplication of the Prophet ﷺ. This balance is among the greatest distinguishing features of the Islamic method: there is no excess in making light of sin, and no neglect of hope in Allah's mercy.

## Da'wah Lessons

The article summarizes the main lessons as follows: the sanctity of the soul must be honored, for it belongs to Allah, not to the servant; suicide is incompatible with complete faith and indicates a defect in patience and reliance on Allah; awareness grounded in faith must be built in order to face psychological crises; scholars and preachers have an important role in treating this phenomenon in language that combines firmness with mercy; and salvation does not come from fleeing pain but from bearing it patiently and seeking its reward from Allah.

## Conclusion

When a person's circumstances become constricted, he may imagine that relief lies in ending his life, when in reality it is a move from a limited hardship to a greater punishment, and from a temporary trial to a lasting reckoning. The duty of the time, therefore, is to rebuild awareness of this issue on the basis of the Shari'ah texts, sound understanding, and effective da'wah discourse. Life, however hard it becomes, is not meaningless; it is a field of testing. Whoever is patient succeeds, whoever seeks Allah's help is helped by Him, and whoever entrusts his affair to Him is sufficed by Him. The article closes by asking Allah to preserve the religion and lives of the Muslims, to grant them well-being and contentment, to heal those who are afflicted, and to guide those who have gone astray.
