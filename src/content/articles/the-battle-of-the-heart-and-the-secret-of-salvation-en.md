## The Battle of the Heart and the Secret of Salvation

The article opens with an image: deep within every person is a battlefield that never rests. The soldiers of revelation light the way, the soldiers of desire adorn the obstacles, and between them stands the heart, the king which, if sound, makes the whole body sound, and if corrupt, corrupts the whole body. Because Allah created man to be tested, He gave him a tireless enemy, Iblis, who swore to lie in wait for people on the straight path and was given means by which the servant's sincerity and steadfastness are tried. In an age of mingled voices and look-alike paths, when desires and doubts are presented in the garb of truth, speaking about revelation and desire becomes necessary to protect a person's religion and the peace of his heart.

The heart is the place Allah looks at and the secret of a person's soundness. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Truly, in the body there is a morsel of flesh; if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupt. Truly, it is the heart" (reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim). Allah says: "The Day when there will not benefit [anyone] wealth or children. But only one who comes to Allah with a sound heart" (ash-Shu'ara 26:88-89). Ibn al-Qayyim wrote in al-Tibyan fi Aqsam al-Qur'an that the heart is the king of the limbs, which are its soldiers and servants: when it is sound its soldiers are sound, and when it is corrupt they are corrupt. A sound heart is therefore not a spiritual luxury but the root of salvation. When the heart loves something, the limbs move toward it without effort, and when it hates something, it recoils without strain. The greatest battle, then, is for the heart to follow revelation rather than be a captive of desire.

The Qur'an makes clear that people take one of two paths, revelation and following, or desire and submission to the self: "But if they do not respond to you - then know that they only follow their [own] desires. And who is more astray than one who follows his desire without guidance from Allah? Indeed, Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people" (al-Qasas 28:50); "and do not obey one whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance and who follows his desire and whose affair is ever [in] neglect" (al-Kahf 18:28). Allah's instruction to David was: "O David, indeed We have made you a successor upon the earth, so judge between the people in truth and do not follow [your own] desire, as it will lead you astray from the way of Allah. Indeed, those who go astray from the way of Allah will have a severe punishment for having forgotten the Day of Account" (Sad 38:26). Desire is the root of every innovation and the substance of every sin, so in every situation, however simple it seems, a person faces a choice: revelation or desire.

When Allah perfected the religion, He left people no room to wander: "This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion" (al-Ma'idah 5:3). Before any word or deed, a Muslim therefore asks: Does it please Allah? Does it have a basis in revelation? Does it accord with the understanding of the Companions among whom the Qur'an was revealed? The article cites Ibn Mas'ud: "Follow and do not innovate, for you have been given what suffices you" (reported by al-Tabarani and al-Darimi), and "Moderation in the Sunnah is better than exertion in innovation" (reported by al-Darimi, al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi); and Ibn Umar: "Every innovation is misguidance, even if people see it as good" (reported by Ibn Battah in al-Ibanah al-Kubra and al-Bayhaqi in al-Madkhal). Ibn Mas'ud also said: "You are the Jama'ah even if you are alone." Outwardly the Jama'ah is a gathering of people, but its true measure is steadfastness on the truth; whoever holds to guidance and stands with the truth is the Jama'ah itself, even if no one agrees with him.

Desire's most dangerous trick is that it never confronts a person openly. It adorns what he likes and pushes away what he dislikes until he picks from religion whatever suits his wishes. The article gives contemporary examples. One is choosing rulings by mood: how many people hear a Shari'ah ruling they dislike, abandon the evidence and start attacking the person who stated it; when desire prevails, dispute arises and the evidence is lost. Another is taking religion from the media rather than from scholars: whoever makes talk shows his religious pulpit has opened a door of fitnah onto his heart and turned his back on the purity of revelation and its clean sources. A third is confusion over economic matters and transactions: many people, on hearing a fiqh ruling about banks or loans, consult not the evidence but their own interest, and listeners divide, as reality shows, into those who submit, those who argue, and those who go looking for a fatwa that resembles their desire.

Scholars and educators carry a particular responsibility. Deceiving students, worshippers or the general public is not mercy. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Each of you is a shepherd and each of you is responsible for his flock: the ruler is a shepherd and responsible for his flock; a man is a shepherd over his family and responsible for his flock; a woman is a shepherd in her husband's house and responsible for her flock; and a servant is a shepherd over his master's wealth and responsible for his flock" (reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim). An educator who sees a defect in creed, worship, or loyalty and disavowal (al-wala' wa al-bara') and stays silent has betrayed the trust. The sincere caller does not offer people a diluted religion tailored to their wishes but what Allah revealed, with mercy, clarity and wisdom. Not everyone addressed can bear the words that benefit him, but abandoning clear explanation out of fear of reactions squanders both the religion and the people.

Another error of the age is attachment to the worldly results of obedience more than to its reality. Some give charity expecting doubled wealth at once, or assume obedience will immediately open the doors of provision. The Qur'an, however, establishes that this world is a test: "And We test you with evil and with good as trial" (al-Anbiya 21:35). The believer worships Allah with insight, not on conditions, and accepts Allah's decree in withholding and giving alike, for both are trials from which only sincere faith brings deliverance.

Steadfastness is the provision for the road. Shahr ibn Hawshab asked Umm Salamah what the Prophet ﷺ supplicated with most when he was with her. She said his most frequent supplication was, "O Turner of hearts, make my heart firm upon Your religion." When she asked him about it, he said, "O Umm Salamah, there is no human being except that his heart is between two of the fingers of Allah; whom He wills He keeps upright, and whom He wills He causes to deviate." Then Mu'adh, one of the narrators, recited: "Our Lord, let not our hearts deviate after You have guided us" (Al Imran 3:8) (reported by al-Tirmidhi in this wording, and by Ahmad and Abu Ya'la). If the heart of the best of mankind needed to be made firm, what of ours, in an age crowded with doubts and desires and adorned paths of deviation? Steadfastness is a blessing and guidance is a gift that only Allah possesses, so we need constant humble supplication, fear of deviation and care for the springs of obedience.

The article concludes that the battle between desire and revelation is not a battle of information but a battle of the heart, of following, and of sincerity in seeking the truth. The heart's salvation depends on revelation being its leader and desire its servant, never its guide. It closes with the supplication: O Allah, Turner of hearts, make our hearts firm upon Your religion, inspire us with right guidance, turn desires away from us, and make us people of insight and uprightness.
